# Clearing 笹: the client is directionally right, and the cost is not in the cutting

The client's challenge: *"for the bamboo I don't think it's a problem to clean off right"*.

**Largely correct — with one structural caveat that changes how it must be budgeted.** Every ski
area in Hokkaido stands on ground that was sasa before it was piste. It is cleared routinely. But
the difficulty is not the cutting, and a plan that budgets for cutting alone will fail.

---

## What sasa actually is

The dominant Hokkaido understory species: `[P2]`

| Species | Height | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **チシマザサ** (*Sasa kurilensis*, ネマガリダケ) | **2–3 m** | Snowy west and north Hokkaido — the snow belts this study is targeting |
| **クマイザサ** (*Sasa senanensis*) | 1–2 m | Widespread |
| **ミヤコザサ** (*Sasa nipponica*) | 0.5–1 m | Drier, less snowy districts |

Note the correlation, because it is unhelpful: **the snowier the district, the taller the sasa.**
チシマザサ is the species found where the snow is best.

## Why cutting alone does not work

> **Sasa is a clonal rhizome network, not a population of individual plants.** The visible culms are
> shoots off a dense underground rhizome mat carrying stored carbohydrate. Cutting the tops removes
> this year's shoots and leaves the plant intact and funded. It resprouts to near-full height in
> **1–3 growing seasons**. `[P2]`

The proof that this is not a soft obstacle comes from Japanese forestry, where sasa is a
long-standing and well-documented barrier to natural regeneration — tree seedlings cannot establish
through a dense sasa mat. **If a birch seedling cannot get through it, a single pass with a brush
cutter is not ending it either.** Standard silvicultural practice (下刈り) is repeated cutting,
typically **annually for 5–8 years**, and the goal is suppression rather than elimination.

Two secondary problems follow from the same fact:

- **Cut culm bases leave stiff, sharp stubble.** At shallow cover this is a genuine hazard to a
  skier and it chews snowcat tracks and tiller blades.
- **Slope limits the machinery.** A tractor-mounted flail handles flat ground. At the 19–25° target
  gradient it does not, safely. The options are hand 刈払機 (slow, brutal labour), a
  radio-controlled tracked slope mower, or a spider excavator with a mulching head.

## The three real routes

| Route | What it is | Cost shape | Fit with the thesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Repeated cutting** | Forestry-standard 下刈り, 5–8 annual passes | ~¥100k–200k/ha per pass on moderate ground, higher on slope `[E]` — for 40 ha that is roughly **¥30–48 m over the programme (≈ RM 760k–1.2 m)** | Poor. This is a large recurring OPEX line against a thesis built on near-zero carrying cost. |
| **Grazing** | Cattle and especially **horses** browse sasa. Japan's 公共牧場 maintained open pasture on former sasa ground by sustained grazing pressure. `[P2/P3]` | **Near-zero marginal cost, and it generates income** | **Excellent — this is the answer.** The ranching activity *is* the vegetation management. Leg 5 of the thesis (ranching offsets holding costs) and the clearing problem solve each other. |
| **Full conversion** | Rhizome removal by excavator or rotary tiller, then seed to pasture grass | High one-off; permanent | Good outcome, but it is 土地の形質変更 — see the permit catch below. |

## The permit catch, and a correction to an earlier finding

Earlier work recorded 保安林 designation as **"acceptable, and a price advantage"** because transfer
is unrestricted and only felling and land-form change are controlled. **That holds for a buyer who
tours an untouched slope. It does not hold for a buyer who intends to clear.**

If the plan is to clear and grade, then inside a 保安林 the very things that were dismissed as
irrelevant — 立木の伐採 and **土地の形質変更** — are exactly what is being proposed, and both
require permission. Two further gates apply outside 保安林 as well:

- **森林法 10条の8** — 伐採届出, notification before felling in 地域森林計画対象民有林.
- **森林法 10条の2** — **林地開発許可**, a prefectural permit for converting more than 1 ha of
  forest. At 40 ha of clearing this is unambiguously engaged.

**Consequence for screening:** 保安林 status must now be scored differently depending on the
operating model. It is a discount for a touring buyer and a constraint for a clearing buyer. The
screening rules issued to the listings workstream treat it as a straight positive and should be
read with this qualification.

## The upside the client has spotted, which earlier work understated

There is a real prize here, and it is larger than "the slope becomes skiable."

`01-framework.md` sets `D_min` for uncleared sasa at **200–300 cm** and already records the
principle for rock: *"summer rock-picking and smoothing cuts `D_min` by 40–60 cm and buys 3–5 weeks
of season."* The same logic applies to vegetation, with a far bigger coefficient:

| State | `D_min` | Season implication |
|---|---|---|
| Uncleared sasa | **200–300 cm** | Deep midwinter only |
| Cleared to pasture | **~30–50 cm** | First durable snowfall to spring melt |

> **Clearing does not merely make the slope skiable. It collapses the depth threshold by roughly an
> order of magnitude, and the season expands at both ends** — plausibly by 6–10 weeks `[E]`.

On a site where the snow is not in doubt, that converts the asset from a short midwinter window
into something closer to a full season. It is the strongest single argument for taking the clearing
cost on.

## The practical form of the answer

**Do not clear the mountain. Clear the runs.**

40 ha of clearing is a ¥30–48 m programme. Three corridors 30 m wide by 1 km long is **9 ha** —
roughly ¥1.35 m per pass `[E]` — which is an ordinary rural-contracting expense, and it delivers a
usable hill immediately. The remainder of the holding stays as it is: sasa and birch are perfectly
good tree-skiing, and uncleared ground carries no clearing cost, no 林地開発許可, and no strict
liability (an ungroomed natural slope is not a 土地の工作物 — 長野地判平成13年2月1日).

Then let the stock hold the line on what has been opened. **That is the ranch and the ski hill
doing each other's work, which is the thesis.**
