## ⚠️ The synthesis finding: New Zealand's snow screen and title screen are structurally opposed

> The snow screen wants a base at **1,500–1,600 m or higher**. Tenure review (1998–2022)
> **freeholded the low country and gave the alpine tops to the Crown**, then closed the process
> in 2022.
>
> **New Zealand freehold is therefore, by construction, *below* the snow line.**

The evidence lines up exactly:

| Property | Highest freehold |
|---|---|
| **Mt Lyford** — the highest freehold ski terrain identified in New Zealand | **~1,460 m** |
| Upton Fells Station — 7,857 ha, 100 % freehold | Peaks 1,250 m; **majority below 850 m** |

> **This is not a market accident. It is the direct result of 24 years of deliberate policy
> separating the two things this mandate needs to combine.**

It only becomes visible by combining the tenure workstream with the snow workstream — neither
sees it alone. **It is the single most important finding in this study about New Zealand.**

## Chile 36–39 °S — the most under-priced region on physical grounds

- **Volcanic ash flanks give the lowest `D_min` in the world (30–50 cm)** — the same physical
  advantage that makes a hard-grazed paddock ski early, at continental scale.
- Snow is reliable, unlike 33 °S where the post-2010 megadrought and a coefficient of variation
  of **45–60 %** make the season a coin-flip and **fail the 8-in-10 test structurally.**
- Large private *fundos* reach 1,400–2,400 m with **600–1,200 m of vertical on real title.**

**Offsets:** active volcanoes and lahars (SERNAGEOMIN hazard maps are mandatory reading),
protected *araucaria*, and no wind shelter.

> **This region ranks first physically and was not covered by the sourcing effort at all.**
> Opening a Chilean sourcing workstream is the highest-value next action in the study.

## Japan — the constraint is tenure and vertical, never snow

The efficient route is **a defunct ski area on 民有地 (private land)** — more than 200 have
closed since the early-1990s peak:

- **Cleared, stumped runs collapse `D_min` from ~250 cm (over *sasa*) to 50–80 cm** — the
  single largest ground-cover improvement available anywhere in this study, already paid for by
  a previous owner;
- **The road and the power are already in.**

**Find them using GSI 地理院地図 1960s–80s aerial photography**, which shows the cleared runs
long after they have regrown at the surface.

> **Screen: base ≥ 700 m, top ≥ 1,300 m.** Japan's projected warming **squeezes from below**,
> so the spectacular 250–400 cm normals at 300–500 m stations describe **exactly the elevation
> band that fails** over a 20-year hold. This is the trap in the Hokkaido case: the snow is the
> best in the world, and it is at the wrong height.
