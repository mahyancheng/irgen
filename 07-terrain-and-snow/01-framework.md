# Snow and terrain — the screening framework

**Evidence status:** the framework, the avalanche analysis and the verification workflow are
analytical and stand on their own. **The regional numbers are unverified recall** — the search
budget was exhausted before this workstream ran and no meteorological source could be reached.
The workflow in `03-verification-workflow.md` is deliberately built so that the buyer
**generates primary data** rather than trusting anyone's recall, including this document's.

## 1. Ground cover reprices elevation — and nobody prices it

> **Minimum skiable base depth ≈ (height of the dominant roughness element) + 30–40 cm.**

| Ground cover | `D_min` — minimum base to ski ungroomed |
|---|---|
| Mown / hard-grazed pasture | **25–30 cm** |
| Volcanic ash | **30–50 cm** |
| **New Zealand snow tussock** | **100–150 cm** |
| **Japanese *sasa* bamboo** | **200–300 cm** |

**A smooth, hard-grazed slope at 1,400 m can deliver more skiable days than a tussock slope at
1,650 m.** Screening on elevation alone will misrank candidates. `[E]`

Two consequences that follow directly, and that no broker will raise:

1. **Summer rock-picking and smoothing cuts `D_min` by 40–60 cm and buys 3–5 weeks of season.**
   It is the **highest-return snow investment available** to this thesis, it costs a fraction
   of any machinery, and it is never modelled as a capital item. It should be.
2. **Century-grazed stations and *fundos* are smoother than adjacent ungrazed conservation
   land.** **Grazing history is therefore a snow variable** — which means the ranch leg of the
   thesis and the ski leg reinforce each other physically, not just financially.

## 2. The 100-day rule fails this client — use the 60/80/1.0 rule instead

The industry standard (Abegg / OECD 2007) is 30 cm of cover for 100 days in 7 winters out of
10, giving the 1,200 m Alpine reliability line that rises ~150 m per +1 °C of warming.

**It does not fit this mandate**, for four reasons:

- Its 30 cm threshold assumes a **groomed piste over European pasture** — see `D_min` above;
- It assumes **snowmaking**;
- It has **no wind term** — and New Zealand loses more operating days to wind than to snow;
- New Zealand's deepest snowpack arrives **late September–October**, *after* the demand peak.

### The proposed replacement

> **In 8 seasons out of 10: a base of ≥ 1.0 m (or the site's own `D_min`, whichever is greater)
> over ≥ 60 % of the terrain envelope, for ≥ 60 days, containing at least one contiguous
> three-week block.**

**The contiguous block is the term most candidates fail**, and it is the term that matters to a
private owner who travels to the property.

### The private-buyer asymmetry — and why it lowers the price

> **A commercial resort needs a reliable *opening date*. A private owner needs *any* good
> window.**

A private mountain can therefore tolerate **two to three times the inter-annual variance** a
resort can. **That widens the acquirable set and lowers the price** — it is one of the few
places where the client's private-first thesis is a genuine *economic* advantage rather than a
constraint. `[E]`

## 3. Buy terrain with no avalanche problem; do not buy terrain and then manage one

| Approach | Cost |
|---|---|
| Minimum credible avalanche programme (2–4 forecaster/controllers, explosives licence, magazine, records) | **NZ$150–400k/yr** |
| Gazex / Wyssen installations | **US$0.8–2.0 m capital** |
| **Terrain selected to have no avalanche problem** | **Under US$10k/yr** |

Both of the first two are flatly incompatible with "minimal CAPEX and OPEX".

### The target terrain profile

- **Pole-facing** — S–SE in New Zealand and Chile; N–NE in Japan
- **Lee-loaded open basin**
- **15–28°**, 300–600 m vertical
- **Rounded headwall**
- **No path steeper than 30° discharging into it**
- **No confined gullies**
- **A road that is itself out of avalanche paths**

That is **ATES Class 0–1**: no explosives, no remote avalanche control systems, no forecasting
payroll, no insurance loading.

It sacrifices **30–55 % of the terrain** — and what remains is exactly the intermediate,
cat-friendly, flat-light terrain a family actually skis 90 % of the time.

> **Screening threshold: reject the property if less than 60 % of the pole-facing 15–28 °
> terrain is free of overhead slopes steeper than 30 °.**

**Two points that are routinely missed:**

- **The access road is usually the real hazard, not the runs.** A safe bowl reached by a road
  that crosses three avalanche paths is not a safe property.
- **In Japan, explosive avalanche control is realistically unobtainable** under the
  火薬類取締法 (Explosives Control Act). **Safe terrain there is mandatory, not optional.**
