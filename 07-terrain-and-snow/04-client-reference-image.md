# The client's reference image: right landform, wrong climate

The client supplied a photograph as a type-example of the target property: *"Imagine this is my
ranch with the mountains and when it snows it turn into a small private ski area."*

This file records what the image specifies, because it is the clearest statement of the mandate so
far — and it also contains the single most dangerous failure mode in the whole search.

---

## What the image shows

A shot through a vehicle windscreen. Foreground: dry, stony rangeland — dead tan grass in clumps,
scattered pale boulders, sparse low shrubs, a wire fence on steel posts. Mid-ground: a flat basin
floor. Background: a range of **rounded, treeless, dun-coloured hills**, smooth-profiled, with what
appear to be **farm tracks already cut into the flanks**. Sky: blue, cumulus, and one pronounced
**lenticular cloud**.

**Reads as Central Otago / Mackenzie Basin (NZ), or US Great Basin / Interior West rangeland.** The
diagnosis below does not depend on resolving which — both are rain-shadow rangeland and both fail
the same test for the same reason.

## The landform: this is right, and better than expected

Measured against the spec in `01-screening/07-mandate-correction.md`, the terrain in this image is
close to ideal, and on several counts it beats the Hokkaido candidates:

| Criterion | Assessment | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Relief** | Plausibly **250–400 m** `[E]` | Hills subtend ~4–5° of frame. At 3–10 km distance that is 225–750 m. Consistent with target; unverifiable from one image. |
| **Gradient** | **~15–25°**, smooth and even | Sits on the 19° target. No cliff bands, no gullies, no terrain traps. |
| **Skiable shape** | Broad open faces, convex ridges | Fall-lines are obvious and long. Nothing technical. |
| **Avalanche risk** | **Negligible** | Too low, too small, too smooth. Wind slab on lee aspects at worst. |
| **Vegetation clearing** | **Zero** | Treeless. This is a real and large CAPEX saving — see below. |
| **Access** | **Tracks already cut** into the hillside | Road-building is a major line item in `04-economics/01-capex.md`. Partly pre-solved. |
| **Base area** | Flat, open, unobstructed | Room for lodge, parking, runout. |
| **Character** | Genuinely ranch-like | Fenced, grazed, extensive. Exactly the asset class in the thesis. |

**On landform alone this would be a strong candidate.**

## The climate: this is wrong, and the image says so itself

Three features of the photograph are all reporting the same fact.

### 1. The grass is dead and brown, and there is no green

Semi-arid rangeland. Central Otago runs 350–500 mm/yr; the Great Basin 150–300 mm. Precipitation is
the raw material of a snowpack. There is not enough of it here.

### 2. The hills are treeless *at low elevation*

Treeless ground at 400–900 m in a temperate latitude means too dry, too burned, or too grazed — not
too high. There is no alpine zone in this picture and no treeline. The brown runs all the way to
the summits.

### 3. The lenticular cloud

> This is the diagnostic feature, and it is worth stating plainly: **a lenticular cloud is the
> visible signature of a föhn wind** — air forced over an upwind range, wrung out on the way up,
> then descending warm and dry on the lee side.
>
> **It is a photograph of a rain shadow.** In New Zealand this is the nor'west arch; the same
> mechanism sits behind the Great Basin's aridity. The cloud is not incidental weather. It is the
> reason the grass in the foreground is dead.

## The trade that decides it: ground cover vs. snowfall

There is a genuine advantage hidden in this terrain, and it should be counted before it is
dismissed. **Bare grazed grass needs far less snow to become skiable than Hokkaido forest does.**

Applying the D_min ground-cover thresholds from `07-terrain-and-snow/01-framework.md`:

| Ground cover | Depth needed to ski safely `[E]` | Depth typically available |
|---|---|---|
| Mown / heavily grazed pasture | ~30 cm | — |
| **Rough tussock, scattered rock — this image** | **~60–80 cm** | **~30–40 cm, intermittent, föhn-scoured** |
| **Hokkaido 山林 with 笹 (sasa bamboo, 1–2 m)** | **~150–200 cm** | **~200–400 cm at midwinter** |
| Young scrub / regenerating forest | 200 cm+ | — |

**That table is the whole answer.** The rangeland in the image needs less and gets less than it
needs. Hokkaido needs much more and gets more than it needs. The ratio is what matters, not the
absolute number — and only one of the two clears 1.0.

The rock scatter visible in the foreground makes this worse, not better: loose boulders on a slope
must be *buried*, not merely covered, before a snowcat or a skier can move safely over them.

## Consequences for the search

1. **The landform spec is now visually confirmed** and should be treated as settled: open, rounded,
   moderate-gradient, ranch-like, 250–400 m of relief, tracks in place, nothing technical.
2. **The climate filter must be applied before the landform filter, not after.** It is easy to find
   this shape of hill. It is hard to find it under a reliable snowpack. Searching shape-first will
   generate a long list of properties that photograph beautifully and never hold snow.
3. **If the image is Central Otago, it fails three tests, not one** — snow (above), OIA farm-land
   consent and the benefit test (`03-country-briefs/new-zealand-regulatory.md`), and the
   consent–economics inversion that makes a low-intensity private plan the hardest kind to get
   approved.
4. **The constructive version of this image exists**, and it is where the same landform sits under
   snow. Two places to test:
   - **US Interior West at 1,800–2,400 m base** — southwest Montana, the Big Hole, Idaho, North
     Park in Colorado. Same open ranch landform, real winter, freehold, no foreign-ownership bar
     for a Malaysian buyer (`03-country-briefs/rest-of-world.md`). This is the closest match to the
     photograph that also holds snow.
   - **Hokkaido / northern Tōhoku**, where the snow is not in question and the problem is finding
     open rather than sasa-choked ground inside one title. Currently under search.
