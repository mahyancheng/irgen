## ⚠️ Correction: I told you this was a gentle hill. It is not.

Earlier in this conversation I reported Seaside Valley's maximum gradient as **20°** and concluded
it "tops out at intermediate" and is "a long cruiser, not a mountain."

**That was wrong.** The 20° figure is the **average** of 第2ゲレンデ. That run's **maximum is 38°**,
it is ungroomed powder, and it carries a **300 m bypass for skiers who cannot ski it.** 第3ゲレンデ
averages 21°. `[P3]`

> **Seaside Valley's steepest pitch (38°) slightly exceeds Charmant Hiuchi's (37°) and
> comfortably exceeds your benchmark's (33°).** The 7.2° average I calculated for the 4 km run is
> arithmetically correct but describes only the longest, gentlest cruiser — not the mountain.
>
> **And the fall line runs toward the sea — broadly north-facing. That is the single best
> attribute the asset has.**

## 1. ⚠️ The snow problem — and it is documented, not inferred

**Itoigawa City's own tourism statistics record that in FY2019 the resort could not open at all
owing to light snow:** 「令和元年度は少雪により、シーサイドバレースキー場が開業できず」 `[P1]`

And the within-city comparison for 2025–26 is the cleanest natural experiment available:

| | Base | Opened | Closed | **Days** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Seaside Valley** | **240 m** | 29 Dec 2025 | 22 Mar 2026 | **~84** |
| **Charmant Hiuchi** | **500 m** | 19 Dec 2025 | 5 May 2026 | **~138** |

> **Same weather systems, twenty kilometres apart, and a 54-day gap — almost entirely elevation.**
> `[P1/P3]`
>
> **No snowmaking was found. Assume none until disproved.** And a season that failed outright
> within the last decade is not a tail risk; it is a demonstrated operating condition.

JMA is no help at this specific question: the **Itoigawa and Nou AMeDAS stations sit at 8 m and
55 m** — useless proxies for a 240 m base — and the monthly normals tables could not be read.
`[U]`

## 2. Tenure — unconfirmed, and the regional precedent is now clearly adverse

The City has only ever used **譲渡 (transfer) of 施設 (facilities).** Nothing published says whether
title to the ~74.5 ha passes, or whether consideration is paid or nil. **No 募集要項 exists.** `[U]`

### ⚠️ Two corrections to what this study previously reported

**The Murakami precedent is worse than the Charmant Hiuchi profile stated.** On **30 September
2025** the Murakami council **unanimously approved a 無償貸与 — a free *loan* — of facilities and
equipment to Thinkfirst Inc. The city kept title.** `[P1/P2]`

> **That is the structure a Niigata municipality reaches for first, and the reason is procedural:
> a free loan of facilities avoids a land-disposal vote entirely.**

**And Tsunan is more precise — and more damaging — than reported.** The council's eventual position
in **May 2026** was to permit a sale **limited to roughly 60–70 ha of built footprint** — the hotel
site and car park — **with the mountain and its catchment retained.** `[P2]`

> **A buyer asking Itoigawa for the mountain and not the buildings is asking for precisely what
> the neighbouring council refused.**

### The ownership history is different from Charmant Hiuchi's, and it matters

**This was never a municipal development.** Built **1974–80 by 糸魚川総合開発** (parent: 村松建設),
which quit in **1997**. **The resort closed in April 1998**, and the **old Itoigawa City** absorbed
land and buildings for the 1998–99 season **as a rescue.** `[P2]`

**Note the irony:** Nou Town's project was **Charmant Hiuchi**, opened December 1998 — **and its
construction is cited as a reason Seaside Valley's private owner walked away.** The 2005 merger
then put **two competing resorts on one balance sheet.** That is the structural reason Itoigawa is
now disposing of both.

The operator, **株式会社糸魚川シーサイドバレー**, is a third-sector company with **city equity
≥50 %** — and **its fate in the transfer is unaddressed anywhere.** `[U]`

## 3. ⭐ Foreign eligibility — the strongest positive in the Japanese file

Itoigawa City stated, unprompted, that it **"also has the intention of considering companies with
foreign capital"** — 「海外資本の企業も視野に入れたい考えもある」 — as transferees. **Confirmed in
two independent retrievals** of the Niigata Nippo report. `[P2]`

**Itoigawa is not in a 重要土地等調査法 注視区域** — Niigata's designations are Kashiwazaki/Kariwa
and Shibata only. `[P1]`

> ⚠️ **The caveat is essential: this is a *staff* statement, not a council resolution — and Tsunan
> proves a Niigata council can void a signed 基本協定書 at the eleventh hour.**

## 4. ⚠️ The regulatory hook that killed Tsunan is pre-loaded here

Under the **新潟県水源地域の保全に関する条例** (Niigata Prefecture Water Source Area Conservation
Ordinance), a **forest-land seller must notify the prefecture 30 days before contracting**, and
designated water-source areas cover **approximately 97 % of privately-owned forest in Niigata.**
`[P1]`

> **Tsunan's council killed the INTRANCE sale on exactly this ground** — 「ほぼ全域が水源涵養地」,
> *"almost the entire area is water-source recharge land."* `[P2]`
>
> **This is the specific mechanism by which a Niigata ski-area sale to outside capital has already
> been defeated once. Prepare a written water commitment before it is demanded, not after.**

This also connects to the national picture: the Cabinet Secretariat's 2026 review of foreign land
acquisition has a **declared focus on water-source and forest conservation.**

## 10. The rational bid structure

> **Bid for a carve-out of the upper mountain — roughly 450–740 m — lift-free, with the City
> retaining and removing the five ageing lift lines and the 1982 hotel.** `[E]`

The reasoning is that **a "free" conveyance carrying a 撤去・原状回復 (removal and restoration)
covenant is a negative-value asset.** Five lift lines of 1980 vintage, plus pre-1981-seismic-code
buildings with probable asbestos, are **the likeliest poison pill in this transaction** — and the
thing a municipality most wants to hand to someone else.

**This also aligns the ask with what the seller can politically deliver:** the City keeps the
visible community assets and the employment attached to them; the buyer takes the upper snow.
