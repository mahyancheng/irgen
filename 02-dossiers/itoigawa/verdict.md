# Itoigawa — combined verdict

days on the same weather, two fewer lift lines to maintain and eventually demolish, a north face,

shoulder on 明星尾根, not a summit. **Parcel extent is unverified at both.**

**And neither is small.** 94 ha and 74.5 ha against a 40 ha target.

## Why this is hard, in order of severity

### 1. The city may not own the mountain

The Murakami precedent — which this study twice mis-reported as a free transfer — turns out to be
a **land lease plus a free loan of plant, with the city keeping title.** And in Murakami's case
**the city does not own the land at all**: it leases from private owners and subleases, so the
incoming operator needed **unanimous consent of every landowner.**

> **If Itoigawa is structured the same way, there may be no mountain to buy at any price.**
> **This is the first thing to check and the cheapest** — ¥500 per parcel.

### 2. The plan fails the evaluation before nationality is reached

The Ministry of Finance's standard form for this kind of disposal is titled
**「用途指定（買戻特約付き）」** — use designation with a repurchase right. Expect business-continuation
and employment covenants, resale restriction, liquidated damages and a removal obligation.

> **A city disposes of a loss-making resort in order to outsource a public service.** *"I will
> close it and ski privately"* is the opposite of what is being procured.

This is the same inversion this study found in New Zealand's benefit test, reached by a completely
different legal route. **It is now a pattern, not a coincidence: public vendors cannot sell to a
private-use buyer, because private use is not a public benefit.**

### 3. Tsunan proves a Niigata council will kill a better-qualified buyer than you

**INTRANCE was a TSE-listed Japanese company** with ¥1.44 bn of capital and 133 employees. It had
preferred negotiating rights, a signed 基本協定書, and had **already conceded the water source out
of the sale.** The council killed it **6–5**, over water, land area and local employment, and
directed the town to pursue succession by the **incumbent local operator** instead. The mayor is
not standing again.

> **Nationality was never raised. It didn't need to be.** Every objection that succeeded applies
> with more force to a non-resident foreigner who intends to stop operating.

### 4. "Free" is a liability transfer

Gokase's numbers: continuing costs ¥500 m of capex plus over ¥40 m/yr; **closing costs about
¥500 m — RM 12.7 m — to demolish the lifts and centre house.**

> **That is why a city will transfer for ¥0, and why it will try to put the demolition covenant in
> your contract.**

And the carrying cost is higher than this study earlier estimated: **ski-area land is assessed as
雑種地, not 山林**, and reclassification follows the *physical removal* of infrastructure, not the
end of ticket sales. **償却資産税 at 1.4 % accrues on idle lifts until they are physically
scrapped.**

### 5. Use can be throttled after you buy

Myōkō's response to Patience Capital Group's 350 ha purchase was not to block title — it cannot —
but to cut the development-permit threshold from **3,000 m² to 1,000 m²** and mandate **prior
consultation and resident consent**, from April 2026.

> **A resident-consent requirement is an effective veto on a private-mountain plan, and it can be
> enacted after completion.**

## The one strongly positive finding

At the 建設産業常任委員会 of **15 December 2025**, Itoigawa's 商工観光課 said, unprompted, that it
**「海外資本の企業も視野に入れたい考えもある」** — foreign-capital companies are within scope.
Confirmed in two independent retrievals. Itoigawa is **not** in a 重要土地等調査法 注視区域.

**That is a staff statement, not a council resolution.** But no other Japanese municipality in this
study has said it.

---

## ⭐ The strategic conclusion

**Every obstacle above exists because the seller is a municipality.**

`[P2]` **Grandeco**, a top-three Fukushima resort, was acquired from **Tokyu Land in 2022** by
foreign-affiliated capital with **no council vote, no public solicitation and no covenants** —
because the vendor was private.

> **The Itoigawa terrain is the best found in this study. The Itoigawa *transaction* is the worst.
> Those two facts are separable, and the second one is the one to act on.**
>
> **Redirect the Japanese search toward privately-owned ski areas and private mountain land** —
> where no council votes, no use designation, no repurchase right, no employment covenant and no
> demolition covenant apply. **Keep Itoigawa as a watch item, not a plan.**
