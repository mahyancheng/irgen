# Acquiring a Japanese municipal ski area — the mechanics

How a non-resident foreign buyer would actually acquire Seaside Valley or Charmant Hiuchi, and
what stands in the way. MYR at ¥1 = 0.02540.

---

## 1. The 行政財産 wall — and it is two or three council votes, not one

`[P1]` Under the **Local Autonomy Act (地方自治法) Art. 238-4(1)**, **administrative property
(行政財産) cannot be sold, leased, gifted, or have private rights attached.** A municipal ski
resort is a **公の施設** under Art. 244 and is therefore administrative property. It must first be
**de-designated (用途廃止)** into ordinary property (普通財産) before Art. 238-5 permits a sale.

**The real sequence — each step gated to a quarterly council session:**

1. **Repeal the establishing ordinance** (設置条例の廃止) — council resolution
2. **Cancel the designated manager's designation** — itself a council resolution (Art. 244-2(6))
3. **用途廃止** — internal mayoral decision, then transfer to the property department
4. **Resolution on the disposal itself**

### Two independent resolution triggers, and no price avoids both

| Trigger | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Art. 96(1)(8)** | For a 市, the Cabinet Order standard (施行令 121-2, 別表第4) is **¥20 m or more AND land ≥5,000 m²**. **A ski resort blows past the area test**, so any price ≥ ¥20 m triggers it |
| **Art. 96(1)(6) / Art. 237(2)** | A transfer **without fair consideration** — free or discounted — requires a resolution |

> ### ⚠️ The counterintuitive part
>
> The **Supreme Court, 17 November 2005 (First Petty Bench)** held that for a below-value
> transfer the council must have deliberated **on the premise that the price is below fair
> value**, and resolved to permit **that specifically.** `[P1]`
>
> **A free or cheap deal is therefore the *most* politically exposed vote, not the least.** The
> instinct that "if they're giving it away, it'll be easy" is exactly backwards.

### The trap to check first

`[P1]` **Art. 244-2(2) requires a two-thirds majority of members present** to abolish a public
facility that an ordinance designates **"particularly important" (特に重要な公の施設).**

> **Whether Itoigawa's ordinance list includes the ski resorts is 未確認. If it does, one-third of
> the council can block the transfer outright.** `[U]`

## 2. ⚠️ Tsunan — and why it should frighten you more, not less

`[P2]` **The full timeline:**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Apr 2025 | Two bidders |
| Jul 2025 | **INTRANCE** granted 優先交渉権 (preferred negotiating rights) |
| Aug 2025 | **6 of 11 councillors publicly oppose — over employment** |
| 10 Oct 2025 | 基本協定書 signed, **expressly excluding the water source and reservoirs from the sale** |
| — | Residents' group 「津南町 人と自然と水の会」 petitions |
| **Mar 2026** | **Council passes a 勧告決議 6–5** opposing the sale and demanding termination, on the stated ground that the site is **「ほぼ全域が水源涵養地」** — almost entirely water-source recharge land — and vast |
| — | Sale 白紙. **Mayor Kuwabara announces she will not seek re-election** |
| **11 May 2026** | Council returns **17 conditions**: limit the area sold, water abstraction subject to prior consultation, and pursue **"Plan B" — succession by the incumbent operator 津南高原開発** |

### The decisive fact

> **INTRANCE is a Japanese company** — 株式会社イントランス, Shibuya, **TSE-listed (code 3237)**,
> capital ~¥1.44 bn, ~133 employees, real estate and hotels. **No source records foreign capital
> as a ground of objection.** `[P2]`

**So the lesson is not "Japan blocks foreigners." It is worse than that:**

> **A Niigata town council killed a domestic, listed, professional buyer by one vote — 6 to 5 —
> over water, land area and local jobs, and preferred the local incumbent operator.**
>
> **Every one of those objections applies with greater force to a non-resident foreigner who
> intends to stop operating.** Nationality would be an *aggravator* layered on objections that
> already succeeded without it. `[E]`

Note also that the buyer had **already conceded the water source out of the sale** — and it was
still not enough.

### The pre-closing gate this study had missed

`[P1]` The **新潟県水源地域の保全に関する条例** requires notification to the **Governor 30 days
BEFORE contract** for forest-land transactions in a designated 水源地域 — with 勧告, 公表 and 過料
on breach.

> **This is a *pre*-closing gate, and it is harsher than the Forest Act's 90-day post-acquisition
> notice.** Whether the Itoigawa parcels sit inside a designated area is **未確認 — check with
> 新潟県治山課 first, not last.**

## 3. Can a foreigner apply? Yes in law — but plan on a Japanese entity

`[P1]` **施行令 167-4** lists the mandatory exclusions from competitive tender: no contractual
capacity, undischarged bankrupts, organised-crime connections. **Nationality is not among them.**
National-property practice states plainly that both corporations and foreign nationals may bid.

**But** `[P1]` local governments **may lawfully impose a business-location qualification (地域要件)**,
and routinely require the applicant to:

- be a **legal entity** — excluding individuals
- have a Japanese or local head office
- produce **national and municipal tax clearance certificates (納税証明書)**
- be on the city's qualified-bidder register

**Frequency in ski-resort solicitations: 未確認** — no actual 募集要項 text could be obtained.
Sapporo Moiwa's and Daisen's are the best templates to request directly.

> ### The actionable conclusion
>
> **A non-resident individual should assume he cannot bid in his own name.**
>
> **Form a 合同会社 (GK) — ¥60,000–100,000 to establish, ~¥70,000/yr 均等割 even at a loss — at
> least twelve months ahead of the solicitation**, because **tax-clearance certificates require a
> filing history.** `[E]`
>
> That is a decision to take now, not when the 募集要項 publishes.

## 4. ⚠️ The plan is a bigger obstacle than the passport

`[P1]` The Ministry of Finance's standard contract form for this kind of disposal is literally
titled **「用途指定（買戻特約付き）」** — *use designation, with repurchase right attached.*

**Expect all of the following:**

- **Use designation** — the land must continue to be used as specified
- **買戻特約** — a repurchase right, maximum 10 years (民法 580)
- **Business-continuation and employment covenants**
- **Resale restriction**
- **Liquidated damages**
- **Removal obligation**

> ### The sentence that matters most in this whole file
>
> **A city sells a loss-making resort in order to outsource a public service.** *"I will close it
> and ski privately"* **fails the evaluation on the merits — before nationality is ever
> reached.** `[E]`

This is the same inversion this study found in New Zealand's benefit test, arriving by a different
route: **the low-cost, low-intensity, private plan is the one the seller is least able to accept.**

## 5. ⚠️ A "free" municipal ski resort is a liability transfer, not a gift

`[P2]` **Gokase gives the closure economics:** continuing operation = ¥500 m capex plus over
¥40 m/yr; **closing = approximately ¥500 m to demolish the lifts and centre house.**

> **That is precisely why a city will transfer for ¥0 — and precisely why it will try to put the
> demolition covenant in your contract.**

| Item | Cost | MYR |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-resort demolition (Gokase) | **~¥500 m** | **~RM 12.7 m** |
| One lift's rope replacement (Pippu scrapped the lift instead) | ¥20 m | RM 508,000 |
| Demolition unit rates | ¥40–60k/坪 steel; ¥50–100k/坪 RC | — |
| A 7-storey SRC building, ~3,100 m² | ~¥180 m | ~RM 4.6 m |

**Per-lift removal figure: 未確認.** `[U]`

> **Price the downside as: purchase price (possibly ¥0) + ~¥500 m contingent demolition + annual
> 償却資産税 + 固定資産税 + snow-load maintenance + liquidated damages.**

**And a correction to this study's own carrying-cost estimate:** `[P1]` ski-area land is valued as
**雑種地 (miscellaneous land)** under the 財産評価基本通達, **not 山林.** Reclassification to 山林
depends on **physical condition on 1 January** — so it follows *removal of the infrastructure*, not
the cessation of ticket sales. **The RM 4,280–21,400/yr figure quoted earlier was based on the 山林
rate and is too low while the lifts remain standing.**

Worse: **償却資産税 at 1.4 %, with a floor at 5 % of original cost, accrues on idle lifts until they
are physically scrapped.** `[P1]`

## 6. Not operating — one genuinely favourable finding

`[P1]` For a **索道 under the 鉄道事業法 Chapter 3**, suspension or abolition is a **notification
"without delay" — not the one-year advance filing that binds railways.** That is genuinely
favourable to a buyer who intends to stop running the lifts.

**Restart after six months or more requires technical-standard conformity confirmation.**

⚠️ **Whether *private, non-commercial* lift use falls outside 索道事業 altogether remains 未確認.**
**Get a written view from the 北陸信越運輸局 before purchase.** `[U]`

**特定空家:** the "six-times tax" headline mostly does not bite here — there is no 住宅用地特例 on a
lodge or 別荘 to lose. **The real risks are 命令 and 行政代執行** (administrative order and
substitute execution at the owner's cost).

## 7. ⭐ The counter-model: buy from a private seller

`[P2]` **Grandeco** — a top-three Fukushima resort — was acquired from **Tokyu Land in 2022** by
Chinese-affiliated capital (IDERA / Fosun, operating via The Court), with:

- **No council vote**
- **No public solicitation**
- **No covenants**

**Because the seller was private.**

> **This is the single most useful strategic finding in the Itoigawa work.** Every obstacle in
> sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 above — the 行政財産 wall, the council votes, the two-thirds trap, the
> use designation and repurchase right, the political exposure, the demolition covenant —
> **exists because the seller is a municipality.** None of it applies to a private vendor.
>
> **The Itoigawa assets are the best-fitting terrain found in this study. They are also wrapped in
> the worst-fitting transaction structure.**

### And Niigata already has a foreign ski buyer — with a warning attached

`[P2]` **Patience Capital Group (Singapore)** holds roughly **350 ha including two ski areas at
Myōkō**, on a ¥200 bn programme, phase 1 about ¥70 bn, with openings from December 2028. The mayor
was cautiously positive; land values rose **9 % year on year** in Sekikawa, the highest in the
prefecture.

> **The municipal counter-move was not to block title — it cannot — but to throttle *use*:** an
> ordinance cutting the development-permit threshold from **3,000 m² to 1,000 m²** in the ski
> zone, and **mandating prior consultation and resident consent**, targeted for April 2026.
>
> ⚠️ **A resident-consent requirement is an effective veto on a private-mountain plan — and it can
> be enacted *after* you buy.** `[P2/E]`

## 8. Structure and tax

**Conveyancing for a non-resident** `[P1]`: no 住民票 or 印鑑証明 — use **署名証明 from the Japanese
Embassy in Kuala Lumpur plus a 宣誓供述書**, or 在留証明; a **司法書士** handles registration.
**A 納税管理人 (tax agent) filing is mandatory** for 固定資産税 and 不動産取得税.

| Tax | Rate |
|---|---|
| 不動産取得税 | 4 % (3 % reduced for land and residential; non-residential buildings 4 %) |
| Consumption tax | **Land is exempt; buildings taxable at 10 %** — **negotiate the price split** |
| Exit | **39.63 % short-term / 20.315 % long-term** |
| Withholding | Buyer withholds **10.21 % of gross** when purchasing from a non-resident |

**Other notification layers** `[P1]`: **国土利用計画法 事後届出** for ≥10,000 m² outside city
planning areas — **within 2 weeks of contract**, stating intended use and price, with the Governor
able to issue a 勧告. And the **外国人土地法 (1925)** remains formally in force, needing only a
**Cabinet Order** to activate reciprocity-based restrictions; none exists today.

**Scale check** `[P1]`: foreign forest acquisition in 2024 was **0.003 % of private forest.**
**Facts will not win a council argument.**

### ⚠️ Inheritance tax is the quiet catastrophe

`[P1]` A non-resident non-national is a **制限納税義務者**: only Japan-situs assets are charged, but
**on the full 10–55 % scale — 55 % above ¥600 m of a statutory share** — and **債務控除 (debt
deduction) is restricted.**

> **Malaysia has no inheritance tax, and the Japan–Malaysia treaty is income-tax only. There is no
> credit and no relief. It is pure cost.**

`[E]` **A Japanese GK does not help** — its shares are Japan-situs. A **foreign holding company**
owning the land might, since foreign shares fall outside a limited taxpayer's charge.

> **This is inference, not advice. Get Japanese international tax counsel before signing —
> restructuring afterwards re-triggers acquisition and registration tax.**
