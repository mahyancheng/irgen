# Europe — small alpine parcels

Re-screened after the 40 ha revision. MYR at EUR 1 ≈ 4.55, NOK 1 ≈ 0.38 (approximate).

---

## 1. The finding: the binding constraint is the passport, not the cadastre

Dropping to 40 ha **does** dissolve the "cannot assemble contiguous scale" objection that killed
the Balkans and Romania in the first screen. **But it exposes a different screen that shuts most
of the European alpine arc to a Malaysian buyer — and shuts it hardest exactly where the good
inventory sits.** `[E]`

### Where a Malaysian can actually buy

| Status | Countries | The gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Open — nationality-blind** | **Sweden, Norway, France** (+ Czechia, Portugal on paper) | Sweden's *jordförvärvslagen* permit and Norway's *konsesjonsloven* concession are **land-use tests decided by a county board or municipality and applied to everyone alike** `[P2]`. France has **no nationality bar at all** |
| **Open, with a real permit gate** | **Spain, Greece** | See below |
| **Closed — stop spending effort** | **Austria** (Grundverkehr authorisation per Land; agricultural acquisitions turn on credible self-cultivation), **Switzerland** (Lex Koller, and farmland is *harder* still under the BGBB), **Slovakia**, **Poland** | `[P2]` |
| **SPV-only — recommended against** | **Romania** (Law 17/2014 — **no reciprocity treaty exists with any non-EU state**, and the individual route needs 5 years' Romanian residence), **Bulgaria** (constitutional bar, art. 22(1)), **Serbia**, and the rest of the Balkans | `[P2]` |

> **Why the SPV route is rejected rather than merely noted.** It defeats **both** hard
> requirements at once: **through an SPV the company is on the title, not the buyer** — which
> fails the mandate's absolute condition — and a dormant local company is a **permanent annual
> cost against an asset that earns nothing.** `[E]`

### France's gate is not nationality — it is SAFER

France has no nationality bar. Its gate is **SAFER pre-emption** over agricultural and forest
land, which can **substitute itself for the buyer and revise the price.** `[P1/P2]`

> **A non-resident foreigner buying an *alpage* for private recreation is close to SAFER's
> archetype of what it exists to displace.** `[E]` France is open in law and difficult in
> practice.

### Spain — and the part of it that actually works

**No ownership bar.** But **Ley 8/1975 requires Ministry of Defence consent for non-EU buyers of
*rustic* property in designated zones**: all islands, Gibraltar, **the French and Portuguese
borderlands — so the entire Pyrenees** — Cartagena, **Galicia**, and Cádiz. The notary and
registry will not proceed without it; 2–4 months stated, often longer. `[P2]`

> **But the Cantabrian range in Asturias and León, the Sistema Central, and the Sierra Nevada sit
> outside the restricted list.** `[E]` **That is the part of Spain to work** — and it is not the
> part anyone thinks of first.

**Greece:** Law 1892/1990 **voids** non-EU acquisitions in listed border prefectures (Kastoria,
Florina, Evros, Xanthi, Rodopi, Kilkis, Thesprotia, Preveza and islands). **Grevena, Trikala,
Ioannina and Drama are outside it.** `[P2]`

### ⚠️ Italy — the painful one, and the irony in it

Italy has **by far the best inventory in Europe** for this mandate. It probably fails.

Italian reciprocity is tested **right by right** by the notary against the MAECI tables. And
**Malaysia generally bars foreigners from agricultural-category land** — the narrow exceptions
require RM 1 m, at least 5 acres, and commercial agriculture. `[P2]`

> **An Italian could not buy a 40 ha hill pasture in Malaysia for private recreation. A notary
> applying the reciprocity test symmetrically should therefore refuse the mirror transaction.**
> `[E]`
>
> **Malaysia's own restrictions on foreign land ownership are what would block a Malaysian buyer
> in Italy.**

**Likely, not certain.** ⚠️ **Buy one notarial opinion before writing Italy off** — the inventory
justifies the cost. Workarounds if it fails: an **EU-incorporated company acquiring under freedom
of establishment**, or Italian residence.

### ⭐ San Simone — uncannily close to the benchmark

**13 runs** (6 red, 3 black, 4 blue), ~12 km of piste, in a cirque under **Monte Cavallo
(2,326 m)**, base ~1,600 m, closed five years. The owner is selling the lifts, the hotel and
*"buona parte dei terreni."* `[P2]`

**Thirteen runs — the same as 吉雪 — at 1,600 m base with ~400 m of vertical.**

⚠️ **Two problems.** *"A good part of the land"* is **not all of it** — and in this mandate the
part that matters is the top. And **the lifts are a removal liability, not an asset.**

### ⭐ La Chapelle-d'Abondance — the most useful single line in the European file

**A real, closed transaction for exactly this asset class and size, in an open jurisdiction:
64 ha of *alpage* plus a 340 m² chalet, €700,000 — about €10,900/ha.** `[P2]`

> **It proves the asset class trades, at a price, at the right size.** ⚠️ **Note who bought it:
> the *département*. Public bodies compete in this market** — the same pattern that took Tolland
> Ranch in Colorado to a state wildlife agency.

## 3. 🆕 The finding the brief did not anticipate: Scandinavia

**It is the only European region evidenced as simultaneously:**

1. **Indifferent to a Malaysian passport** — the permit tests are land-use, not nationality;
2. **Snow-secure at an *affordable* altitude** — latitude substitutes for altitude, and the
   reliable season sits at **500–900 m**, where land is cheap and privately held;
3. **Full of failing small ski areas.**

**Bjorli (Lesja, Norway) delivers 650 m of vertical and 11 runs with a base near 600 m — and went
bankrupt in 2025 with NOK 40 m of debt.** `[P2]`

> **Why they are failing is the point.** Small Scandinavian areas are collapsing under **lift
> electricity and staff costs — precisely the two costs a snowcat-and-skins owner does not
> carry.** `[E]` The thing killing them is the thing this thesis has already designed out.

### ⚠️ But two things must be resolved first, and they conflict with an earlier finding

This study's earlier jurisdiction screen **killed Sweden** on exactly two grounds:

- ***Renskötselrätt*** — the Sámi reindeer husbandry right, constitutionally protected by
  *urminnes hävd*, which **burdens private freehold, survives sale, and cannot be excluded**
  across the whole fjäll west of the *odlingsgränsen*;
- ***Allemansrätten* / *allemannsretten*** — statutory public access over *utmark*.

**Neither prevents ownership. Both may make "private" impossible.**

> **The two analyses do not agree, and the disagreement is not resolvable from the evidence
> either produced.** It turns on a question only the client can answer:
>
> ### **Does "private" mean legal exclusivity, or practical solitude?**
>
> If the requirement is a **legal right to exclude**, Scandinavia is out, and the earlier screen
> was right. If it is **practical solitude** — a remote hill where nobody else wants to be, and
> the public right of access is real but unexercised — then Norway and Sweden are among the
> strongest options found anywhere, on all three of passport, snow and price. `[E]`

## 4. ⚠️ The snow screen was too loose and must be tightened

The 1,200 m base / 1,600 m top figure used earlier in this study is **a 1990s number.** Abegg's
reliability line was 1,200 m for Switzerland, **rising ~150 m per °C**; current consensus is
**1,500–1,800 m**, and **snowmaking cannot rescue a season below ~1,500–1,600 m.** `[P2]`

**Revised European thresholds:**

| Range | Base | Top |
|---|---|---|
| **Alps and Pyrenees** | **1,500 m** | **1,900 m** |
| Apennines | 1,600 m | 2,000 m |
| Carpathians | 1,200 m | 1,600 m |
| **Scandinavia** | **500 m** | **800 m** |

**Three cheap-because-no-snow cases already in the file, named so they are not revisited:**

- **El Bierzo village** (Spain) — 20 ha, €420,000, at ~700 m
- **Les Fourgs** (France) — €800,000, 900–1,150 m
- **Krågeland** (Agder, Norway) — NOK 2.5 m, and **opened two days in a season**

## 5. The risk that most threatens the mandate in Europe

> **"The title must include the top" is the hardest clause in Europe, because in most of the
> continent the top is not privately owned at all.**

| Country | What sits on the summit |
|---|---|
| **Italy** | Frequently ***demanio civico*** — and **Law 168/2017 makes collective assets inalienable and non-usucapible.** `[P1]` **A sale of such land is *void*, not merely burdened** |
| **Spain** | *Montes de utilidad pública* |
| **France** | Communal or sectional land |

**Two further traps:**

- **Austria: *Weiderechte* (grazing rights) survive sale and bind third parties even when
  unregistered**, after 30 years' prescription. `[P2]` **A clean *Grundbuch* extract is not proof
  the land is unburdened.**
- **Italy: art. 1051 c.c.** gives a statutory right of way to a landlocked parcel — **but the
  Cassazione holds that merely tolerated physical passage is not enough.** `[P1/P2]` **You need a
  real registered right, or you are litigating.** This mirrors the Japanese 囲繞地通行権 finding
  exactly: **across jurisdictions, a statutory access right is not a usable access right.**
