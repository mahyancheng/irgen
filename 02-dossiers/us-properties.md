## 1. The finding: the arbitrage is real, and the supply is not

> **Nothing currently listed clears the client's alpine test.** The reason is structural, not
> financial: **in the Rockies the deed almost always stops at the National Forest boundary,
> because that is where the homestead and patent process stopped in 1900. The flank is private;
> the summit is federal.** `[E]`

Two properties in the survey unambiguously carried alpine terrain in fee. **Both are sold.**

| Property | Detail | Price | USD/acre | MYR/acre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cielo Vista Ranch** (Costilla Co., CO) | **83,368 deeded acres including Culebra Peak, 14,047 ft**, plus 18 peaks over 13,000 ft and 22 miles of Sangre de Cristo ridgeline | USD 105 m, 2017 | **1,259** | RM 5,147 |
| **Tolland Ranch** (Gilpin/Boulder Co., CO) | 3,334 deeded acres, **8,000 ft to ~13,000 ft**, taking in Buckeye and Tennessee Mountains | Asked USD 9.9 m | 2,969 | RM 12,139 |

`[P2/P3]` **Tolland sold to The Conservation Fund and passed to Colorado Parks & Wildlife. It is
now public land.**

### Read those two prices carefully

> **Deeded alpine traded *cheaper per acre* than mid-elevation grazing ground.** Culebra Peak —
> a 14,000-foot summit in fee — came at USD 1,259/acre against USDA's 2025 mountain-region
> pastureland average of USD 946/acre and Montana pasture at USD 920.
>
> **The client's arbitrage thesis is correct, and this is the hardest evidence for it anywhere
> in the study.** Altitude is worth nothing to an agricultural bidder.

**The constraint is supply, not price.** This asset surfaces roughly **once every five to eight
years** — and **state agencies and land trusts are the competing bidders**, as Tolland
demonstrates. That is a different kind of competition from a market one: they are not
price-sensitive in the same way, and they take the asset permanently out of private hands.

### ⭐ Black Hawk Ranch — the only live US property that could compete

**20,000 fee acres, no leased component, at USD 1,750/acre, with 1,067 m of verified vertical,
on the Wasatch Plateau.** `[P3]`

Three things make it the value pick:

- **All deeded.** No BLM or USFS permit component to discount.
- **The largest verified deeded vertical of anything live**, at the lowest price per acre.
- **Wasatch Plateau means intermountain snowpack** — which, per §5 below, is materially safer
  than Colorado's continental pack — on **rolling high plateau that is naturally low-angle**,
  exactly the terrain profile the screening framework asks for.

Its 9,500 ft high point is **2,896 m**, just under the 3,000 m Utah bar.

> **The entire question turns on a plat and a USGS overlay this study could not obtain.**
> Verifying Black Hawk's deeded boundary against elevation is the single highest-value action in

**Two conclusions** `[E]`:

1. **Irrigated valley ground is 6–10× pasture and is what inflates most asking prices.** Filter
   it out rather than paying for it.
2. **Resort proximity costs about 4× for the same dirt** — and for a *private* mountain with no
   lift and no public, **that premium buys nothing.**

> **Working band: USD 1,700–2,600 per deeded acre.** At that, **USD 20 m (RM 81.8 m) buys
> 7,700–11,800 deeded acres.**

In per-hectare terms, USD 1,750/acre ≈ **RM 17,700/ha** — against **RM 7,200/ha** for New
Zealand high country and **RM 1,024/ha** for Chilean cordillera. **The US is roughly 17× the
Chilean price for land, and about 2.5× New Zealand's.**

## 5. Snow — this inverts the intuition

`[P2]` **Colorado is *continental* snowpack statewide and is notorious for depth hoar: by
February the entire pack may be facets.** The **Wasatch Plateau (Utah), most of Idaho and
Montana are *intermountain***, where depth hoar is an early-season phase that heals.

**Grand Mesa and the Flat Tops are excellent *terrain* in the *worst* snowpack** — workable, but
only with a hard-coded sub-30° protocol and professional avalanche staffing, **an OPEX line not
yet budgeted anywhere in this study.**

**Ranking:** **Wasatch Plateau > Bighorns > SW Montana** (Pioneer / Beaverhead / Big Hole — snow
excellent, **midwinter road access is the binding constraint**) **> Sawtooth / Smoky** (a spring
asset, stable after mid-February) **> Colorado Flat Tops.**

## 6. Conservation easements — and the one strong reason to prefer Colorado

`[P1]` **IRC §873(b)(2) explicitly allows a non-resident alien the §170 charitable deduction**,
whether or not connected with effectively connected income. But `[P2]` in practice it only
shelters income already taxed on a net basis — and a cattle operation's ECI is small, so a large
donation generates a deduction **the client cannot absorb.**

> **Colorado sidesteps this entirely: a state credit at 90 % of easement value, capped at
> USD 5 m (RM 20.4 m), which is transferable and sellable for cash.** `[P1]` **Its value does not
> depend on the owner having any US tax liability at all.**
>
> **This is the strongest single reason to prefer Colorado** — and it partly offsets Colorado's
> bad snowpack.

*Not verified: whether Colorado imposes donor residency conditions.* `[U]`

⚠️ **The catch:** easements can reserve skiing rights, but land trusts resist new roads,
run-cutting and public commercial recreation — **and perpetuity conflicts directly with the
"expand if demand justifies" clause of the thesis.**

## 7. Two cost lines that could decide it

### Insurance — hardening fast

`[P2]` NSAA reports **ski-area insurance up 54 % year on year**; Timberline (OR) saw a **premium
up 166 % in one year** with a deductible roughly 10× higher and **liability up ~600 % since
2020**. Colorado non-renewals rose **77 % (2018–23)**, and the **FAIR Plan is capped at
USD 750,000 (RM 3.07 m) — likely below lodge replacement cost. Montana has no FAIR Plan at all.**

`[E]` The owner-and-guests phase is cheap. A fee-paying club is **USD 50k–250k/yr**. And
**snowcat passengers in avalanche terrain may be unplaceable at any price.**

> **No quotation was obtained. This is the largest unpriced item in the US case.**

### Severed minerals — the default, not the exception

`[P2]` *"Most of the mineral rights in the western U.S. have been partially or entirely
severed,"* and **the mineral estate is dominant** — the holder can access the surface.

> **Verify by a full chain-to-patent, not by a title commitment.**

## 8. Patented mining claims — a supporting asset, not a foundation

This study earlier identified patented claims as the mechanism behind private US summits, and
Silverton Mountain as the proof. That stands — **but it must be qualified.**

`[P2]` Claims trade at **USD 1,058–9,982/acre at 10,000–11,600 ft.** However:

- **A lode claim maxes out at ~20.66 acres.**
- **The gaps between claims are National Forest** — so any lift or groomed line crossing them
  **needs exactly the USFS permit this thesis exists to avoid.**
- Fractional undivided interests are common.
- **CERCLA strict, joint-and-several liability** applies, and the **Bonita Peak Superfund
  district** makes Eureka Gulch acquisitions genuinely dangerous.

> **Patented claims are a supporting asset, not a foundation.** Silverton assembled ~600 acres of
> them successfully — but that was a specialist doing it on the ground over years, with a
> tolerance for federal-boundary complexity that this mandate does not have.

## 9. Verdict

> **On this mandate as written, the United States probably does not beat Chile — because you
> cannot currently buy the mountain.**

Every live listing tops out below the client's threshold. The two properties that qualified are
gone, and the most recent went to a **state wildlife agency**. Layered on that: a hardening
wildfire market with **no Montana backstop**, ski liability up ~600 % in five years, severed
mineral estates as the default, and a conservation-easement subsidy that is **federally near-
useless to a Malaysian owner outside Colorado.**

**The US case remains strong on:** clean freehold title with no foreign-ownership bar, a deep and
transparent brokerage market, genuinely cheap high-elevation dirt at **USD 1,750–2,600/acre**,
working agricultural tax classification, and — uniquely — **Colorado's cash-sellable easement
credit.**

**Recommendation: do not close the US search, but do not lead with it. Verify Black Hawk Ranch's
elevation and deeded boundary first.** Twenty thousand fee acres with 1,067 m of vertical at
USD 1,750/acre in an intermountain snowpack is the only US property that could plausibly beat a
Southern Hemisphere alternative.
