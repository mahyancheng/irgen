## 1. ⭐ The farm-land question resolves — and it changes the New Zealand strategy

This was flagged as the highest-value legal question in the study. It has a provisional answer.

**Section 6(1), Overseas Investment Act 2005** `[P1]`:

> *"**farm land** means land … **used exclusively or principally** for agricultural,
> horticultural, or pastoral purposes, or for the keeping of bees, poultry, or livestock
> **(which, to avoid doubt, do not include forestry activities)**."*

**Two features decide it:**

1. It is a **current-use test** — not zoning, not capability, not intention.
2. **Forestry is expressly carved out.**

And LINZ's own published worked example of rural land that is **not** farm land is:
*"Investment in 9 hectares of rural land to develop luxury tourist accommodation."* `[P1]`

**So a defunct ski area, or a de-stocked alpine title, is provisionally NOT farm land.** The land
remains **sensitive** (non-urban, over 5 ha) and still needs consent — but it **escapes the
30-working-day advertising obligation and the whole farmland benefit machinery.**

### Two consequences the client must act on `[E]`

> **(a) "Ranch **or** forest" is a choice, not a pair.**
>
> The client's thesis lists ranching *and* forestry together. Under s 6(1) they are opposites:
> **grazing makes the land pastoral, and therefore farm land. Forestry does not.** Running
> stock on a New Zealand property is the act that pulls it into the hardest consent regime in
> the study. A forestry-and-recreation holding does not.

> **(b) The test bites at the transaction, not at the plan.**
>
> **Buying a working grazed station is buying farm land, whatever you intend to do next.** The
> escape route only works for land that is *already* not principally pastoral — a defunct ski
> area, a forestry block, or a de-stocked alpine title.
>
> **This is an argument for buying the resort title, not the station.** It inverts the search:
> the thing to look for in New Zealand is not a big run with a mountain on it, but a
> non-pastoral alpine title.

⚠️ **This is a reading of the statutory words plus one LINZ example. It is not precedent.** No
OIO decision or law-firm commentary testing a ski area was found. **A tightly-scoped New Zealand
counsel opinion on this single question is the highest-leverage legal spend available in the
whole study.** One unexamined lead: OIO case 201010070, Washland Holdings HB Trust.

## 2. Mt Lyford — it did not sell, and the reason it was listed matters

**No sale found. On the balance of the record the Simpson family still owns and operates it.**
Every 2025–26 description is present-tense and names the family as owner-operators of both
basins (Terako and Lake Stella); the field opened for the 2026 season on **2 July 2026**. `[P2]`
No asking price and no achieved price was ever published — the "tens of millions" was vendor
talk, never tested by a buyer. `[P3]`

*This is absence of evidence, not proof: New Zealand prices are routinely withheld.*

> **The trigger matters more than the campaign.** Co-founder **Hamish Simpson died on 18 July
> 2024, aged 44, of brain cancer**; the expressions-of-interest campaign launched four months
> later. `[P2 — The Press]` **This is a succession sale.** Two years on with no result, **a
> direct, unbrokered approach to Doug Simpson is the most favourable negotiating posture a
> private buyer will find anywhere in the New Zealand file.**

### ⚠️ Correction to the elevation figures

Earlier drafts of this study put Mt Lyford at ~1,460 m and treated it as failing the altitude
screen. The better figures are:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Base | **1,249 m** |
| Summit | **1,750 m** |
| **Vertical** | **501 m** |
| Skiable area | 180 ha |
| Lifts | 6 surface lifts |

`[P3 — aggregator data, unverified against survey]`

**A 1,750 m summit clears the 1,600 m screen, and 501 m of vertical meets the target band.** The
asset is better on paper than this study previously recorded. Two qualifications stand:
**1,750 m in a maritime coastal range is still marginal for snow**, and — decisively —
**whether the 677 ha title actually contains that summit is a title question that remains
unanswered.**

## 3. The Inland Kaikōura thesis fails

The most promising unexplored ground in New Zealand was tested and **does not deliver.**

**The alpine core is Crown land.** Tapuae-o-Uenuku's Mt Gladstone block was returned to the
Crown through a Pastoral Occupational Licence review and is now scenic reserve, and *"the Camden
Crown pastoral lease includes much of the land below the peak."* `[P1 — DOC/LINZ]` The range
reproduces the national pattern exactly: **tops to the Crown, valleys freehold.**

| Station | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Glazebrook** | 8,877 ha, 100 % freehold — but the high country only *"runs to 1,600 m"* `[P3]`. 2021 sale outcome **not established** |
| **Upton Fells** | 7,857 ha freehold; peaks 1,250 m, majority below 850 m. **Fails decisively** |
| **Muzzle** | 2007 tenure review sent ~10,000 ha to DOC, ~8,000 ha retained freehold — expected to be valley country `[E]`. Not for sale |
| **Middlehurst** | 1998 split: 5,303 ha freehold / 11,420 ha lease. Current tenure not established |
| Bluff (~13,800 ha), Tempello (~5,000 ha), Erina | Tenure not established; Tempello is on the wrong range and too low |

**Which hold freehold above 1,600 m? On the evidence, none.**

### But one property proves freehold alpine title exists in New Zealand

**Puhi Peaks Station** — 1,618 ha on the *Seaward* Kaikōura range, described as *"the highest
privately owned property in New Zealand, rising from 400 m to 2,600 m."* `[P3 — owner]`

**It is not for sale, and 810 ha sits under a QEII covenant registered on the title, binding all
successors.** `[P1-adjacent]`

> **Two lessons.** Freehold alpine title in New Zealand is not impossible — it exists, at
> 2,600 m. And **any New Zealand alpine freehold with conservation value is a covenant
> candidate**, which would defeat the development optionality the client is buying. **Check the
> title for QEII covenants before anything else.**

## 5. 🆕 New lead — Porters / Crystal Valley

**A documented DOC-to-private freehold grant of alpine land**, which this study did not previously have.

In 2011, **DOC granted the Crystal Valley freehold to Blackfish Ltd** in exchange for 70 ha on
Banks Peninsula plus 320 ha in Porters Valley — a competing account gives 198 ha, and the figures
conflict. `[P2, 2011]` Blackfish was subsequently sold to **PSA Capital, reported as 60 %
Russian / 40 % Australian.** `[P2 — dated]`

**Current ownership is not established.**

> This is a **third New Zealand freehold alpine candidate**, and the only one created by an
> explicit Crown grant rather than by tenure-review history. It is worth pursuing.
>
> ⚠️ **Sanctions screening is required before any approach**, given the reported ownership.
