> **ODEPA's national *minimum* observed farmland price is 5.8 UF/ha. The Colbún cordillera
> block prices at 5.9 UF/ha.** These blocks transact **at the observed floor of the entire
> Chilean rural land market** — 30× to 79× below irrigated Ñuble ground, and a 30× to 150×
> discount to the CLP 18–35 m/ha seen around Chillán Viejo and Bulnes.

**Why the floor exists, and why it is the arbitrage.** Chilean farmland is priced off
**irrigation water and soil class.** Cordillera has neither. **Every attribute this client wants
— vertical, aspect, altitude, remoteness — is worth exactly zero to every other bidder.** `[E]`

**The corollary, which must be stated plainly:** resale liquidity is close to nil. Nobody else
wants it, which is why it is cheap. **This is a hold-forever asset, not a trade.**

For scale: **RM 1,024/ha in Chile against RM 7,200/ha for New Zealand high country** — seven
times cheaper, in a jurisdiction with no foreign-ownership consent regime.

## 3. Does freehold-to-the-summit exist? — the question resolves, and it splits favourably

**The finding does not invert. It splits along "developed vs undeveloped", not "freehold vs
concession".**

**Developed, lift-served mountains are mostly not freehold** `[P2]`:

| Mountain | How the land is held |
|---|---|
| **Nevados de Chillán** | A **30-year exploitation concession over the Termas predio, awarded by the Municipalidad de Chillán.** The operator does not own it — 10,000 ha skiable domain, only 500 ha developed |
| **Corralco** | CONAF concession inside **Reserva Nacional Malalcahuello** |
| Volcán Osorno | Parque Nacional Vicente Pérez Rosales |
| Antillanca | Parque Nacional Puyehue |
| Chapa Verde | **Codelco** corporate land |
| La Parva | Bienes Nacionales confirmed it **will sell a fiscal property in 2026** as a residents' concession expires |

**But undeveloped alpine terrain is ordinary private property, and two cases prove it:**

> ### ⭐ Nevados de Longaví (Maule, ~36.2 °S) is private freehold, on the record
>
> Owner-spokesman **Enrique Gardeweg** states the family *"do not want to cede the lands, and
> wish to always conserve ownership"* — while remaining **open to a commercial agreement.** A
> ski centre is under study on it: **~1,000 m of vertical**, with good snow quality. `[P2]`
>
> **A named family holds a Chilean *nevado* with assessed ski potential, in the priority
> latitude band, and is publicly courting private partners.** This is the single most important
> property finding in the study — and the correct approach is a **lease, usufruct or joint
> venture, not a purchase offer**, because they have said they will not sell.

> ### Ski Arpa is the exact operating precedent
>
> Tony Sponar **bought mountain land in the 1960s** and runs South America's only cat-ski
> operation on it — **freehold alpine, snowcats, no lifts.** `[P2]` This is the client's Phase 1
> model, already working, in Chile.

**Why the split runs this way** `[E]`: lifts cluster inside SNASPE and municipal land because
that is where the public roads went. **The no-lift, no-construction model is precisely the one
that does not need the concession estate.**

⚠️ **The caveat that no amount of searching can close:** Andean titles often stop at a reserve
boundary, a contour, or the *línea de altas cumbres*. **Whether a given predio's title reaches
the summit is answerable only from the Conservador's *deslindes*.** That was verified for
**zero** properties here.

## 4. Holding cost — cheap, with one number still missing

`[P1/P2]` The agricultural **exempt *avalúo* is CLP 24,222,145**, and the rate was **cut from
1 % to 0.514 %.** *Avalúo* depends on soil class, surface, sector, and carries a deduction for
road type and distance to the municipal seat — **remote, bad road, class VIII produces the
system's lowest values.**

At 0.514 %, a CLP 500 m *avalúo* costs about **CLP 2.45 m a year ≈ RM 10,950 ≈ USD 2,574.**

> **The one number still missing is the class-VIII CLP/ha figure** — the SII table sits behind a
> blocked link. **It decides whether a 19,000 ha holding lands near the exemption or an order of
> magnitude above it**, and it is free to obtain from an unrestricted connection. It remains the
> highest-value open number in the Chilean case.

## 6. Volcanic risk — and why the no-build model mitigates it

`[P1]` SERNAGEOMIN's worst case for **Antuco is lahars down the Río Laja at the end of winter,
when snow volume peaks — the hazard maximum coincides with the ski season.** At Chillán, modelled
lahar paths run down **Renegado and Shangri-La**, the Las Trancas corridor.

Volcanic cover **is** available in Chilean multi-risk policies (named-peril; adobe excluded; ash
removal only where ash caused direct damage). `[P2]`

> **The no-construction model is a genuine structural mitigant: lahars destroy buildings, not
> grazing land.** `[E]` A thesis with no permanent build has materially less at risk on an active
> volcano than a resort does — another instance of the low-CAPEX design paying off in a way that
> has nothing to do with cost.
