## 1. The municipal disposal market is real, public, and enterable

Two structural facts, and they matter more than any individual asset `[P2/E]`:

- **More than ten Japanese ski areas effectively cease operating every year.**
- **Sellers have moved from 水面下の交渉 (behind-the-scenes negotiation) to public portals.**

> **That second shift is what makes this market enterable by a non-resident without a Japanese
> sponsor.** It is a recent change and it is the reason Japan is worth the effort despite the
> tenure problem.

### Confirmed: the Murakami template was a free transfer

Previously unverified, now confirmed `[P2]`: the transfer of **村上市ぶどうスキー場** to
**株式会社シンクファースト** was **無償 — free of charge.** The city closed it in March 2025 and
a private operator reopened it as ぶどうスノーリゾート for the 2025/26 season.

**That is the working template for the whole play.**

Corroborating case: **だいせんホワイトリゾート (Daisen White Resort, Tottori)** — Nihon Kotsu
exited and **transferred all facilities to the town free of charge**; after one designated-manager
candidate failed to agree a plan, the town re-solicited and **Adoba took over in April 2026.** `[P2]`

### ⚠️ And the warning case, which is equally instructive

**ニュー・グリーンピア津南 (New Greenpia Tsunan, Niigata)** `[P2]`. Town-owned. **Savills Japan
ran a formal broker tender.** Two bidders. **INTRANCE (Tokyo) was granted preferred negotiating
rights** — and then, on **13 March 2026, the town assembly passed a resolution opposing the sale
and demanding cancellation of the basic agreement.**

> A properly-run, broker-managed municipal disposal was **overturned by the elected assembly
> after the winning bidder was selected.** In Japanese municipal ski disposals, **the political
> risk sits after the commercial agreement, not before it.** Budget time and legal cost for it,
> and do not treat preferred-bidder status as a deal.

23 it is unknown, **and it is the only field that matters.**

## 2. Itoigawa — timing and economics resolved, tenure not

The city owns **both** areas, on two separate clocks `[P2 — Niigata Nippo, Joetsu Times]`:

| Asset | Transfer timetable |
|---|---|
| **シーサイドバレー (Seaside Valley)** | Concrete procedures **begin FY2026 (令和8年度)**, target completion **by FY2028**. Designated management moved to **1-year renewals** from FY2026 — an explicit "keep it short so we can hand it over" posture `[E]` |
| **シャルマン火打 (Charmant Hiuchi)** | Transfer targeted **FY2029 (令和11年度) or later**, studied within a 3-year FY2026–28 management period |

> ⚠️ **Correction to earlier drafts of this study: there is no 募集要項 (prospectus).** What was
> reported is *entry into the procedures leading toward* a 公募 — not a published tender. No
> conditions, no eligibility rules, and nothing on foreign applicants.

### The published economics — and they are the negotiating lever

Total operating cost, of which 指定管理料 is the cash subsidy `[P2]`:

| FY | Seaside Valley | of which subsidy | Charmant Hiuchi | of which subsidy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | ≈ ¥90.9 m | **¥39.0 m** | ¥95.54 m | **¥46.5 m** |
| 2023 | ≈ ¥87.53 m | ¥39.0 m | ≈ ¥105.23 m | **¥43.5 m** |
| 2024 | ≈ ¥80.42 m | ¥39.0 m | ≈ ¥91.73 m | ¥43.5 m |

The city's 商工観光課 said of Seaside Valley: **「市の指定管理料がないと黒字にできない」** — *it
cannot be made profitable without the subsidy.* `[P2]`

> These are **two structural ¥39–46 m/year losses.** The price may well be nominal or zero.
> **The liability is the deficit and the lift fleet, not the purchase price.** `[E]`

### The physical assets

| | Charmant Hiuchi | Seaside Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Base → top | **500 → 1,009 m** | 240 → 740 m |
| Vertical | **509 m** | 500 m |
| Area | ≈ **94 ha** | 74.5 ha |
| Lifts | 1 quad + 2 pairs | 5 |
| Courses | 16 | 6 (4 km longest, **max 20°**) |

`[P3]`

**Charmant Hiuchi is the better asset** — its 1,009 m top clears the warming-resilience threshold
where Seaside Valley's 740 m does not, and a 20° maximum pitch is a gentle hill. `[E]`

> ⚠️ **But at 74.5 ha and 94 ha, neither is a landholding.** They are operating businesses on
> parcels the size of one medium 山林. **They do not satisfy the mandate's "large alpine
> landholding" requirement on any reading.**

**And the decisive question is unresolved:** whether the pistes are 市有地, a 国有林 lease, or
財産区 land; whether *land* is even in scope — **the Murakami precedent transferred facilities,
with no land mentioned**; and whether a foreign entity may apply at all.

> **Working expectation `[E]`: a facilities-and-business transfer on land the city retains or
> leases.** The reporting is consistently about the deficit and never about a land price. **If
> so, Itoigawa fails the freehold requirement** — and becomes a Mt Dobson-style "control, not
> title" asset. Three phone calls settle it.

## 3. The finding that could reframe the whole Japanese case

`[P1]` **The Forestry Agency names スキー場跡地 (former ski area sites) as an established category
of national-forest land leased for non-forestry use** — alongside former golf courses, nurseries
and dam material yards.

This cuts both ways `[E]`:

- ✅ It is **the strongest support found anywhere for a "freehold base + national-forest lease
  above" structure** — the arrangement that would let a Japanese acquisition satisfy "controls
  the mountain" even where it cannot satisfy "owns the peak."
- ❌ It also means **a large part of the ex-piste inventory is not purchasable at all**, because
  it sits on land the state owns and merely leases out.

**On the lease terms** `[P1]`: national forest leases are **term-limited and renewed by
application** (国有林野貸付等更新申請書). MAFF publishes sector manuals, and the **wind and
geothermal manual, amended March 2025**, is the template carrying term, rent formula and
eligibility.

> ⚠️ **The foreign-eligibility question never ran** — it hit the exhausted search budget. There
> is **no evidence either way**, and given the Cabinet Secretariat panel reporting in summer
> 2026, it should be treated as **open and politically live.** It remains the single highest-value
> unanswered question in the Japanese file.

## 4. Corrections and unresolved items

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Large land (≥500 ha)** | ⚠️ **NOT ESTABLISHED.** Nothing above **363.6 ha** (Hamatonbetsu, price unpublished) was found across two research passes. **Akaigawa, 74.4 ha at ¥25 m (≈ ¥336k/ha, 地目 山林) remains the best land lead.** `[E]` **A single 500 ha private title with real vertical looks close to non-existent on the open market.** The realistic structures are **parcel assembly** or **freehold base plus lease** |
| **JMA snow normals** | **UNRESOLVED — never reached.** The snow-depth table in `07-terrain-and-snow/` remains self-flagged as recalled and unverified, and Furano, Akaigawa/Yoichi and Hachimantai are absent from it entirely. Two real elevation profiles were obtained (§2). `[E]` Both Niigata coastal areas yield almost exactly **500 m of vertical** — which looks like what a Sea-of-Japan coastal massif gives you, and that is **the bottom of the useful range** |
| **専用索道 exemption** | **Material progress, not closed.** `[P1]` Art. 32 requires MLIT permission per ropeway **"except ropeways prescribed by MLIT ordinance"** — and that exemption list could not be retrieved. It is the provision that answers the question. Best new fact: the Act's Chapter 4 regulates **専用鉄道** defined by **physical connection to a commercial railway line** `[E]` — the statute's theory of when private own-use infrastructure is pulled back in is **network connection**, and a tow on a private mountain connects to nothing. **Honest caveat: two searches conflicted on whether 専用索道 survives as a live statutory category, and the study will not guess between them.** The paid-lodging trap remains unanswered, and the expectation remains adverse |
| **Public pastures (公共牧場)** | Not reached — but the legal test is already settled and needs no re-work: **山林 / 原野 / 牧場 sit outside 農地法.** Buy those and graze them; never buy a 牧場 registered as 農地 |
| **Municipal 公有財産売却 pages** | **Not checked — and this is the zero-cost channel to hit first.** Start with **士別市 Shibetsu** (abolished 30 June 2026, "future use not announced") and **室蘭市 Muroran** |
| **Lift removal cost** | Not reached. `[E]` **This is a deal term, not trivia.** A "free" municipal area with three lifts to demolish is priced at an unknown decommissioning liability, and that liability could exceed any plausible land value |
