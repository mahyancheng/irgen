# Can you legally ski a mountain you don't own in Japan?

**Short answer: yes. The premise holds.** But *skiing* and *commercially guiding* are different
questions with different answers, and the liability exposure is the part that should worry you.

MYR at ¥1 = 0.02540.

---

## 1. National forest (国有林) — recreation needs no filing

Administered by the 林野庁 under the 国有林野の管理経営に関する法律 (Act No. 246/1951). The default
is that advance procedure (**入林届**) is required "except in defined cases" — **and recreation is
one of the exceptions.** The Kanto Bureau states it flatly `[P1]`:

> **「登山や森林浴などの森林レクリエーションを目的として入林される場合は、入林届の提出は不要」**
> — *no 入林届 is required for entry for the purpose of forest recreation such as mountaineering.*

**What *does* require 入林届:** hunting, drones, surveys, filming, events, alpine-plant collection,
and government staff. **Skiing appears on none of them.**

### ⚠️ The grey zone, stated honestly

**Hokkaido's Bureau words its exemption *facility-linked*:** no notification when entering "places
where recreation forests, ski areas, campgrounds, trails, footpaths or huts **are established**."

> **Read literally, trackless off-piste terrain is not obviously inside that text.** Nobody files,
> nobody is asked, and **no enforcement against recreational skiers was found** — but *unenforced*
> is not *exempt*. **A business must get this answered by the 後志森林管理署 directly.**

### The insight that explains why this works

**The penalties attach to *taking*, not to *being there*.** 森林窃盗 under 森林法197条 — theft of
forest produce including 山菜 and mushrooms — carries up to **3 years or ¥300,000** (5 years or
¥500,000 in 保安林). `[P1]`

> **No penal provision was found for mere recreational presence.** Skiing over snow removes nothing
> and damages nothing, which is precisely why it sits outside the enforcement architecture.
>
> **Corollary: never let guests forage. That is the actual crime.**

## 2. ⭐ Hokkaido prefectural forest (道有林) — the regulation names the business model

From the **後志総合振興局森林室** — the office covering Rusutsu and Niseko `[P1]`:

> **「道有林野内での自然活動を目的とする入林（旅行会社、ツアーガイド事業者等の組織が道有林野内で
> 登山やバックカントリースキーなどを企画し入林する場合など）については、入林承認申請書の提出が
> 必要です。」**
>
> *Entry into prefectural forest for nature activities — including where travel companies or tour
> guide operators organise mountaineering or **backcountry skiing** — requires an entry approval
> application.*

**Commercial guided backcountry skiing in 道有林 requires an approval application** (not merely a
notification), reviewed on location, purpose, duration and permits held, with the **入林承認証
mailed in about a week.**

> **This is a light, routine, free administrative step — not a barrier. It is also the regulator
> describing the client's intended business in its own words, and permitting it.**
>
> **It confers zero exclusivity.**

## 3. Private forest (民有林) — the understanding is correct on both counts

| Provision | Reach | Applies to open mountain forest? |
|---|---|---|
| **刑法130条** (unlawful entry) | Residences, guarded buildings, vessels, and **囲繞地** — where 囲繞地 requires **physical enclosure *plus* attachment to a building** | ❌ **No** |
| **軽犯罪法1条32号** | (a) a place where entry is **prohibited by a manifested notice**, or (b) another's **田畑** | ⚠️ **Only via limb (a)** — 山林 is not 田畑, which means *cultivated* ground. Penalty 拘留・科料, the lowest tier |

`[P1]`

> ### The operational rule that falls out of this
>
> **No sign, no rope, no fence → no offence. Obey every sign and every rope, absolutely.**
>
> That single discipline keeps the entire activity lawful.

⚠️ **Two qualifications.** The client's **own lodge yard, or a leftover ranch paddock, *would* be
囲繞地** — the line is drawn at the buildings, not at the property boundary. And the civil remedy
(**妨害排除請求**) yields an **injunction, not damages** — still serious for a business, because it
can close a signature route with no compensation.

> **Japan has no right to roam. The tolerance is real, but it is revocable.**

## 4. ⭐ Natural parks — the best finding in the file

The **自然公園法 works by enumerating regulated acts**: construction, felling, extraction, land-form
alteration, taking plants or animals, changing colours, fire, grazing, storing materials, and
**車馬等の乗入れ**.

> **Entry itself is not on the list** — with one narrow exception, 自然公園法21条3項1号, covering
> **Minister-designated wetlands.** `[P1]`
>
> ### **Ski touring — no structure, no felling, no earthworks, no collection, no vehicle — is not a regulated act, even inside a 特別保護地区.**
>
> **This removes the "the high ground is all national park, therefore unusable" obstacle
> entirely** — the obstacle that has shaped this entire study.

### But snowmobiles are squarely caught

**車馬 is defined** by 環境省 and the 北海道地方環境事務所 as animals capable of carrying a person,
plus **車 moved by human, animal, combustion or other power** — cars, mopeds, **snowmobiles**,
bicycles, carts.

**Skis appear in no enumeration, and a ski is not a 車.** One source states it directly:
**「スキーは規制の対象に含まれていません」.** Snowshoes likewise. `[P1]`

| | Inside a park |
|---|---|
| **Skis, snowshoes** | ✅ **Not regulated** |
| **Snowmobiles, snowcats** | ❌ **1 year's imprisonment or ¥1,000,000** |

Hokkaido coverage is near-total for the alpine zone — all six national parks and all quasi-national
parks — and **enforcement in 大雪山 is active**, with season-long patrols and drone monitoring under
consideration.

**Two carve-outs to check per parcel:** designated **wetland entry zones**, and **利用調整地区**
(Use Adjustment Districts, such as 知床五湖) where entry *does* require certification. Rare, but
dispositive where they exist.

### ⭐ And a geographic find that matters

支笏洞爺国立公園's **羊蹄山地域 covers Kutchan, Kyōgoku, Kimobetsu, Makkari and Niseko.**

> **留寿都村 is not on the list.** `[P3]`
>
> `[E]` **So 尻別岳 (1,107 m) — the actual backyard mountain, with Rusutsu Resort sitting on its
> spur 橇負山 — is most likely outside every park.** That would mean **no Natural Parks Act
> constraint at all, including for snowmobiles and snowcats.**
>
> ⚠️ **This is inference from a municipality list, not a boundary reading. Confirm on 国土数値情報
> before relying on it.** Ownership split (国有 / 道有 / private) is unverified and determines which
> of the two regimes above applies.

## 5. ⚠️ Liability — the part that should genuinely worry you

### Civil, on the terrain: **leaving it untouched is a legal defence**

**長野地判平成13年2月1日** held that a steep natural slope — excluded from the lease, absent from the
operating licence, never felled or graded, with no-entry signage posted — was **not a 土地の工作物**,
so **民法717条 did not apply.** `[P2]`

**A groomed piste — felled, shaped, maintained — *is* a 工作物**, and carries the owner's **strict
liability, with no defence of care.**

> ### The design consequence is significant
>
> **Leaving the terrain untouched is not merely cheaper. It is a legal defence.**
>
> **Cutting glades, grading, or building a lift destroys that defence and imports strict liability
> that no amount of care can discharge.**
>
> Every previous argument in this study for the no-lift, no-grooming model was financial or
> regulatory. **This one is a liability argument, and it is stronger than either.**

### Criminal, on guiding: **this is the actual exposure**

| Case | Facts | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **札幌地裁小樽支部, 21 Mar 2000** | Paid snowshoe tour on **ニセコアンヌプリ**; avalanche at 春の滝; one dead, one injured | **Two guides convicted** of 業務上過失致死傷 — 禁錮8月, suspended 3 years |
| **宇都宮地裁, 30 May 2024** (那須) | **Eight dead** | **Three leaders sentenced to 禁錮2年 — actual imprisonment**, the court calling it **「相当に重い不注意による人災」**. Under appeal |

`[P2]`

> **The 2000 case is on all fours with the proposed activity — a paid winter tour, in the same
> region, an avalanche, convictions. The 2024 sentence shows the courts hardening.**

**Free, self-directed backcountry skiing is 自己責任. Paid and guided imports a high 安全配慮義務** —
weather analysis, participant assessment, equipment checks, rehearsed emergency procedure.

### Waivers do not save you

**民法90条** voids terms contrary to public policy; **消費者契約法** voids blanket exclusions and any
exclusion for 故意 or 重過失. `[E]`

> **And no waiver touches criminal liability at all.**

### Insurance

| Cover | Terms |
|---|---|
| 山岳ガイド賠償責任保険 (日本山岳ガイド協会 / 三井住友海上) | **¥200 m per person (RM 5.08 m) / ¥500 m per occurrence (RM 12.7 m)** bodily; ¥5 m property. One historical premium: **¥13,750 (RM 349)** |
| 施設賠償 / 旅館賠償 | Priced individually — ¥14,100/yr (RM 358) quoted for a restaurant |

**NWGA mandates helmets and transceivers for guides *and* guests — that is the de facto standard of
care**, and departing from it is what a prosecutor would point to.

## 6. ニセコルール — the working proof of the whole thesis

**It is not law.** It is a set of private ski-area rules enforced by pass confiscation.

But the Niseko documents state plainly that **the off-piste terrain is 国有林 or 道有林, inside a
quasi-national park, and unmanaged by the resorts.** `[P1/P2]`

> **An entire international powder economy runs on land nobody bought.**

**And every Hokkaido operator found fits the same pattern** — NWGA members, HME, 登り返し倶楽部
(based at *someone else's* lodge, 白銀荘), ゆきのこ, TAC, and **NAKAFU Mountain Base, founded in
2022 on nothing but a guide certification.**

> **The barrier to entry is certification, local knowledge and insurance — not title.**
>
> **Which cuts both ways: terrain buys no moat.** If the objective is a *business*, owning the
> mountain confers no advantage over competitors who own nothing. If the objective is a *place*,
> that is a different and entirely legitimate reason to buy — but it should be named as such.

## 7. ⚠️ Two structural risks outside the original brief

### The 経営・管理 visa got roughly six times harder on 16 October 2025

| | Before | **From 16 Oct 2025** |
|---|---|---|
| Capital | ¥5 m | **¥30 m (RM 762,000)** |
| Employees | Capital *or* one employee | **≥1 full-time employee mandatory** |
| Language | — | **Requirement added** |

**And holders may not perform operational work themselves.** `[P2]`

> `[E]` **The realistic structure is therefore: a non-resident owns a Japanese company** — lawful
> since 2015, with no resident director required, though **opening the bank account is the real
> chokepoint** — **with a resident Japanese manager or guide actually running it.**
>
> **The client would be a passive investor in his own lodge.** That is worth naming plainly,
> because it may not be the life he is imagining.

### Accommodation licensing — one genuine piece of good news

**From 15 June 2026 the 旅館業法 abolishes the minimum room count** — **one room is enough** — sets
a floor of ≥7 m² per room, and permits front-desk omission with ICT verification. `[P1]`

The **農家民宿** route waives the 33 m² floor-area rule entirely and escapes the 180-day minpaku cap
— **but presupposes genuine farming activity, which loops straight back into the 農地法 problem.**

**Unlicensed operation: 6 months and/or ¥1 m.**

### Guiding needs no travel-agency registration

**Pure guiding requires no 旅行業 registration.** Self-supplying the accommodation and bundling
other services sits **outside** the Travel Agency Act — the published worked example is a lodging
operator selling a package including a third party's golf course, which needs no registration. Free
guest shuttles within socially accepted bounds need no permit. `[P1]`

> `[E]` **Mountain Memories' "complimentary and optional" phrasing reads as deliberately engineered
> around exactly this boundary.** It is a well-understood structure, and it is copyable.
