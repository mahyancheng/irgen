# Japan — small parcels (40–200 ha)

Re-sourced against the revised 40 ha / 300 m benchmark.

---

## 1. The verdict, and a correction to this study's own reasoning

**No property meets all four conditions at once** — 40–200 ha, ≥ 300 m vertical, freehold
including the ridge, and currently on the market at a stated price.

> ### ⚠️ Correction: I over-claimed on the last revision
>
> The small-plot revision said Japan's *"two fatal flaws both evaporate."* **That is only half
> right, and the half that matters is wrong.**
>
> - ✅ **The size flaw does evaporate.** 40–150 ha parcels are ordinary; the 農地法 ranch problem
>   is void; holding costs are trivial. All confirmed.
> - ❌ **But the binding constraint was never the 500 ha scarcity.** It is **tenure over the
>   ridge — and shrinking the target does not relax it at all.**
>
> A smaller parcel is just as likely to run into national forest at the top. **Screening must
> start from the ownership map, not from the ski geometry.**

## 2. Why freehold-to-the-summit is the hard part

`[P1 — Forestry Agency]` **国有林 (national forest) is 7.69 m ha — 30 % of Japan's forest — and
is "widely distributed across the remote backbone mountain ranges (奥地の脊梁山地) and headwater
areas."** That is precisely the high-vertical snow terrain this mandate wants.

The Agency does sell national land, but **prioritises public-use disposal, and what it actually
offers is surplus nurseries and log yards, not ridges.** For ski use the mechanism is **貸付
(lease)**, with rent set from the inheritance-tax assessed value. `[P1]`

> **Where a summit is national forest, the client's absolute condition is unachievable at any
> price.**

### The second trap: a national park binds private land identically to public land

`[P1]` Under the 自然公園法, **special areas require permission for buildings and earthworks, and
1st-type special areas and special protection districts are held at status quo.**

> **Buying freehold inside a national park does not buy the right to cut a piste or erect a
> tow.** Most of the target snow regions are inside parks. `[E]`

Two further consents that bite at this scale:

- **林地開発許可** — any clearing or earthworks **over 1 ha** in private forest needs the
  governor's permission, and **ski-resort construction is expressly named** in the regime. `[P1]`
- **保安林** de-designation is *"in principle not possible"* — though the public-interest
  exception **expressly lists ski resorts.** Serious, but not automatically fatal. `[P1]`

### Seaside Valley — the actionable one on timing

**74.5 ha, 500 m of vertical**, same city-owned land-and-buildings structure. **Procedures begin

## 5. The small-parcel risks, sharpened

### Access — and a trap that would be fatal to discover late

`[P1]` A landlocked parcel gets a **statutory right of passage under Civil Code Art. 210
(囲繞地通行権)** that neighbours cannot refuse. **But vehicular passage is not automatically
guaranteed**, the route is confined to the least-damaging line, and a payment is owed.

> **That can leave you with a *walking* right — worthless for a snowcat, a piste basher or a fuel
> truck.**
>
> **Make any offer conditional on either direct frontage to a public road, or a registered
> 通行地役権 (right-of-way easement) expressly covering tracked and heavy vehicles year-round.**

### Winter clearing — decisive for a non-resident owner

`[P1 — multiple municipal sources]` **Municipalities do not clear private roads.** For
生活道路 it is the beneficiaries' own responsibility, and dead-ends with nowhere to push snow are
practically unclearable. And for mountain villa land specifically, **because the owner is not
resident-registered, the city does not plough to the property.** `[P3]`

> **Prefer terrain whose access road is already a 市町村道 on an existing ploughing route — and
> verify the legal classification, not the appearance of the road.**

### Boundaries

`[P1]` Cadastral survey completion is **53 % nationally but only 47 % for forest land and 44 % in
mountain villages — over 100,000 km² of forest has never been surveyed.**

**Buy on 実測売買** (sale by measured area) with boundary confirmation. Where the uphill neighbour
is national forest, **the Forest Management Office must be a party to the boundary agreement.**

### Neighbours

**If the upper boundary abuts 国有林 or 保安林, your vertical is permanently capped at whatever
sits inside the title** — establish this before valuing any parcel on its drop. `[E]` Downhill
neighbours can clear-fell your runout; consider optioning it.

## 6. ⚠️ A timing risk that has hardened since the last pass

**Foreign acquisition rules are actively tightening, and the focus is precisely on this asset
class.**

- Forest acquisition already requires notification to the mayor within 90 days regardless of area
  (森林法 10-7-2), and **nationality became a reporting item from 1 July 2024**, with the form
  revised again on **1 April 2025.** `[P1]`
- The Cabinet Secretariat convened the **外国人による土地取得等のルールの在り方検討会 on
  4 March 2026**, to produce a basic policy in **summer 2026** and a legislative outline — with a
  **declared focus on water-source and forest conservation.** `[P1/P2]`

> Acquisition by a foreign individual remains **legal and unrestricted today.** But this is
> **the least favourable moment in memory to be a foreign buyer of Japanese forest and headwater
> land**, and the declared policy focus lands squarely on it. **Re-check the summer-2026 output
> before committing.**

## 7. Method caveat, and the right next step

Every specialist property domain was blocked (yamaichiba, happiness-wood-bank, skibumpslabo), and
the search index returned only category pages for 山いちば, 家いちば, ハピネスウッドバンク,
不動産連合隊, ジモティー, 楽待 and 健美家 — **never an individual large-parcel listing with area,
price and elevation together.**

> **The absence of a qualifying 40–200 ha listing here is a tooling limit, not evidence that none
> exists.** The structural findings above are the durable output.

**The right next step is a Japanese-speaking 山林売買 broker, briefed on *shape* rather than
size:** fall-line-elongated, freehold to the ridge, with existing municipal-road frontage.
