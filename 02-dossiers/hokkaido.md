## ⚠️ My hypothesis was wrong, and it was wrong in an instructive way

I proposed that Hokkaido would be **better** than Honshū for private summit ownership, because the
**北海道国有未開地処分法 (1897)** granted land in fee to settlers who developed it.

**The aggregate data says the opposite, by roughly 2×.**

| Ownership of forest | **Hokkaido** | Japan |
|---|---:|---:|
| **国有林** national forest | **55 %** | 31 % |
| **私有林** private forest | **28 %** | 58 % |
| **Public total** | **72 %** | **43 %** |

`[P1 — 北海道データブック 2025; MAFF]` Hokkaido's own publication flags the 72 % vs 43 % gap
explicitly. Forest is 5.54 m ha = 71 % of the island.

And the decisive line comes from the Forestry Agency itself: **北海道森林管理局 manages ~3.04 m ha,
which "covers most of the backbone mountain ranges, including Daisetsuzan and Hidaka."** `[P1]`

> ### The mechanism I cited as the reason Hokkaido would be better is exactly why it is worse
>
> **The 1897 Act granted land free to settlers who *cultivated* it.** Cultivable lowland went to
> settlers in fee. **Uncultivable steep ground stayed with the state.** `[E]`
>
> Hokkaido's private 28 % is therefore **disproportionately the colonised lowland flat** — the
> land that could be ploughed. The mountains were never in the grant.

### Three confirmations

- **羊蹄山 (1,898 m) is a 特別保護地区** — the strictest zone in Shikotsu-Tōya National Park. `[P1 環境省]`
  **Categorically out.**
- **The entire Niseko 1,000 m range** — Annupuri, Iwaonupuri, Chisenupuri, Raiden — sits inside
  **ニセコ積丹小樽海岸国定公園.** `[P1 北海道庁]` The 2024 boundary revision de-designated 17 ha at
  developed Hirafu but **added 87 ha of intact terrain. The trend runs against you.**
- **札幌藻岩山, 2025 — the perfect test case.** Sapporo ran an open tender; 北海道スクエア won; and
  **171.63 ha remains owned by the state and the city and is leased to the operator, who owns only
  the buildings.** `[P2 — 日経BP + Sapporo tender document]`

> **That is the Murakami structure again, in Japan's fifth-largest city, decided in 2025.** Muroran's
> Danpara is worse still: the city is **not selling — it is re-investing**, redeveloping the site as
> municipal parkland with a new snow park in FY2027–29. `[P2 道新]`

### What does survive of the hypothesis: size and title quality, not altitude

28 % of 5.54 m ha is still **~1.55 m ha of private forest**, laid out on the **殖民区画 rectangular
survey grid** rather than by inherited village custom — **large, clean, single-owner blocks.** And
MLIT confirms Hokkaido is among the most cadastrally-surveyed regions of Japan (national 地籍調査
completion 53 %; "Hokkaido, Tōhoku and Kyūshū relatively advanced"). `[P1]`

> **That is a real and material due-diligence advantage — in area and in title. It is not an
> advantage in altitude.**

⚠️ **H3 at ¥336k/ha is *below* the band despite sitting beside Kiroro.** That is a red flag, not a

### And the closed ski areas are a trap

**34 Hokkaido ski areas are listed as closed** `[P3]` — but the national-forest mechanism explains
why they are not buyable. National forest is let by **貸付 / 使用許可 only**, and on termination
the lessee must **remove structures and revegetate (原状回復)**, then file a **返地届**. `[P1 林野庁]`

> **A closed ski area on 国有林 is a liability, not an asset.**

The one M&A listing — tranbi 3202, a profitable Hokkaido ski area plus lodge at **¥10–30 m** — is a
事業承継: a going concern, with land tenure unstated.

## ⚠️ 北海道水資源の保全に関する条例 — worse than previously flagged

| | Niigata | **Hokkaido** |
|---|---|---|
| Notification before | 30 days | **3 months** |
| Trigger point | Contract | **Contract — not completion** |

`[P1]` **Three times Niigata's notice period, and it sits ahead of signing.**

Filings go to the 振興局 — **except in 稚内市, 北斗市, 倶知安町, 厚真町, むかわ町, 上富良野町,
下川町 and 枝幸町**, where they go to the town hall.

> **Kutchan being on that list tells you how much designated water-source area sits inside the snow
> belt.**

### And Kyōgoku stacks a second ordinance on top

**京極町 has its own 京極町水資源保全条例 (2013).** `[P2]` Kyōgoku is home to **羊蹄のふきだし湧水**
— roughly **80,000 tonnes a day**, one of Japan's 名水百選 and a 北海道遺産.

> ⚠️ **H1 is a 93.7 ha purchase directly upslope of the aquifer that defines the town's identity.**
> **This is precisely the transaction both ordinances were drafted to catch.** `[E]`
>
> **Verify the designation against the 指定地番一覧 for 京極町字春日 before spending any money.**

This is the same mechanism — water — that killed the Tsunan sale in Niigata. It is now the single
most recurrent political obstacle in the Japanese file.

### Two more that would have been missed

- Ski-area construction involving **land-form change ≥ 1 ha** needs **both** a 林地開発許可 under
  the Forest Act **and** a separate permit under the **北海道自然環境等保全条例.** `[P1]`
  > **The no-lift, snowcat-only model is a genuine edge here: with zero grading you may clear both.**
  > Felling alone still needs a 伐採届.
- **From April 2026 the 森林の土地の所有者届出書 requires the acquirer's nationality.** `[P1]`

## ⚠️ Correction: Kiroro is not YTL — but the Malaysian precedent is bigger than I said

**Kiroro's chain is Yamaha → 三井不動産 (2007) → シェアグループ (2012) → parent Property Perfect,
Thailand.** `[P2 日経]` I was wrong to attribute it to YTL.

**The real precedent is larger:**

> **YTL bought Niseko Village for ¥6.0 bn in March 2010** from Citigroup Financial Products, **and
> holds 615 ha of 所有地 — "six times Tokyo Disney Resort" — developing 48 ha of it over 10–15
> years.** `[P2 — 東洋経済 / 日経BP]`
>
> **A Malaysian group has already executed a 615-hectare Hokkaido freehold assembly.** There is no
> nationality bar; Japan restricts nothing here.
>
> **The highest-value introduction available to the client is the counsel, the agents and the
> 司法書士 who did that assembly.** `[E]`

**Scale context** `[P1 北海道庁]`: cumulative foreign-owned Hokkaido forest is **3,830 ha across 317
owners** (31 Dec 2024); 2024 alone saw 49 deals over 295 ha, the second-highest year on record
`[P2 日経]`; and **Niseko plus Kutchan account for 969 ha — about 30 % of the Hokkaido total**
`[P2 coki]`.

> Average foreign holding is about **12 ha. H1 at 93.7 ha would be conspicuous.**

`[E]` Central tendency around **600 m** for the flagships; a true all-Hokkaido median including the
small hills is probably **250–400 m**.

> **Your 300 m target is a normal amount of Hokkaido mountain, not an exotic one.**

### The snow number that reframes the altitude question

> **倶知安 (Kutchan): annual snowfall normal ≈ 1,770 cm — the highest of any major JMA station in
> Japan — at an elevation of 176 m.** `[P1/P2]`

Record depth 312 cm (1970); 幌加内's 313 cm (2018) is the Hokkaido record; 京極 publishes a
最深降雪量 of 216 cm. `[P2]` *(未確認: the 30-year 最深積雪平年値 in cm — JMA normals pages were
blocked. Pull `block_no=47433` directly.)*

> **In Honshū you buy altitude in order to buy snow. In the Kutchan belt the snow arrives at the
> valley floor.** `[E]`
>
> **A 300–600 m parcel here outperforms a 1,200 m Honshū parcel — which materially relaxes the
> summit requirement, if the client will let it.**

**And under the farmstead model, he already has.**

## The realistic outcome

> **A large freehold flank running from the valley floor to a boundary somewhere on the slope, with
> the crest in state hands.**

If "summit included" were still absolute, the only remaining architecture would be **an isolated
sub-800 m wholly-private hill outside the 国有林 backbone, outside every park, not 保安林, and in a
snow belt** — the 赤井川 caldera rim, the 黒松内低地, or the low hills west of 京極 and 喜茂別.

**No such parcel was identified. Finding one is a GIS overlay job, not portal browsing.**
