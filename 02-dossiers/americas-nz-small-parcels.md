# Americas and New Zealand — small parcels

Re-sourced against the 40 ha / 300 m benchmark. MYR at USD 1 = 4.0885, NZD 1 = 2.4000,
CAD 1 ≈ 2.95, CLP 1 = 0.0044695.

---

## 1. Geometry, refined

Fitting 296 m of vertical inside 40 ha means a parcel of roughly **860 × 465 m**. A *squarish*
40 ha block dropping 300 m implies a **25° mean gradient — the top of 吉雪's own range.** `[E]`

> **Practical band: 60–150 ha** for 300 m of vertical at genuinely skiable gradients, **once
> run-out and a base bench are included.** `[E]`
>
> This refines rather than contradicts the geometric minimum: **20–45 ha is the theoretical
> floor for a perfectly shaped parcel; 60–150 ha is what real-world parcels need.**

### ⭐ St Mary's Glacier — the only asset found that clears both tests with the top in the title

**373 acres held as 81 individual parcels — 78 on-mountain plus 3 parking.** `[P2]`

> **Eighty-one parcels is the signature of consolidated patented mining claims** `[E]` — and it is
> **why a ~12,000 ft summit is privately titled at all.** This is the 1872 Mining Law mechanism
> identified earlier in this study, and Silverton's assembly method, found as a finished product
> rather than a project.

- Base ~10,000 ft → top ~12,000 ft = **~1,200 ft / 366 m of vertical, inside the deed**
- **An inholding**, described as *bordered by* Arapaho NF and James Peak Wilderness, **with no
  USFS permit referenced**
- 8821 Fall River Road, **45 miles west of Denver**
- Cleared runs, old lifts and buildings already in place
- **USD 7 m** — LIV Sotheby's, listed September 2023, still quoted in 2026

⚠️ **The serious negative: it sits on the south aspect of Kingston Peak — the worst possible
aspect for spring snow retention in the Northern Hemisphere.** `[P2/E]` That is a direct failure
against this study's own aspect rule, and it is not fixable.

### Powder Ridge — cheapest per hectare, and one gap decides it

**269.72 ha at USD 4,995,000 — MYR 75,717/ha, the cheapest in the Americas set.** Explicitly
conceived as a **private ski area**: 12 trails planned, **3 already cut and reported steep**,
42 miles from Park City at around 8,000 ft. `[P3]`

> ⚠️ **No base, summit or vertical figure is published anywhere reachable.** That single gap is
> all that separates it from being the leading candidate. **One plat and a USGS overlay settles
> it.**

### Cherry Peak — meets the spec exactly, at 4.5× the price

**174 deeded acres, 386 m of vertical — the closest analogue to 吉雪 found in the Americas on the
numbers.** But **220+ acres of skiable terrain against 174 deeded means roughly 46 acres of the
terrain is off the deed** `[E]`, and it is a **going concern with three chairlifts, snowmaking and
staff — the opposite of the low-OPEX brief.**

### Meadowlark — good numbers, unresolved title, probably gone

800+ ft of vertical, 230+ acres of terrain, two lifts, ~300 in/yr of snowfall, on **ploughed
US-16**. `[P3]` But **whether the 320 acres contains the ski terrain or only the lodge and lake
resort is unestablished** — and in the same state as Sleeping Giant and Antelope Butte,
**assume permit until a title report says otherwise.** `[E]` Best-and-final offers were due
1 March 2026 with a 1 June close.

### Mt Lyford — the direct answer to the question asked

**No larger block and no separate sale of the ski field is currently advertised.** What is
available is **village sections at NZD 150k–695k and one lifestyle block at NZD 1.5 m + GST.**
`[P3]` The family still owns and operates the field through 2026.

⚠️ **And note the gate that does not go away: any New Zealand purchase over 5 ha of non-urban
land is sensitive land requiring OIO consent — regardless of the farm-land point.** `[P1]`

## 4. ⚠️ Correction: Chile is not as cheap as this study reported

**The market near every Chilean ski centre subdivides to 0.5–4 ha** — Fundo Las Raíces 96 ha into
35 lots of 1–4 ha from UF 2,300; Corral de Caballos over 87 ha; Las Trancas parcels of 1.5–2 ha
off ruta N-555 via 16 km of dirt. `[P3]`

**No 20–300 ha parcel with confirmed 300 m of in-title vertical surfaced.**

The one large priced block — **Futaleufú, 251.5 ha at CLP 7,000,000/ha ≈ RM 7.87 m
(RM 31,287/ha)** — states that its highest part **adjoins *cordillera fiscal* Cerro Teta: the top
is state land.** `[P3]`

> ### The correction
>
> This study's headline of **RM 1,024/ha** for Chilean cordillera came from **very large,
> high, roadless blocks.** It does **not** describe what is actually on the market at 40–250 ha
> with road access.
>
> **Budget CLP 7–20 m/ha ≈ RM 31,000–89,000/ha for serviced precordillera** — an order of
> magnitude above the CLP 229k–600k/ha band. `[E]`
>
> Chile remains cheaper than the United States. **It is no longer 7× cheaper than New Zealand at
> the size the client actually wants.**

**Best sector identified:** **Campos de Ahumada / San Esteban**, near Ski Arpa, at ~1,550–1,600 m
with water rights, around **RM 88,671/ha.** `[P3]`

## 5. Argentina and Canada

**Argentina — deprioritise, now on firm ground.** `[P1]` Ley 26.737 caps a single foreign
titleholder at **1,000 ha**, imposes a 15 % provincial ceiling, and **prohibits foreign title in
the *Zonas de Seguridad de Frontera* — which covers the Neuquén / Río Negro ski corridor.** A
removal bill was before the Senate in August 2026 but **is not law.**

**Canada — fails on tenure, not nationality.** The foreign-buyer ban to 1 January 2027 **does not
apply to vacant land (since 27 March 2023) and does not apply outside CMAs and CAs** — **rural
recreational land is clear.** `[P2]` But **~94 % of British Columbia is Crown land**, and any
tourism use needs a tenure. Powder King's 900 acres sits on a **60-year Crown development
agreement** at CAD 6,495,000 ≈ RM 19.2 m. `[P2]`

## 6. Patented mining claims — good title, no scale

`[P2]` Patented claims **convey surface *and* minerals by warranty deed, with title insurance** —
which is a genuinely clean instrument.

⚠️ **But listings cap at ~20.65 acres, and no contiguous 40 ha+ package is on the market.**
Closest found: 45.58 acres plus ~20 contiguous below Antoine Butte, Montana; a 41-acre claim
surrounded by San Isabel NF; and a 40.8-acre Backcountry-zone parcel near Leadville. `[P3]`

**St Mary's Glacier is the exception that proves the rule: someone else already did the assembly,
across 81 parcels, and is now selling the finished article.**

## 7. Small-scale risks — the American specifics

| Risk | The specific rule |
|---|---|
| **Access** | Accept **only a recorded, appurtenant, insurable easement.** Colorado prescriptive rights need **18 years of *adverse* use** — and **permissive use creates nothing, however long it runs** `[P2]` |
| **Ploughing** | Counties routinely do not plough. **CRS 33-14-110's statutory fallback for an unmaintained road is literally a snowmobile** `[P1]`. Budget **USD 5–15k/season** `[E]` |
| **The land above** | **No USFS permit is needed for private, non-commercial ski touring on adjoining National Forest** `[P1]` — but **any tow, groomed run, snowcat road or paying guest requires a special use authorisation.** ⇒ **Underwrite on deeded vertical only, and treat adjoining NF touring as a free option, never as inventory** |
| **Minerals** | The mineral estate is **dominant**. BLM administers **57.2 m acres of federal minerals under private surface, 90 % of it in the West** `[P1]` |

### 🆕 And one zoning category that is a near-bespoke fit

**Lake County, Colorado's Backcountry Zone — 160-acre minimum — is expressly intended *"to
accommodate personal use by the property owner"*, and not commercial use.** `[P1]`

> **A zoning district written for precisely this mandate.** Worth searching hard within it — and
> worth checking whether comparable "backcountry" or "resource" zones exist in the Utah, Montana
> and Wyoming counties on the shortlist.
