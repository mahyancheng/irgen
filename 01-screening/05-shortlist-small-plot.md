## Tier 2 — the best physical fit anywhere, timing-gated

### 2a. シャルマン火打 Charmant Hiuchi, Niigata, Japan

**94 ha · 500 → 1,009 m · 509 m vertical · longest run 1,623 m · mean gradient ≈18.2°**

> **The closest physical match to 吉雪 found anywhere in the world.** Your benchmark is 19°
> average; this is 18.2°. Your benchmark is ~296 m of vertical; this is 509 m. Your benchmark is
> 40 ha; this is 94 ha.

**Land *and* buildings owned by Itoigawa City since April 1998**, with the terrain assembled by
**municipal land purchase (用地買収) in 1993–97** — the best available indication that it is held
in fee rather than leased from national forest. `[P2/P3]`

**⚠️ Gated on timing: the city is studying private transfer from FY2029.** Not priced, not on the
market.

### 2b. 糸魚川シーサイドバレー Seaside Valley, Niigata, Japan

**74.5 ha · 240 → 740 m · 500 m vertical · same city-owned land-and-buildings structure**

**⚠️ The most actionable timing anywhere in the study: procedures begin FY2026, transfer targeted
complete by FY2028, via 公募 (open solicitation).** The city absorbs ~¥80–91 m/yr in operating
cost — buyer leverage, and equally proof the hill does not pay for itself. `[P2]`

**⚠️ A base at 240 m is a genuine snow-reliability worry for the lower third**, and Japanese
warming squeezes from below.

## Tier 3 — proven asset class, jurisdiction-gated

### 3a. *Alpage* class, French Alps

**La Chapelle-d'Abondance, Haute-Savoie: 64 ha plus a 340 m² chalet, €700,000 — about €10,900/ha
= RM 49,766/ha, RM 3.19 m all in.** A **closed transaction**, not an asking price. `[P2]`

> **This is the cheapest proof in the study that the right asset class trades at the right size.**
> France has **no nationality bar**. ⚠️ Its gate is **SAFER pre-emption**, which can substitute
> itself for the buyer and revise the price — and a non-resident foreigner buying an *alpage* for
> private recreation is close to SAFER's archetype of what it exists to displace. ⚠️ Note also
> that the buyer here was the *département*: **public bodies compete in this market.**

### 3b. Failing small areas, Norway

**Bjorli (Lesja): 650 m of vertical and 11 runs with a base near 600 m — bankrupt in 2025 with
NOK 40 m of debt.** `[P2]`

**Latitude substitutes for altitude:** the reliable Scandinavian season sits at **500–900 m**,
where land is cheap and privately held. And **these areas are failing on lift electricity and
staff costs — the exact two costs this thesis designs out.**

Norway's *konsesjon* is a **land-use test applied to everyone alike, not a nationality bar.**

> **⚠️ Carried under your "depends on the property" instruction.** *Allemannsretten* gives
> statutory public access. On a genuinely remote hill with no trail access, that is theoretical;
> near a town it is not. **Judge per property — but never assume legal exclusivity in
> Scandinavia.** In Sweden, add *renskötselrätt*, which burdens the freehold and survives sale.

## Eliminated at this scale

**Nine US "ski areas for sale" are not land sales at all** — Sleeping Giant (structures only),
Antelope Butte (improvements), Spout Springs (leased NF), Soldier Mountain ("assets do not include
land"), Teton Pass (NF), Hesperus (lease), Cuchara (county-owned), Bear Paw (tribal land),
Hatley Pointe (not listed).

**Chile does not deliver at this size.** The market near every ski centre subdivides to
**0.5–4 ha**, and **no 20–300 ha parcel with a confirmed 300 m of in-title vertical surfaced.**
The one large priced block, Futaleufú at 251.5 ha, **states its highest part adjoins *cordillera
fiscal* — the top is state land.**

**Argentina is out** — Ley 26.737 caps a foreign titleholder at 1,000 ha and **prohibits foreign
title in the border security zone covering the entire ski corridor.**

**Austria, Switzerland, Slovakia and Poland are closed** to a Malaysian buyer. **Romania,
Bulgaria and Serbia are SPV-only**, which fails the mandate outright — through an SPV **the
company is on the title, not the buyer.**

## ⚠️ Two corrections this study owes you

**1. Chile is not as cheap as reported.** The headline **RM 1,024/ha** came from very large, high,
**roadless** blocks. At 40–250 ha **with road access**, the real band is **RM 31,000–89,000/ha.**
Chile remains cheaper than the US. **It is no longer 7× cheaper than New Zealand at the size you
want.**

**2. The small-plot revision over-claimed on Japan.** It said Japan's two fatal flaws "both
evaporate". Only one does. **The size flaw evaporates; the tenure-over-the-ridge flaw does not** —
a 40 ha parcel is just as likely to run into national forest at the top as a 500 ha one.
**Screening must start from the ownership map, not the ski geometry.**
