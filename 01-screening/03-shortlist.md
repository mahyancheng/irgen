# The shortlist

## The result, stated plainly

> **No property anywhere in this study was verified as passing all six gates.**
>
> One asset was confirmed to pass **Gate 1 (title includes the mountain)** — Mt Lyford — and it
> fails Gates 3, 4 and 6. The **strongest structural case, Chile at 36–39 °S, has zero verified
> properties**, because the research budget was exhausted before any Chilean sourcing ran.

That is the honest state of the search. What follows is therefore a ranked set of **target
positions**, not a set of executable deals — with the specific work needed to convert each into
one.

---

## Rank 1 — Chile, Ñuble / Biobío cordillera, ~36 °S

**The strongest structural fit found. Zero properties verified.**

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 — Tenure | ✅ **Pass in law.** Freehold to the summit, on the Argentine frontier, available to a Malaysian with no consent regime. DL 1939 art. 6 reaches only *fiscal* land; art. 7 is nationality-limited to Argentina, Bolivia and Peru |
| 2 — Acquirability | ✅ **Best in the study.** No screening, no residency, RUT obtainable without residency, remote purchase by power of attorney, 1–3 weeks to inscription |
| 3 — Snow | ✅ **Ranks first** on the combined snow × terrain × title score. Volcanic ash flanks give the **lowest `D_min` in the world (30–50 cm)**; materially wetter than 33 °S, which fails the 8-in-10 test structurally |
| 4 — Terrain | ✅ Private *fundos* reach 1,400–2,400 m with **600–1,200 m of vertical** |
| 5 — Carry | ✅ **Cheapest by 2–4×.** And Model 1 — grazing leased out, minimal staff, private skiing — is **actually permitted**, unlike New Zealand |
| 6 — Price | ❓ **Unknown. No prices obtained.** |

**Why it wins on structure:** it is the only jurisdiction where the client's *actual* operating
model is lawful without compromise. The SEIA — which regulates *"centros o canchas de esquí"* —
is anchored on *"obras de edificación y urbanización"*, so a private operation on existing
roads with no permanent build **arguably falls outside it entirely.** The environmental wall
sits between Phase 1 and Phase 2, exactly where the thesis wants it.

**The one question that could invert this:** does freehold-to-the-summit actually exist on the
market, or is the good terrain all held under CONAF / Bienes Nacionales concession over fiscal
land and National Reserves? **If the latter, Chile's excellent ownership law is moot.** This has
not been tested.

**Next actions:** open a Chilean sourcing workstream; pull a free *certificado de avalúo fiscal*
for a sample cordillera *rol* to settle holding cost; file a *consulta de pertinencia* with the
SEA describing the Phase 1 operation.

## Rank 2 — New Zealand, Mt Lyford Village & Alpine Resort

**The only asset confirmed to pass the title test — and it fails on price and altitude.**

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 — Tenure | ✅ **Pass.** 677 ha freehold. "One of the few freehold ski-fields in New Zealand"; "some of the highest altitude freehold land in New Zealand" |
| 2 — Acquirability | ⚠️ Requires OIO consent. **Viable only via the s 16 intention-to-reside pathway** — which avoids the benefit test and public-access conditions, at the price of emigration and NZ tax residence on worldwide income |
| 3 — Snow | ❌ **Fail.** ~1,460 m in a North Canterbury coastal range. Marginal now, worse later |
| 4 — Terrain | ❌ Tops out below the 1,516 m summit; below the 1,600 m screen |
| 5 — Carry | ⚠️ NZ$371k–1,280k/yr on a comparable holding, but partly offset by the ACC liability bar and by section sales |
| 6 — Price | ❌ **Fail as asked.** No published price; vendor aspiration "tens of millions" implies ~NZ$29,500/ha — **roughly 9× the NZ$2,500–4,000/ha band high country actually clears at.** The premium is for 149 consented subdivision sections and a going concern, not for land |

**Why it is still on the list:** it is the only confirmed freehold ski mountain in the client's
first-choice country, and the 149-section consent is a **built-in capital recycling mechanism** —
sell sections to fund mountain CAPEX. That is a real answer to the funding question.

**Bid discipline:** the land is worth the high-country band; the consent and the business are
worth what the section absorption rate says they are worth. **Anchor on ~NZ$8–12 m, not on the
vendor's number.**

**Status unknown since the November 2024 campaign. This is the first phone call to make.**

## Rank 3 — Japan, a defunct ski area on 民有地, Honshū Sea-of-Japan side

**The cheapest asset to hold in the study, and the least alpine.**

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 — Tenure | ⚠️ **Conditional.** Freehold is open to a Malaysian with no consent — but most ski areas *lease* their slopes, and the summits are 国有林. Must be verified parcel by parcel |
| 2 — Acquirability | ✅ **Open today** — no consent, no residency, no local partner. ⏳ But a foreign land-acquisition framework is due **summer 2026** and nationality declaration at registration from FY2026. **Grandfathering has real value: move deliberately, but move** |
| 3 — Snow | ✅ Excellent — but **warming squeezes from below.** Screen base ≥ 700 m, top ≥ 1,300 m. The spectacular 250–400 cm normals at 300–500 m describe **exactly the band that fails** |
| 4 — Terrain | ⚠️ 300–450 m of freehold vertical is the realistic ceiling — against a Japanese *median* commercial area of 300–700 m |
| 5 — Carry | ✅ **Best in the study.** 山林 fixed-asset tax runs thousands of yen per hectare; the ¥300,000 municipal exemption means many holdings pay **zero**; 保安林 is exempt outright. ❌ But **the ranching leg dies** — 農地法 art. 3 blocks farmland acquisition by a non-resident |
| 6 — Price | ✅ **Order-of-magnitude cheaper than anywhere else.** Ski businesses at ¥10 m (**RM 254,000**); 山林 at ¥336,000/ha (**RM 8,530/ha**) |

**Why a defunct area specifically:** cleared, stumped runs **collapse `D_min` from ~250 cm over
*sasa* bamboo to 50–80 cm** — the single largest ground-cover improvement available anywhere in
this study, already paid for by a previous owner — and the road and power are already in. Find
them via **GSI 地理院地図 1960s–80s aerial photography.**

**The live channel:** 糸魚川市 enters **公募 (open solicitation) for Seaside Valley in FY2026**,
and is spending ¥80–90 m/yr to keep two ski areas open. The precedent is 村上市ぶどうスキー場,
closed by the city in March 2025 and reopened by a private operator for the 2025/26 season.

## Rank 4 — United States, a deeded mountain ranch in MT / WY / ID / CO

**The only jurisdiction where the entire thesis works with legal certainty. Zero properties
verified, and the highest entry price.**

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 — Tenure | ✅ **Pass — and uniquely so.** Deeded alpine exists because of the Homestead Acts, the railroad land grants, and the **1872 General Mining Law**, under which a *patented* claim conveyed outright fee |
| 2 — Acquirability | ✅ **Malaysia is on no adversary list.** AFIDA is reporting only. ⚠️ Residual risk: Texas-style statutes delegate the list to the DNI assessment *and the governor* — prefer states naming countries in statute |
| 3 — Snow | ✅ 8.5/10, 130–170 days | 
| 4 — Terrain | ✅ 600–1,200 m achievable. ⚠️ **High avalanche burden** — continental depth hoar is the worst snowpack in the study |
| 5 — Carry | ✅ Agricultural/forest classification taxes on **earning capacity, not market value**. ⚠️ **Keep the cattle and timber running — they are the tax shield, not just income.** ❌ US ski liability insurance is a hard, thin market and a top-three OPEX line |
| 6 — Price | ❌ Highest in the study |

**Do not pursue the closed-ski-area listings.** Most US ski terrain is a **USFS special-use
permit**, not deed — a "65 acres, US$5.8 m" headline is a base area plus a permit, and permits
require Forest Service consent to transfer. **Bluebird Backcountry operated on leased terrain
and closed in 2023: there is no land asset to buy.** The right target is a **2,000–20,000 ha
deeded mountain ranch** where the alpine comes with the deed.
