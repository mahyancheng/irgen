*Revised after the second research phase. Supersedes the first-pass ranking.*

## The result

> **One property is now both priced and structurally compliant: the Colbún cordillera block in
> Chile, 7,860 ha at RM 8.05 m — roughly RM 1,024 per hectare.** It has not been title-checked,
> and its stated price carries an unresolved 2× contradiction. That is the state of the search.

The second phase changed three things materially. **Chile acquired real prices and a confirmed

### The pricing finding

**ODEPA's national *minimum* observed farmland price is 5.8 UF/ha. The Colbún cordillera block
prices at 5.9 UF/ha.** `[P1/P3]` These blocks transact at the floor because **Chilean farmland is
priced off irrigation water and soil class, and cordillera has neither — every attribute this
thesis wants is worth zero to every other bidder.**

**That is the arbitrage, and it is 30–150× below irrigated ground in the same region.**

The corollary must be stated: **resale liquidity is close to nil. This is a hold-forever asset.**

### The open question resolved — it splits, and the split favours this thesis

**Developed, lift-served Chilean mountains are mostly concessions**: Nevados de Chillán is a
30-year municipal concession; Corralco sits inside Reserva Nacional Malalcahuello; Osorno and
Antillanca are in national parks; Chapa Verde is Codelco land.

**But undeveloped alpine is ordinary private property**, and two cases prove it:

- **⭐ Nevados de Longaví (~36.2 °S) is private freehold.** The Gardeweg family states publicly
  that they *"do not want to cede the lands"* but are **open to a commercial agreement**. A ski
  centre is under study: **~1,000 m of vertical**, good snow. `[P2]`
- **Ski Arpa** — bought as mountain land in the 1960s, now South America's only cat-ski
  operation. **Freehold alpine, snowcats, no lifts: the client's exact model, already working.**

> **Lifts cluster on public land because that is where the public roads went. The no-lift model
> is precisely the one that does not need the concession estate.** `[E]`

## Rank 2 — New Zealand, but buy a different thing than planned

### ⭐ The legal finding that changes the New Zealand strategy

**Section 6(1) OIA 2005 defines farm land by *current use*, and expressly carves out forestry.**
LINZ's own worked example of rural land that is *not* farm land is *"9 hectares of rural land to
develop luxury tourist accommodation."* `[P1]`

Two consequences:

> **(a) "Ranch **or** forest" is a choice, not a pair.** Grazing makes land pastoral, hence farm
> land, hence subject to the hardest consent regime in the study. **Forestry does not.**
>
> **(b) The test bites at the transaction, not the plan.** Buying a working grazed station is
> buying farm land whatever you intend next. **The escape works only for a defunct ski area, a
> forestry block, or a de-stocked alpine title — so buy the resort title, not the station.**

This is a reading of the statutory words plus one LINZ example, **not precedent.** A tightly
scoped counsel opinion on it is **the highest-leverage legal spend available anywhere in this
study.**

### Mt Lyford — it did not sell, and the reason it was listed is the opening

**No sale found; the Simpson family still owns and operates it**, and the field opened for the
2026 season on 2 July 2026. `[P2]`

**The trigger was a death, not a valuation.** Co-founder **Hamish Simpson died on 18 July 2024,
aged 44**; the campaign launched four months later. **Two years on with no result, a direct
unbrokered approach to Doug Simpson is the most favourable posture a private buyer will find in
the New Zealand file.** `[E]`

**⚠️ Correction to the first pass:** base **1,249 m**, summit **1,750 m**, **501 m vertical**,
180 ha skiable, 6 surface lifts. `[P3]` **A 1,750 m summit clears the 1,600 m screen and 501 m
meets the vertical target** — the asset is better than previously recorded. It remains maritime
and marginal for snow, and **whether the 677 ha title contains that summit is unanswered.**

### What was eliminated

- **Inland Kaikōura fails.** The alpine core is Crown land; the Camden pastoral lease covers much
  of the ground below Tapuae-o-Uenuku. **None of Glazebrook, Upton Fells, Muzzle, Middlehurst or
  Bluff holds freehold above 1,600 m.**
- **Matangi eliminated** — 11,329 ha but **primarily Crown pastoral leasehold in perpetuity.**
- **Fox Peak downgraded** — the freehold premise is **unverified**, and in South Canterbury
  "private land" routinely means a privately held Crown pastoral lease.
- 🆕 **Porters / Crystal Valley** — DOC granted the freehold to Blackfish Ltd in 2011 in exchange
  for land elsewhere. A **documented Crown-to-private grant of alpine freehold.** Current
  ownership unestablished; **sanctions screening required** given reported Russian shareholding.
- **Puhi Peaks Station** proves freehold alpine title exists in New Zealand — 1,618 ha rising
  **400 m to 2,600 m** — but **810 ha sits under a QEII covenant on the title.** Not for sale.
  **Check every NZ alpine title for QEII covenants before anything else.**

## Rank 3 — United States: the arbitrage is proven, the supply is not there

> **In the Rockies the deed stops at the National Forest boundary, because that is where the
> homestead and patent process stopped in 1900. The flank is private; the summit is federal.**

**Two properties unambiguously carried alpine terrain in fee. Both are sold.** Cielo Vista
(83,368 deeded acres **including Culebra Peak, 14,047 ft**) at **USD 1,259/acre** in 2017 —
*cheaper per acre than USDA's mountain pastureland average of USD 946*. Tolland (8,000–13,000 ft)
**went to Colorado Parks & Wildlife and is now public land.**

**The arbitrage is confirmed by hard transaction evidence. The constraint is supply: this asset
surfaces once every 5–8 years, and state agencies and land trusts are the competing bidders.**

### The one live candidate

**⭐ Black Hawk Ranch** — Carbon County, Utah. **20,000 deeded acres, zero leased**, USD 35 m
(**RM 143.1 m**), **USD 1,750/acre**, **6,000 → 9,500+ ft = 1,067 m of vertical**, on the
**Wasatch Plateau — intermountain snowpack, rolling and naturally low-angle.** `[P3]`

**Verify its deeded boundary against elevation before anything else in the US.** It is the only
American property that could plausibly beat a Southern Hemisphere alternative.

**Watch for:** headline acreage that is mostly permit (Pathfinder was **10.8 % deeded** of
916,076 marketed acres); **severed mineral estates, which are the western default and are
dominant over the surface**; ski liability **up ~600 % since 2020**; and **no FAIR Plan in
Montana.** Colorado's **transferable, cash-sellable easement credit at 90 % of value, capped
USD 5 m**, is the one subsidy that works for a foreign owner — and partly offsets Colorado's poor
continental snowpack.

## Rank 4 — Japan: cheapest to hold, and it fails the landholding test

**Itoigawa resolved.** Seaside Valley enters transfer procedures in FY2026 targeting FY2028;
Charmant Hiuchi is FY2029 or later. **There is no prospectus yet** — only entry into the
procedures. The city publishes **two structural ¥39–46 m/year losses**, and states plainly that
Seaside Valley *cannot be made profitable without the subsidy.* **Price may be nominal or zero;
the liability is the deficit and the lift fleet.** `[P2]`

**Charmant Hiuchi is the better asset** — 500 → 1,009 m, **509 m vertical**, 94 ha. ⚠️ **But at
74.5 ha and 94 ha neither is a landholding.** They are operating businesses on parcels the size
of one medium 山林, and the Murakami precedent transferred **facilities, with no land mentioned.**

**Nothing above 363.6 ha was found across two research passes.** `[E]` **A single 500 ha private
Japanese title with real vertical looks close to non-existent on the open market.**

**The realistic Japanese structure is therefore freehold base + national-forest lease above** —
and the Forestry Agency **names スキー場跡地 as an established category of national-forest land
leased for non-forestry use.** `[P1]` **Whether a foreign entity may hold such a lease is
unanswered and politically live.**

**What Japan still wins on:** holding cost near zero, the **confirmed 無償 free-transfer template**
(Murakami → Sync First), and a disposal market that has **moved from private negotiation to
public portals** — which is what makes it enterable without a Japanese sponsor. **What it warns:**
at Tsunan, a properly run broker tender with a selected preferred bidder was **overturned by the
town assembly in March 2026.** Political risk sits *after* the commercial agreement.
