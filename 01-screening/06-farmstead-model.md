# Mandate revision 3: the farmstead model

**The reference is Mountain Memories, 留寿都村 (Rusutsu-mura), Abuta-gun, Hokkaido** — a hosted
ski-week lodge near Rusutsu Resort, easy reach of New Chitose Airport. `[P2/P3]` The client's
reading of it: **an abandoned farm or ranch, bought outright, with a mountain behind it used for
backcountry skiing.**

---

## What changes: everything, and mostly for the better

The mandate has moved through three shapes:

| Rev | Target | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Large alpine landholding — station, ranch, forest estate, with skiable terrain in the title | **Tenure over the summit** |
| 2 | Small plot, ~40 ha with 300 m of vertical, title includes the top | **Tenure over the summit** |
| **3** | **A farmstead with buildings. Own the base; tour the mountain behind — which you need not own.** | **地目 (land category) and access** |

> ### The single hardest constraint in this entire study just dissolved.
>
> Every dead end in fourteen months of this file traces to one requirement: *the registered title
> must include the peak.* That is what put New Zealand's alpine in Crown ownership, Japan's in
> 国有林, America's behind a Forest Service permit, and Europe's in *demanio civico*.
>
> **If the mountain is toured rather than owned, none of that matters.**

## What the client owns under this model

| Owned | Not owned |
|---|---|
| A farmhouse and outbuildings | The mountain |
| A few hectares to a few tens of hectares of 宅地 + 山林 + 原野 | The summit |
| **Road frontage and winter access** | The snowpack |
| The business, if one is run | — |

## The trade, stated honestly

**What is gained:**

- **The tenure problem disappears.** No summit to buy, no concession, no national forest, no
  council vote.
- **The price falls by one to two orders of magnitude.** A rural Hokkaido farmhouse with land is a
  ¥1–10 m proposition, not ¥100 m+.
- **The buildings already exist** — house, barn, sometimes a silo — which is most of the Phase 1
  CAPEX that was previously modelled from scratch.
- **The vendor is private**, not a municipality. No 行政財産 wall, no 用途指定, no 買戻特約, no
  employment covenant, no demolition liability. The Grandeco lesson, applied at a scale one person
  can transact.
- **It is proven.** Somebody is already running exactly this, in exactly this village.

**What is given up — and it should be said plainly:**

- **No exclusivity.** You cannot exclude anyone from the mountain, because it is not yours. In the
  Niseko–Rusutsu belt, the backcountry is busy.
- **No grooming, no lifts, no piste-cutting** on land you do not own. The operating model is
  genuinely limited to touring, and to whatever a snowcat can legally do on your own ground.
- **No control over what happens above you** — logging, closure, a change of landowner policy.
- **Snow reliability is whatever the mountain gives you.** No option to move uphill.

> **For a private ski mountain in the sense of "a mountain I own and control", this is not it. For
> "a base I own, from which I ski a mountain", it is exactly it — and it is the only version of
> this plan that a single individual can actually execute.** `[E]`

## The new binding constraint: 地目, not tenure

The obstacle moves from *who owns the summit* to **what the land is registered as.**

| 地目 | Treatment | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **田 / 畑** (farmland) | **農地法第3条** applies. The 全部効率利用要件 and 常時従事要件 (~150 days/yr of personal farm work) survived the 2023 abolition of the minimum-area rule, and are judged by a locally-composed 農業委員会. **A sale without permission is void** | ❌ **Effectively closed to a non-resident foreigner** |
| **宅地** (residential) | Outside 農地法 | ✅ |
| **山林 / 原野** (forest / moor) | Outside 農地法 | ✅ |
| **牧場** (pasture) | ⚠️ **Needs checking** — 採草放牧地 *is* caught by 農地法 art. 3, and the relationship between the 地目 "牧場" and that category must be confirmed | ⚠️ |

> **A "farm" is exactly the thing the client cannot buy if it is registered as 農地.** The target is
> therefore a **farmstead whose registered categories are 宅地 + 山林 + 原野**, with little or no
> 田/畑 — or one where the farmland can be dealt with by 農地転用 or a 非農地証明.
>
> **This is the first thing to check on any listing, before location, price or view.**

## What must now be re-examined

Several things this study rejected were rejected on a test that no longer binds:

| Previously rejected because | Status under the farmstead model |
|---|---|
| "The title does not include the peak" | **No longer disqualifying** |
| "Too small to be a landholding" | **No longer disqualifying** |
| "The summit is 国有林" | **No longer disqualifying, if touring on national forest is lawful** |
| "The ski terrain is a concession, not title" | **Irrelevant — no concession is needed to tour** |

**The open question that now decides the model:** *is it lawful to ski-tour on land you do not
own in Japan — national forest, private forest, and inside national parks?* If yes, this works. If
no, it does not. **That is under investigation and is the single most important question left in
the file.**

A second, quieter question: **車馬等乗入れ規制区域** — vehicle-entry restriction zones in parks —
carry up to a year's imprisonment or a ¥1 m fine, and would end any snowcat or snowmobile use.
Whether skis are caught, and where the zones lie, must be established.

## The area: Rusutsu and its cheaper neighbours

Mountain Memories sits in **留寿都村**, in the ring of farming villages around 羊蹄山 (1,898 m) and
下 尻別岳 (1,107 m), inside the Niseko–Rusutsu snow belt.

**The pricing logic to exploit:** Niseko and Kutchan land has repriced dramatically on foreign
demand. **The farming villages 20–40 km out — 喜茂別, 真狩, 京極, 蘭越, 黒松内, 赤井川 — sit in the
same snow belt at a fraction of the price.** `[E]` Quantifying that gap is a live research task.

**And one precedent worth weighing:** **YTL Corporation — a Malaysian company — owns Kiroro and
Niseko Village.** `[U — under verification]` If that holds, a Malaysian buying Hokkaido ski land is
not a novelty requiring anyone's indulgence. It is a well-trodden path.
