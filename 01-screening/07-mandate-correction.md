# Mandate correction: own the slope

**Status: this document supersedes `06-farmstead-model.md` and governs the search from here.**

The client corrected the brief twice in succession:

> *"Wait I didn't told you to just get a ranch with small mountains in it that can be converted to a
> 吉雪size ski?"*
>
> *"Nope I was hoping I own that land. Imagine a land like a ranch but with a slope or mountain that
> has snow and I can ski on it."*

Both corrections point at the same error, and it was this file's error, not the client's.

---

## The drift, stated plainly

Rev 3 concluded that "the single hardest constraint in this entire study just dissolved" because the
mountain would be toured rather than owned. That was a real finding about a real model — it is how
Mountain Memories operates — but **it was not the mandate.** The client never asked to tour someone
else's mountain. They asked to own a ranch with a hill on it.

The drift happened in one step and it is worth naming so it does not recur:

| What the client said | What this file heard | Why they differ |
|---|---|---|
| "a small mountain" | *an alpine summit* | A 小山 at 400–900 m is not a 3,000 m peak. The search was screening for the wrong landform. |
| "ranch with a slope I can ski on" | *a base to tour from* | Ownership was the point, not the base. |

The consequence is that every "no" recorded in this study was a no to the **wrong question**. The
study kept testing whether a private party can own an *alpine summit*. Under Japanese law, largely
no — 国有林 holds 31% of the forest estate and takes the high ground almost everywhere. But the
client asked about a **hill**, and hills are precisely what ended up in private fee simple.

## Why the corrected question has a much better answer

This is the structural reason the corrected mandate is more tractable, not less:

> **The 北海道国有未開地処分法 (1897) granted land to settlers on condition they cultivated it.**
> Ground that could be farmed passed into private fee. Ground too steep to farm stayed with the
> state. The dividing line ran at roughly the gradient where a plough stops working — which is
> *below* the gradient where skiing starts being interesting, but well above flat.

So the terrain the client wants — a cultivable or grazeable base with an uncultivable slope rising
off the back of the same holding — is exactly the terrain that a settler would have been granted as
one parcel and farmed the flat part of. **The hill came with the farm.** That is not a loophole; it
is how the land was distributed.

The corollary is equally important: **this is a lowland-margin search, not a mountain search.** The
target is the edge of a valley, the rim of a caldera, the flank of a low range — where a farm's
back boundary runs uphill. It is not the Hidaka spine and it is not Niseko.

## The corrected specification

| Parameter | Requirement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenure** | **Freehold (所有権), registered in the buyer's name.** Not a lease, not 地上権, not a concession, not a municipal 貸付. | The client stated this twice. It is the binding constraint again — but now against a landform that can actually satisfy it. |
| **The slope is inside the boundary** | The skiable fall-line, top to bottom, must sit within the registered parcel(s). | Owning the base and touring the top is Rev 3, and Rev 3 is withdrawn. |
| **Relief** | **250–400 m** from parcel low point to parcel high point. | 吉雪 benchmark: ~300 m vertical. |
| **Summit elevation** | **400–900 m.** | High enough for snow in a Hokkaido snow belt; low enough to be private land and outside park designations. |
| **Skiable area** | **~40 ha** of the holding at a workable gradient. Total holding may be larger. | 吉雪 benchmark. |
| **Gradient** | Average ~19°; nothing critical above 33°. | Below the dry-slab avalanche band; groomable by cat if it ever comes to that. |
| **Character** | **Ranch-like.** Open or semi-open land, buildings preferred, bought as cheap rural land. | The client's own framing, consistent across every revision. |
| **Snow** | Hokkaido snow belt. 60/80/1.0 rule (see `07-terrain-and-snow/01-framework.md`). | |
| **Park status** | **Outside** national and quasi-national park boundaries. | 自然公園法's 車馬等乗入れ規制 catches snowmobiles and snowcats at 1 yr / ¥1 m — **and it applies on private land inside the boundary.** Skis are not caught, but the client is buying the option to mechanise. |
| **農用地区域** | Avoid 青地. | Conversion applications inside 農用地区域 are refused essentially without exception. |
| **保安林** | **Acceptable, and a price advantage.** | Transfer is unrestricted; only felling and land-form change are controlled. A buyer who intends to ski an untouched slope is not constrained by either, and pays less for the land. |

## What is retained from the earlier work

Not everything was wasted. These findings survive the correction and still apply:

- **The 吉雪 geometry** (`04-small-plot-revision.md`) — the benchmark that defines "how big" is
  unchanged, and it is what makes a 300 m hill sufficient rather than a compromise.
- **The Japanese tenure and consent law** (`03-country-briefs/japan-regulatory.md`,
  `05-verification/03-japan-tenure-forms.md`) — 農地法, 地目, 森林法, 自然公園法 all bear directly
  on buying a hill inside a farm title. The 農地法第3条 常時従事要件 problem is *avoided*, not
  solved, by targeting parcels whose 地目 is 山林 / 原野 / 宅地 rather than 農地.
- **The liability finding** (長野地判平成13年2月1日) — an ungroomed natural slope is not a
  土地の工作物 and does not attract strict liability; a groomed piste is. This argues for the
  no-lift model on legal grounds independent of cost, and it applies with more force on land you own
  than on land you tour.
- **The snow framework and the evidence standard** — unchanged.

## What is withdrawn

- `06-farmstead-model.md` — the touring model. Withdrawn as the mandate. Retained on file as a
  **fallback** if the corrected search fails, and as an accurate description of how the reference
  property (Mountain Memories) actually operates.
- Any screening result that rejected a candidate for **not including an alpine summit**. That was
  the wrong test. Candidates rejected on that ground alone are re-openable.

## The method this implies

The corrected mandate is not a portal-browsing exercise, and it should not be run as one. No listing
describes itself as "ranch with 300 m of private vertical." The sequence is:

1. **Find the landform first** — named hills of 250–400 m relief, summit 400–900 m, in a snow belt,
   outside park boundaries, on the margin of farmed lowland.
2. **Establish the tenure overlay** — is the slope private forest or 国有林? The 1897 grant logic
   predicts private on the cultivable margins, state on the steep interior.
3. **Then find the owner** — via listings if any exist, and via direct approach if not. Approaching
   a rural landowner directly is a normal way to buy land in Japan, not an exotic one.

Steps 1 and 3 are running now as parallel workstreams. Step 2 is the decisive one and is where the
verification effort should concentrate.
