# The screening framework: six gates

A candidate is scored only after it passes the gates. Gates are **sequential and
eliminating** — the order reflects how expensive each failure is to discover late.

---

## Gate 1 — Tenure. *Is the mountain inside the title?*

**Test:** Does a registered freehold / fee-simple estate (or an equivalent perpetual,
transferable, developable interest) cover contiguous terrain from a road-accessible base to
a summit or ridge, with no third-party consent required to ski it?

**Pass:** Freehold to the ridge; or freehold plus a perpetual lease with development rights
in the same ownership chain.

**Conditional:** Freehold base with the upper mountain under a long, assignable, renewable
concession — recorded as *"controls but does not own"*, and disclosed as such.

**Fail:** Ski terrain wholly on public conservation land, national forest, Crown land or a
revocable permit.

> **Why this gate is first.** Worldwide, alpine terrain above the economic grazing line is
> usually public. In New Zealand the tenure-review process from 1998 to 2022 systematically
> moved the alpine tops into Crown conservation ownership while freeholding the valley
> country — the exact inverse of what this thesis needs. In Japan the high mountains are
> overwhelmingly 国有林 (national forest) inside 国立公園 (national park). In the United
> States most ski terrain is a US Forest Service special-use permit. A buyer who screens on
> price or snow first will spend months on assets that can never satisfy the mandate.

---

## Gate 2 — Acquirability. *Can this buyer actually buy it?*

**Test:** Can a non-resident Malaysian individual (or a vehicle they control) take title,
and at what regulatory cost, delay and probability of refusal?

Scored on four sub-factors:

| Sub-factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Legal capacity | Is foreign freehold permitted at all — outright, company-only, or barred? |
| Screening | Is consent required, on what test, and what is the realistic grant probability? |
| Timeline | Months from agreement to settlement, and does the seller tolerate it? |
| Reversal risk | Is the regime tightening? Could rules change between offer and completion? |

> **The asymmetry that defines this mandate.** Screening regimes reward *development*.
> New Zealand's farm land benefit test asks whether benefits are *substantially higher than
> the current state*. A buyer proposing minimal-intensity ranching plus private recreation is
> proposing, in the regulator's frame, **almost nothing** — while a buyer proposing a
> commercial resort scores well. The client's low-CAPEX thesis is therefore *hardest to
> consent precisely where the land is best*. Any candidate in a screened jurisdiction must be
> assessed on this inversion explicitly.

---

## Gate 3 — Snow. *Does it hold skiable snow, reliably, where the road already goes?*

**Test:** ≥ 60 skiable days in ≥ 8 years in 10, on terrain reachable from existing formed
access, with enough base depth to cover the natural ground surface.

Ground cover is the under-appreciated variable. Over smooth pasture, ~30–40 cm of settled
snow is skiable. Over tussock, matagouri, scrub, boulder field or windfall, the same terrain
needs closer to **1 m** before it is skiable at all — which pushes the effective season start
weeks later and makes early-season depth, not seasonal total, the binding constraint.

---

## Gate 4 — Terrain. *Is there a mountain, or just a hill?*

**Test:** ≥ 300 m of contiguous skiable vertical inside the title (≥ 500 m strongly
preferred), on slopes in the 15–30° band, on snow-holding aspects, with a base that a vehicle
can reach.

| Metric | Threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical | ≥ 300 m minimum; ≥ 500 m target | Below 300 m a snowcat lap is not worth the climb |
| Slope band | 15–30° for the bulk of the terrain | Skiable, groomable, cat-accessible, and **below the 30–45° avalanche band** |
| Aspect | S / SE dominant (Southern Hemisphere); N / NE (Northern) | Shaded aspects retain snow weeks longer |
| Treeline | Terrain above treeline, or open forest | Avoids clearing consent and native-forest law |
| Access | Formed vehicle track to the base at minimum | New mountain road formation is the single largest Phase 1 cost |

**Deliberate design choice: stay below 30°.** Restricting the operating terrain to the
15–30° band removes avalanche control — explosives licensing, a snow-safety programme,
Gazex capital, and the associated liability — from the cost structure entirely. It sacrifices
the steep terrain that expert skiers want, and that trade is the correct one for Phase 1.

---

## Gate 5 — Carry. *Does the ranch pay the rates?*

**Test:** Verifiable rural income covers ≥ 50 % of annual carrying cost (target: ≥ 100 %),
without new capital.

Carrying cost = property tax/rates + insurance + minimum labour + machinery + road
maintenance + pest and weed control + compliance and accounting.

Pest and weed control is routinely omitted from buyer models and is not small: in the New
Zealand high country, wilding conifer and rabbit control are recurring, legally-backed
obligations on the landowner.

---

## Gate 6 — Price. *Is the whole ten-year position affordable?*

**Test:** Purchase price + transaction costs + Phase 1 CAPEX + 10 × net annual carry, in MYR,
against budget.

The screen is deliberately **not** price per hectare alone. A cheap price per hectare on
20,000 ha of unskiable country is worse than a dearer price on 3,000 ha that includes the
mountain. The relevant denominators are:

- **MYR per hectare** — the rural-asset test (T1)
- **MYR per metre of owned skiable vertical** — the ski test (T2)
- **Net annual carry in MYR** — the holding test (T3, T5)

---

## Scoring

Candidates passing Gates 1–4 are scored 1–5 on each of nine weighted factors.

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---|
| Title includes alpine terrain (depth of pass, not binary) | 20 % |
| Acquirability by a Malaysian non-resident | 15 % |
| Snow reliability and trend under warming | 15 % |
| Skiable vertical and terrain quality owned | 15 % |
| Entry price against rural-asset value | 10 % |
| Rural income offset available | 10 % |
| Phase 1 CAPEX required | 5 % |
| Expansion and adjoining-land optionality | 5 % |
| Legal, environmental and political risk | 5 % |

A candidate that fails Gate 1 outright is not scored, regardless of merit elsewhere.
