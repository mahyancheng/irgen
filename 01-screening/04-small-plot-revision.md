# Revision: the small-plot mandate

**The large-landholding requirement is dropped.** The target is now a **small private ski
mountain**, benchmarked on **吉雪滑雪场 (Jixue Ski Resort, Jilin City)**.

## The benchmark, measured

| Metric | 吉雪滑雪场 |
|---|---|
| Area | **400,000 m² = 40 ha** |
| Runs | 13 (beginner / intermediate / advanced) |
| Longest run | **910 m**, average gradient **19°** |
| Gradient range | **6° – 33°** |
| **Implied vertical** | **~296 m** (910 m × sin 19°) |

`[P2 — Chinese tourism listings]`

Two observations before anything else. **The implied vertical, ~300 m, is exactly the minimum
this study's terrain gate already set.** And **the 6–33° range sits almost entirely inside the
15–30° band the framework recommended** — the benchmark is not merely acceptable, it is close to
the optimum this research had already independently derived.

---

## 1. The geometry: how much land does 300 m of vertical actually need?

Vertical is a function of **horizontal run × slope angle**, not of area. Area follows from the
shape you need.

`V = L × tan θ`, so `L = V ÷ tan θ`.

### Horizontal distance required

| Average slope | For **300 m** vertical | For **500 m** vertical |
|---|---:|---:|
| 15° | 1,120 m | 1,866 m |
| **19°** *(the benchmark)* | **871 m** | 1,452 m |
| 22° | 743 m | 1,238 m |
| 25° | 643 m | 1,072 m |
| 30° | 520 m | 866 m |

### Area required, at 400 m width (enough for several runs plus a base)

| Average slope | For **300 m** vertical | For **500 m** vertical |
|---|---:|---:|
| 15° | 44.8 ha | 74.6 ha |
| **19°** | **34.8 ha** | 58.1 ha |
| 22° | 29.7 ha | 49.5 ha |
| 25° | 25.7 ha | 42.9 ha |
| 30° | 20.8 ha | 34.6 ha |

> **20–45 hectares genuinely delivers 300 m of vertical.** The benchmark is well founded, and
> 500 m — a serious mountain — needs only **35–75 ha**. `[E]`

### ⚠️ But shape matters more than area, and this is the screening rule

A **square** 40 ha parcel is 632 m × 632 m. At 19° that yields **only ~218 m of vertical.** To
get 300 m from a square 40 ha block you need an average slope of **25.4°**.

An **elongated** 40 ha parcel — 871 m up the fall line by 459 m wide — yields the full **300 m at
19°.**

> **Same area. Different answer. 吉雪's 910 m run inside 40 ha tells you the parcel is
> elongated.**
>
> **The screening rule: measure the parcel's longest dimension *in the fall-line direction*, not
> its area.** A long, narrow parcel running up the slope is worth far more than a square one of
> the same size on the same hill. Ask for the survey plan, not the hectare figure.

## 2. What this changes — and it is more than a matter of degree

### Japan's two fatal flaws both evaporate

The previous phase demoted Japan on two grounds. **Both were artefacts of the large-landholding
requirement:**

| Previous objection | Status under the new mandate |
|---|---|
| *"A single 500 ha private title with real vertical looks close to non-existent."* Nothing above **363.6 ha** was found across two research passes | **Void.** 40–150 ha parcels are the *normal* transaction size in Japanese 山林. The Akaigawa block alone is 74.4 ha |
| *"農地法 art. 3 makes buying a ranch effectively impossible for a non-resident."* | **Void.** At 40 ha there is no ranch. Buy 地目 山林 or 原野, which sit outside 農地法 entirely |

And the assets rejected as "operating businesses, not landholdings" are now **comfortably above
the benchmark**:

| Asset | Area | Vertical | vs benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| **シャルマン火打 Charmant Hiuchi** | **94 ha** | **509 m** (500 → 1,009 m) | 2.4× area, 1.7× vertical |
| **シーサイドバレー Seaside Valley** | **74.5 ha** | **500 m** (240 → 740 m) | 1.9× area, 1.7× vertical |
| **赤井川村 Akaigawa 山林** | **74.4 ha** | Unmeasured | 1.9× area |

> **Japan goes from fourth to first on availability.** Whether the land itself is in scope at
> Itoigawa remains the open question — but the *size* objection is gone.

### Europe reopens

The rest-of-world screen killed Romania, Bulgaria, Bosnia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Albania
and Kosovo substantially on the ground that **"cadastre and restitution chaos means you cannot
assemble contiguous scale."** At 40 ha, one parcel or a handful is enough. **That objection
largely dies**, and Europe's advantages — roads, power, a continent of closing small ski areas,
and *alpage*/*malga*/*planina* holdings that are exactly 20–100 ha of open moderate-angle high
ground — become relevant for the first time.

### Closed micro ski areas become the leading asset class

Previously dismissed as too small, they now fit precisely — and they carry four things that are
expensive to create from scratch:

1. **The pistes are already cleared.** In Japan this alone collapses the required base depth from
   ~250 cm over *sasa* bamboo to **50–80 cm**.
2. **The access road exists**, and someone has already solved winter clearing.
3. **Power is already in.**
4. **The vertical is proven** — somebody already decided this hill was skiable.

## 3. The cost structure inverts

At 40 ha, land price becomes a **rounding error** against Phase 1 CAPEX.

| Jurisdiction | MYR/ha | **40 ha costs** |
|---|---:|---:|
| Chile — cordillera | 1,024 | **RM 41,000** |
| New Zealand — high country | 7,200 | RM 288,000 |
| Japan — remote interior 山林 | 8,534 | RM 341,000 |
| United States — deeded mountain | 17,700 | RM 708,000 |

Against Phase 1 CAPEX from `04-economics/01-capex.md`:

| Tier | MYR |
|---|---|
| **1 — Minimum viable** (snowmobiles, hut, safety kit) | **RM 1.49 – 3.98 m** |
| **2 — Snowcat-served** | **RM 2.55 – 7.03 m** |
| A single used snowcat with passenger cabin, landed | RM 444,000 – 1.13 m |

> ### **At 40 hectares, the snowcat costs more than the mountain.**
>
> In three of the four jurisdictions, one used piste-basher outprices the entire land purchase.
> The land is **1 % to 25 %** of Tier 1 CAPEX.

**The consequence is a different decision rule.** The whole price-per-hectare analysis in this
study was built to serve a large-landholding thesis. **At this scale, stop optimising for
$/ha.** Optimise instead, in strict order:

1. **Vertical inside the title**
2. **Legal, ploughed road access to the base**
3. **Snow reliability at the base elevation**
4. **Clean, surveyed title**

Then buy the best parcel that satisfies those and spend the money on the machine.

### Carrying cost largely dissolves

The finding that *"a cheap alpine station is not a low-cost one"* was driven by **area** —
pest and weed control, tracks, fencing and land management all scale with hectares. **Divide the
area by 200 and those lines collapse with it.** `[E]`

| Jurisdiction | Likely annual land tax on 40 ha |
|---|---|
| **Japan** (地目 山林) | **Plausibly zero** — the ¥300,000 municipal exemption |
| **Chile** | **Plausibly zero** — far below the CLP 24.2 m exempt *avalúo* |
| **New Zealand** | Small rural rates |
| **United States** | Meaningful **without** agricultural classification — and 40 ha is too small to farm into one |

> **What remains is insurance, machinery and access maintenance — none of which scale with
> area.** The carrying-cost problem that dominated the large-landholding analysis is
> substantially solved by shrinking the plot.

## 4. What is lost, stated honestly

**Two legs of the original thesis do not survive the change of scale**, and it is better to say
so than to pretend the plan is unchanged:

- **T1 — "buy the land primarily as a ranch / farm / forestry asset."** At 40 ha there is no
  meaningful ranch and no meaningful forestry block. **The asset is a ski hill, not a rural
  business with a ski hill on it.**
- **T5 — "ranching, forestry, hunting and accommodation offset holding costs."** At 40 ha there
  is very little income to be had. Accommodation remains possible; the rest largely does not.

**This is an acceptable trade, because the costs those legs existed to offset also disappear.**
The original thesis needed farm income because 8,000 hectares cost RM 890k–3.07 m a year to hold.
Forty hectares does not. **The plan has changed shape rather than weakened: from "a ranch that
carries a ski hill" to "a ski hill small enough not to need carrying."**

## 5. The new binding constraints

Shrinking the plot solves the cost problem and creates four new risks, all of which are about
**not controlling your surroundings.**

| # | Risk | Why it is now first-order |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Legal road access** | On 8,000 ha you own your access. **On 40 ha you very often do not.** The parcel may be landlocked, served by a private road, or reliant on a permissive rather than deeded right. In Japan this is 接道義務 and 囲繞地通行権; in the US the difference between a deeded and a prescriptive easement. **This becomes the single most common deal-killer at this scale** |
| **2** | **Winter road clearing** | Who ploughs, under what obligation, and at what cost? A public road that is not cleared in winter is not access to a ski hill |
| **3** | **The land above you** | **If the top of the parcel is not the top of the hill, your vertical is capped forever** — and if the land above is state forest or national park, it is capped permanently. Buy to a ridge or a summit, not to a contour partway up |
| **4** | **Neighbours and expansion** | No buffer. Someone may log above you or build below you. And thesis proposition **T6 — "expand gradually"** — now depends entirely on whether adjoining land can be bought later. **Check who owns the neighbouring parcels before offering, not after** |

A fifth, quieter one: **you get the aspect you buy.** On a large holding you can choose a
different face. On 40 ha you cannot. **Pole-facing (S–SE in the Southern Hemisphere, N–NE in the
Northern) is no longer a preference at this scale — it is a requirement.**

## 6. The revised gates

| Gate | Change |
|---|---|
| **1 — Tenure** | **Unchanged.** Still the binding screen. The title must contain the slope *and its top* |
| **2 — Acquirability** | **Unchanged** — but more jurisdictions are now affordable, so the comparison widens |
| **3 — Snow** | **Unchanged, and now harder to mitigate.** No option to relocate uphill within the holding |
| **4 — Terrain** | **Reframed: measure the fall-line length, not the area.** Target ≥ 300 m vertical; 20–45 ha suffices if the parcel is correctly shaped |
| **5 — Carry** | **Largely retired.** Replaced by: *is annual cost under ~RM 50,000 without any income?* |
| **6 — Price** | **Demoted.** Land is a minor line against CAPEX. Replaced by: **is there legal, ploughed, year-round road access to the base?** — which is now the real gate six |
