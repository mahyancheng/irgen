## 7. Is a defunct ski area, or a non-pastoral alpine title, "farm land" for OIA purposes?

**Provisional answer: very probably NOT — because the statutory test is a *current-use* test, and because forestry is expressly excluded. This is the strongest structuring result in the NZ file, and it should now be put to New Zealand counsel for a written opinion.**

### The statutory text
Overseas Investment Act 2005, **s 6(1)** `[P1 — legislation.govt.nz, quoted via search extract; the section itself could not be fetched]`:

> **"farm land** means land (other than residential (but not otherwise sensitive) land) used exclusively or principally for agricultural, horticultural, or pastoral purposes, or for the keeping of bees, poultry, or livestock (which, to avoid doubt, do not include forestry activities)."

Last amended 5 July 2021 by the Overseas Investment Amendment Act 2021.

### Two features decide the question
1. **It is a use test, not a zoning, capability or title test.** The words are *"used exclusively or principally"*. Land that is not being used for those purposes is not farm land, however rural, however large, whatever its district-plan zone, and whatever it was used for historically.
2. **Forestry is carved out on the face of the definition.** Land used principally for forestry activities is *not* farm land.

### LINZ guidance `[P1 — LINZ Overseas Investment Guidance, "Farm land" and "Land types and areas"]`
- *Agricultural purposes* = growing crops or raising stock, and harvesting/extracting primary products from those crops and animals **on a farm**; it does **not** extend past the farm gate to processing.
- *Horticultural purposes* = plants or trees in gardens, greenhouses, shade-houses, orchards, vineyards or hydroponically.
- *Pastoral purposes* = **the grazing of livestock**.
- LINZ's own worked example of rural land that is **NOT** farm land: **"Investment in 9 hectares of rural land to develop luxury tourist accommodation."**

That example is close to the client's fact pattern and is the most useful single line found in this session.

### What follows
- **The land is still sensitive land.** Non-urban land of **5 ha or more** is sensitive; consent is required regardless. Escaping "farm land" does not escape the Act.
- **It does escape the farm-land-specific machinery** — the **30 working days' open-market advertising** obligation, and (per the March 2026 reform already verified upstream) the farmland carve-out from the national interest test. That materially shortens and de-risks the pathway.
- `[E]` **The client's own framing is internally inconsistent and must be resolved before any offer.** "Hold cheaply as a **ranch**" means running stock — which makes the land *pastoral*, hence **farm land**, hence advertising plus the benefit test. "Hold as a **forest** asset" — permanent/carbon forest, no grazing — is **expressly outside** the definition. **The client cannot have both.** The forestry framing is the cheaper OIA path on the face of s 6 and should be the working assumption.
- `[E]` **But note the trap on acquisition.** The test bites on the use **at the time of the transaction**. Buying an operating station that is currently grazed is buying **farm land**, no matter that the buyer intends to de-stock it the following week. The escape route works for a **defunct ski area, a de-stocked alpine title, or a forestry block** — not for a working station bought with the stock on it. This is decisive for how the Mt Lyford and Fox Peak approaches should be structured, and it is an argument for buying the *resort* title rather than a *station*.

### What is NOT established — and must go to counsel
1. **No OIO decision or law-firm commentary directly testing a ski area, a defunct ski area, or a de-stocked alpine title was found.** The search budget ran out at exactly this point. The proposition above is a reading of the statutory words plus one LINZ example — it is **not** supported by decided precedent in this file.
2. **Timing of the test.** Whether LINZ assesses use at the date of the agreement, at settlement, or looks through to a recent history of farming, is unresolved.
3. **The "principally" test in mixed use.** A 677 ha or 8,000 ha block with a ski area, a lodge, subdivided sections *and* some grazing is a factual apportionment question. Where the balance tips is unknown.
4. **Interaction with the forestry regime.** Forestry has its own OIA pathways (special forestry test / benefit test); escaping "farm land" into "forestry" is not automatically the softer route and needs to be modelled.
5. One adjacent OIO decision surfaced but was **not examined**: **case 201010070 — Washland Holdings HB Trust** (appeared alongside the Porters/Blackfish material). May bear on ski-area land classification.

`[E]` **Recommendation:** commission a short written opinion from NZ counsel (Chapman Tripp / Bell Gully / Russell McVeagh all have dedicated OIA practices) on the single question: *"Is an alpine title containing a non-operating or club-operated ski area, not grazed at the time of acquisition, 'farm land' within s 6(1) of the Overseas Investment Act 2005?"* On the evidence above this is the highest-leverage legal spend available in the whole New Zealand study, and it can be scoped tightly enough to be inexpensive.
