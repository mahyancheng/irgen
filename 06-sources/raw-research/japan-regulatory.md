## 1. Can a foreign national / foreign company own land in Japan? (Position as at Aug 2026)

### 1.1 The baseline: yes, full freehold, no nationality test

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` Japan places **no general restriction on land acquisition by foreign
individuals or foreign corporations**. Freehold ownership (所有権 / *shoyūken*) is available on the
same terms as to Japanese nationals. There is no requirement to be resident, to hold a visa, to
have a Japanese partner, to incorporate locally, or to obtain any pre-approval. There is no
equivalent of Malaysia's state-consent regime, no minimum purchase price floor, and no Bumiputera-
style quota.

- Sources: 外国人の土地購入は禁止？日本の制度を解説 (Starts) — https://www.starts.co.jp/kaigai/kaisetu005_foreigner_260508/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 外国人の日本不動産取得に制限はある？ — https://www.pright-si.com/2025/07/07/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 外国人による土地の所有に関する質問主意書 (House of Representatives written question, with government answer) — https://www.shugiin.go.jp/internet/itdb_shitsumon.nsf/html/shitsumon/a213106.htm `[PRIMARY]`
- 我が国における外国人による土地取引の規制の系譜と今後の課題 (Minto Foundation urban research paper) — https://www.minto.or.jp/assets/pdf/urban/u81_01.pdf `[SECONDARY]`

**This is the single most favourable fact in the whole Japan thesis.** Compared with New Zealand
(Overseas Investment Act consent — effectively unobtainable for a foreign individual buying
"sensitive land"), Australia (FIRB), or Switzerland (Lex Koller), Japan is wide open.

### 1.2 外国人土地法 (Alien Land Act, Act No. 42 of 1925) — a dead letter

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` The **Alien Land Act 1925 (外国人土地法)** is still technically on the
statute book. Article 1 adopts **reciprocity (相互主義)**: it *contemplates* prohibiting or
restricting land acquisition by nationals of countries that restrict Japanese nationals, and
Article 4 contemplates restrictions around national-defence areas. Both mechanisms were to be
implemented by **Imperial Ordinance (勅令)**.

**No such ordinance was ever enacted, and none is in force today.** The Act is therefore
substantively inoperative — it has no present legal effect on a purchase.

- 外国人の日本国内の土地取得と土地法制度上の根本問題 (Prof. Jun Masuda, Chuo Univ. Law School — Japan Legal Information Institute) — https://www.lij.jp/html/jli/jli_2014/2014fall_p094.pdf `[SECONDARY, academic]`
- 【専門家が解説】外国人土地法「相互主義」で日本の土地を守る方法 — https://ogaware.jp/alien-land-law-reciprocity/ `[SECONDARY]`

**Practical reciprocity analysis for a Malaysian buyer** `[INFERENCE]`: If Japan ever *did* activate
the 1925 Act's reciprocity clause, Malaysia is a plausible target, because Malaysia restricts
foreign land acquisition heavily (state consent under the National Land Code, minimum price
thresholds, Malay reserve land, agricultural-land bans in several states). A crude reciprocity
test — "does country X let Japanese buy freehold on equal terms?" — would flag Malaysia. **However:**
- `[SECONDARY]` Existing, lawfully acquired rights would be protected; reciprocity would bite on
  *new* acquisitions only (this protective principle is noted in the reciprocity commentary above).
- `[INFERENCE]` A nationality-based ban would sit badly against Japan's WTO/GATS commitments, its
  investment treaties, and OECD Codes of Liberalisation obligations — and the 2026 expert panel
  (§2) is explicitly wrestling with exactly that constraint.
- **Takeaway:** reciprocity is a tail risk, not a current obstacle. But it argues strongly for
  **buying sooner rather than later**, and for the grandfathering value of an early completion.

## 3. 重要土地等調査法 (Important Land Survey and Regulation Act, in force 2022)

Full name: 重要施設周辺及び国境離島等における土地等の利用状況の調査及び利用の規制等に関する法律
(Act on the Investigation and Regulation of the Use of Land around Important Facilities and on
Remote Territorial Islands).

### 3.1 What it does and does not do

`[PRIMARY]` It **does not prohibit foreign ownership.** It gives the state a mechanism to survey
*how land is used* near sensitive facilities, and to order cessation of "function-impairing acts"
(機能阻害行為).

- 内閣府 重要土地等調査法 portal — https://www.cao.go.jp/tochi-chosa/ `[PRIMARY]`
- 区域の指定について (designated-area list, PDFs) — https://www.cao.go.jp/tochi-chosa/kuiki.html `[PRIMARY]`
- FAQ — https://www.cao.go.jp/tochi-chosa/faq.html `[PRIMARY]`

### 3.2 The two zone types

| Zone | Trigger | Obligation on a buyer |
|---|---|---|
| **注視区域 (Watch Zone)** | Within approx. **1,000 m** of an important facility (defence installations, Self-Defense Force / US Forces bases, Coast Guard facilities, designated critical infrastructure), or within a **border remote island (国境離島等)** | **No pre-transaction filing.** The state may survey ownership and use; may require reports; may order cessation of function-impairing use |
| **特別注視区域 (Special Watch Zone)** | A Watch Zone where the facility's function is *especially* important, easily impaired, and not substitutable | **Mandatory PRE-transaction notification to the national government** for any contract transferring ownership etc. of land/buildings **of 200 m² or more** (land) |

`[PRIMARY]` Designation is made after consulting the 土地等利用状況審議会 (Land Use Council).
Zones are published progressively as PDFs on the Cabinet Office site.

- 山口県 designation notice — https://www.pref.yamaguchi.lg.jp/soshiki/19/252941.html `[PRIMARY]`
- 熊本県 designation notice — https://www.pref.kumamoto.jp/soshiki/21/191083.html `[PRIMARY]`
- 相模原市 — https://www.city.sagamihara.kanagawa.jp/shisei/1026823/beigun_kichi/1026744/1030476.html `[PRIMARY]`
- 防府市 — https://www.city.hofu.yamaguchi.jp/soshiki/1/jyuuyoutochitouchuushikuiki.html `[PRIMARY]`

## 4. 森林法 (Forest Act) — the single most relevant statute for this thesis

Most large, cheap Japanese mountain land is **森林 (forest)**, and most of it is inside a
**地域森林計画対象民有林 (privately-owned forest subject to a Regional Forest Plan)**. Three
separate mechanisms bite.

### 4.1 Post-acquisition notification — 森林法 第10条の7の2 (Article 10-7-2)

`[PRIMARY]` **Confirmed and unambiguous:**

- Anyone who **newly becomes the owner** of land in a 地域森林計画対象民有林 must **notify the mayor
  of the municipality (市町村長)** where the land is located.
- **Deadline: within 90 days** of becoming the owner.
- **Applies regardless of area** — there is no de-minimis threshold.
- **Applies to individuals and corporations alike**, and to acquisition by sale, inheritance, gift,
  or corporate merger.
- On inheritance, the 90 days runs from the date of commencement of inheritance, and the filing is
  made as co-owned property by the statutory heirs even if the estate is undivided.
- Typical attachments: notification form, a map showing the location (位置図/公図), and evidence of
  the acquisition (contract, registry extract).

### 4.2 保安林 (Protection Forest) designation — the biggest hidden constraint on a ski project

`[PRIMARY]` A 保安林 is forest designated to secure a public-interest function. There are 17
statutory categories; the ones most likely to sit on ski terrain are:
- **水源涵養保安林** (water-source recharge) — by far the largest category
- **土砂流出防備保安林** (soil-runoff prevention)
- **土砂崩壊防備保安林** (landslide prevention)
- **なだれ防止保安林 (avalanche prevention)** and **落石防止保安林** `[INFERENCE]` — directly relevant:
  Japanese avalanche-prone slopes above settlements are frequently designated in this category

**Restrictions:**
- **Felling of standing timber (立木の伐採) is regulated** — must conform to the 指定施業要件
  (designated management requirements) attached to that 保安林; permission or notification to the
  **prefectural governor (都道府県知事)** depending on cutting method.
- **Change of the form or quality of the land (土地の形質の変更) requires the governor's permission.**
- `[INFERENCE]` **This is the decisive point for a ski project.** Cutting trails, cutting a
  lift line, grading a slope, building a road, or excavating for a base area all constitute
  土地の形質の変更 and/or 立木の伐採. Inside a 保安林 they require an affirmative permission whose
  criteria are protective, not developmental. **Assume trail-cutting inside a 保安林 is
  effectively unavailable to a private buyer** absent an exceptional public-interest case.

**Tax offset — genuinely significant:**
`[PRIMARY]` Land designated as 保安林 is **not subject to**:
- **固定資産税 (fixed asset tax)**
- **不動産取得税 (real property acquisition tax)**
- **特別土地保有税 (special land holding tax)**

`[SECONDARY]` And it attracts relief in **相続税/贈与税 valuation (commonly cited as a 30%–80%
deduction depending on the severity of the felling restriction)**, plus preferential access to
afforestation subsidies and public finance.

- 林野庁 保安林制度 — https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/tisan/tisan/con_2.html `[PRIMARY]`
- 徳島県 保安林 FAQ — https://www.pref.tokushima.lg.jp/FAQ/docs/00016995/ `[PRIMARY]`
- 保安林の相続税評価額 (Enman Souzoku tax corp.) — https://osd-souzoku.jp/hoanrin/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 保安林の相続税評価方法 (Chester tax) — https://chester-tax.com/academy/blog/hyouka/protected-forest-977 `[SECONDARY]`

`[INFERENCE]` **Strategic read — this is a real fork in the road:**
> 保安林 is close to a **perfect fit for a pure "hold cheap, ski it, don't build it" strategy**
> (zero fixed asset tax, zero acquisition tax) and close to a **deal-breaker for a
> "later expand into a commercial ski area" strategy** (grading and felling gated behind protective
> permissions). The client's stated plan is explicitly *option-value on commercial expansion*.
> **Therefore: establish 保安林 status parcel-by-parcel before offering.** A cheap mountain that is
> 100% 保安林 is a snowmobile-and-touring asset forever. If the plan requires the option to cut
> trails and install lifts, you need **non-保安林 forest** — and you will pay fixed asset tax on it.
> Note that a 保安林 can also be **de-designated (指定解除)**, but that is a discretionary
> prefectural/national act `⚠ criteria UNVERIFIED` and should not be underwritten.

### 4.3 林地開発許可 (Forest land development permission) — 森林法 第10条の2

`[PRIMARY]` For **地域森林計画対象民有林** (privately-owned forest under a Regional Forest Plan,
**excluding** 保安林 and 保安施設地区, which are governed by §4.2 instead):

- A person intending to carry out a **開発行為 (development act)** must obtain the **prefectural
  governor's permission**.
- 開発行為 = "quarrying of earth/rocks or tree roots, land clearing, or **other acts that change the
  form or quality of land**".
- **Threshold: development exceeding 1 hectare** (森林法施行令第2条の3). Roads are captured using a
  width criterion (3 m stated in one prefectural manual).
- **Ski areas are explicitly named** in the guidance alongside golf courses as a captured
  development type.

地域森林計画対象民有林 generally requires a 伐採及び伐採後の造林の届出 (notification of felling and
post-felling reforestation) to the municipality**, typically 90–30 days before felling. I did not
verify this in this session but it is standard practice and should be assumed.

> **Design implication that materially de-risks Phase 1:** keeping every intervention **below 1 ha**

> low-regulatory path; a cut piste is the high-regulatory path.

## 5. 農地法 (Agricultural Land Act) — why "buying a ranch" is the hardest part of this plan

### 5.1 The Article 3 permission

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` Transfer of ownership (or lease) of **農地 (farmland)** *or*
**採草放牧地 (land for grass-cutting or grazing — i.e. pasture/ranch land)** requires
**permission of the 農業委員会 (Agricultural Committee)** under **農地法第3条**. A sale without
permission is **void** — the transfer of ownership does not take effect and cannot be registered.

### 5.2 The 2023 liberalisation — helpful but widely misread

`[PRIMARY]` Effective **1 April 2023**, the **下限面積要件 (minimum area requirement)** in
農地法第3条第2項第5号 was **abolished**. Previously a buyer had to end up farming at least
**50 a (5,000 m²)** in most prefectures, or **2 ha in Hokkaido**. Small plots (e.g. 10 a) can now
be acquired with committee permission.

Sources:

- 小川町 農業委員会 下限面積の撤廃 — https://www.town.ogawa.saitama.jp/gyosei/sosiki/10/8/1/183.html `[PRIMARY]`
- 和歌山市 農地法第3条 — https://www.city.wakayama.wakayama.jp/kurashi/sangyo_koyo_roudou/nougyou/1003216.html `[PRIMARY]`

### 5.3 The requirements that survived — and that block this client

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` The abolition of the area floor did **not** remove the substantive tests.
All of the following must still be satisfied:

1. **全部効率利用要件** — the applicant must **efficiently cultivate/utilise ALL** the farmland
   they will hold (existing plus acquired), using their own labour and machinery.
2. **農作業常時従事要件** — the applicant (or a household member) must be **continuously engaged in
   farm work**. `[SECONDARY]` The operational benchmark is **150 days or more of farm work per
   year**, applied **irrespective of nationality**.
3. **地域調和要件** — the acquisition must not adversely affect surrounding agricultural land use
   (irrigation, pest control, communal water management).
4. For corporations: the **農地所有適格法人 (farmland-owning qualified corporation)** tests —
   business-scope, shareholding, and officer-engagement requirements. `⚠ specific thresholds
   UNVERIFIED this session`

### 5.4 Foreigners and farmland specifically

`[PRIMARY]` **農地法 contains no nationality-based rule.** MAFF's position, as reflected in a
municipal Q&A response, is that Article 3 judges the applicant on **whether they will actually and
efficiently farm the land**, and "there is no provision in the Act distinguishing acquisition by
nationality or country of origin."

### 5.5 THE PRACTICAL BARRIER — stated plainly

`[INFERENCE]` **A non-resident Malaysian individual cannot realistically acquire 農地 or 採草放牧地
in Japan.** The blocking test is not nationality — it is the **150-days-a-year personal farm-work
requirement combined with the efficient-utilisation test**. A non-resident owner who visits for ski
season and hires a contractor to run cattle will fail. The Agricultural Committee is a **local
body composed of local farmers**, exercising real discretion, meeting monthly, and it will refuse.

**This is the hardest "no" in the entire Japan analysis, and it directly attacks the "ranch"
component of the strategy.**

**Workarounds, ranked** `[INFERENCE]` `⚠ each requires local legal confirmation`:

| Option | Viability |
|---|---|
| **A. Buy only 山林/原野 (forest / wild land), not 農地** | **Best.** Most Japanese mountain and ex-ski-area land is registered as 山林 or 原野, **not** 農地 — so 農地法 never engages. Grazing on 山林 is a land-use question, not an 農地法 question. **Make "no 農地 in the parcel schedule" a screening criterion.** |
| **B. 牧野 (grazing land under 牧野法) rather than 採草放牧地** | Possible but obscure; treat as `⚠ UNVERIFIED` — I could not research 牧野法 this session |
| **C. Lease the farmland rather than buy it** | Lease of 農地 **also** requires Art. 3 permission — no escape, though the 農地中間管理機構 route has different criteria `⚠ UNVERIFIED` |
| **D. Set up a 農地所有適格法人** | Requires majority agricultural revenue, and officers who actually farm — not compatible with a passive foreign investor |
| **E. Japanese-resident partner or manager acquires and farms** | Works legally but creates counterparty and control risk; the permission attaches to *that person's* farming |
| **F. 農地転用 (conversion) under 農地法第4条/第5条** | **Art. 4** = owner converts own farmland to non-agricultural use; **Art. 5** = acquisition *for* conversion. Both need permission (農業委員会 → 都道府県知事 for larger areas). In practice conversion of good farmland is heavily restricted; conversion of marginal upland farmland is more attainable. **Art. 5 is the correct route if the client wants to buy pasture and convert it to ski/recreation use** — but it is a discretionary permission and must be secured *before* completion `⚠ criteria UNVERIFIED` |

> **Bottom line on ranching:** the "buy a ranch, offset costs with livestock" leg of the strategy is
> **the weakest part of the Japan case**. Japan is a good jurisdiction for buying a *mountain*; it is
> a poor jurisdiction for a foreign non-resident buying a *ranch*. Recommend restructuring the
> Japanese version of this thesis around **forest + hunting + lodging**, and dropping livestock, or
> confining livestock to non-農地 山林/原野 grazing.

## 6. 自然公園法 (Natural Parks Act) — where the high terrain actually is

### 6.1 Park types and zoning

`[PRIMARY]` Three park classes: **国立公園 (National Park)** — managed by the Minister of the
Environment; **国定公園 (Quasi-National Park)** — designated by the Minister, managed by the
prefecture; **都道府県立自然公園 (Prefectural Natural Park)**.

Each is divided into:

| Zone (地種区分) | Character | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| **特別保護地区** (Special Protection Zone) | Strictest. Even collecting fallen leaves or stones is prohibited | Permission for almost everything; effectively no development |
| **第1種特別地域** | Landscape equivalent to Special Protection Zone; present scenery to be preserved as far as possible | Permission; development essentially refused |
| **第2種特別地域** | Neither 1st nor 3rd class; **agriculture/forestry activity to be coordinated as far as possible** | Permission |
| **第3種特別地域** | Lowest need to preserve scenic beauty within Special Areas; **normal agriculture/forestry in principle unlikely to harm scenery** | Permission, most permissive of the Special Areas |
| **普通地域** (Ordinary Zone) | Buffer | **No permission required** — but **prior notification (届出)** for larger-scale acts |
| 海域公園地区 | Marine | n/a here |

`[PRIMARY]` In **特別地域 (excluding 特別保護地区)**, the **new construction, reconstruction or
extension of a 工作物 (structure — expressly including buildings and vehicle roads) requires
permission**. Permitting authority: **Minister of the Environment** for National Parks,
**prefectural governor** for Quasi-National and Prefectural parks.

`[PRIMARY]` In **普通地域**, permission is not required, but **prior notification is required for
acts above a certain scale**.

### 6.3 "Most of Japan's high ground is National Park or national forest" — status of this claim

`[INFERENCE]` `⚠ PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED` I could **not** retrieve the total natural-park share of
Japan's land area (search quota exhausted). My prior understanding — **natural parks (national +
quasi-national + prefectural) cover roughly 14–15% of Japan's land area, with National Parks alone
around 5.8%** — is **UNVERIFIED and must not be quoted to the client as fact**.

What I *can* state with confirmed sources (§7) is the national-forest side, and it is decisive.

**Where privately-owned high ground can exist** `[INFERENCE]`:
1. **Below the national-forest belt.** National forest is concentrated in *steep, deep, remote
   interior mountains and river headwaters*; private forest dominates the accessible mid-elevation
   zone. Privately-owned terrain therefore tends to be **mid-mountain, road-accessible, and
   lower-angle** — which is exactly what a snowcat operation wants and *not* what an alpine
   summit-and-couloir programme wants.
2. **第2種/第3種特別地域 and 普通地域 within parks**, where private ownership plus permitted use
   coexist.
3. **Outside parks entirely** — large tracts in Hokkaido (especially the Hidaka/Tokachi periphery,
   Kitami, Shiretoko fringe), northern Tohoku, and the Niigata/Nagano borderlands.
4. **Defunct ski areas (廃止スキー場)** — these are the highest-value targets precisely because they
   are *already* private, *already* cleared, *already* road-served, and *already* have a
   development history that establishes the land is not 保安林-locked. `⚠ I ran no searches on the
   defunct-ski-area market this session — flagging as a gap for the property workstream.`

> **The honest structural conclusion:** Japan will readily sell a foreigner a mountain. It is much
> less likely to sell them a mountain with a **genuine alpine summit and steep skiable fall-line**,
> because that terrain is disproportionately **national forest** and/or **Special Protection /
> Class 1 Special Area**. The realistic Japanese acquisition is a **privately-held mid-elevation
> forested massif or a defunct ski area**, not a private 3,000 m peak.

## 8. 索道 (aerial ropeways / ski lifts) — regulation, and what disappears if you don't build one

### 8.1 The statutory frame

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` Since **1987**, passenger ropeways are regulated under the
**鉄道事業法 (Railway Business Act, Act No. 92 of 4 Dec 1986)**, which replaced the former
索道規則 (Ropeway Regulations). The Act covers both railway business and **索道事業 (cable transport
business)**.

**Definition:** 索道事業 = **"a business that transports passengers or freight by ropeway
*in response to the demand of others* (他人の需要に応じ)."**

**Permission:** a person intending to operate a 索道事業 must obtain the **permission (許可) of the
Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism for each ropeway**, subject to exceptions
prescribed by ministerial ordinance (e.g. freight-only, government-operated).

### 8.3 専用索道 (private / own-use ropeway) — the key question, only partly answered

`[PRIMARY]` **Confirmed definition:** **専用索道 = a ropeway installed exclusively for one's own use
(自己の用に供するため設置する索道).**

`[INFERENCE]` **The logic that follows** — and I want to be explicit that this is *my reading*, not
a verified statement of the regulator's position:

> 索道事業 is defined by transporting **"in response to the demand of others."** A ropeway that
> carries only the owner, their family and their non-paying guests is not answering the demand of
> others in the commercial sense, and so should fall **outside 索道事業** and therefore outside the
> Article 3 business permission.

## 10. Lodging and hospitality

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` Three legal routes to paid accommodation:

| Route | Statute | Operating days | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **簡易宿所営業** (simple lodging) | **旅館業法 (Hotel Business Act)** — permission (許可) from the prefectural public health authority | **365 days** | Full business licence. Higher fire-safety and building standards. Materially better economics |

## 13. Water rights (水利権)

`[PRIMARY]` Framework under the **河川法 (River Act)**:
- **許可水利権**: taking water from a Class 1 or Class 2 river requires a **水利使用許可 (water use

  recognition of its legitimacy.
- Permits specify a **permit period, permitted intake volume, and reporting method** for intake.

造雪用取水. `[INFERENCE]` Snowmaking is a **new, consumptive, seasonal, high-peak-rate** use in
exactly the winter low-flow period, competing with existing agricultural 慣行水利権 holders. Expect
it to be **difficult and slow to permit**, and expect existing downstream irrigation rights to have
priority.

`[INFERENCE]` **Groundwater (地下水)** is, under the Japanese civil-law default, an incident of land
ownership and freely usable by the landowner — but this is **heavily overlaid by prefectural and
municipal ordinances** on pumping, and by the 工業用水法 / ビル用水法 in designated subsidence areas.
`⚠ UNVERIFIED.`

> **Practical conclusion:** snowmaking is a Phase 2 problem, and a hard one. **Phase 1 (natural snow
> only, snowcat access) requires no water permit at all** — another point in favour of the low-CAPEX
> design. If snowmaking ever matters, **buy a property with an existing water right attached**, and
> treat that right as a priced asset.

## 14. Avalanche, safety and liability

`[SECONDARY]` **Japanese courts do impose a duty on ski area operators.** The established position:
> An operator has a **duty to secure skiers' safety** — including by **closing or barring dangerous
> locations** (滑走禁止措置 / 進入禁止措置). Failure to do so, causing an accident, gives rise to
> **damages liability**.

Precedent illustration `[SECONDARY]`: where a skier fell from a bridge on a course whose running
surface was poorly maintained and prone to catching skis, and the operator had not provided adequate
equipment/protection, the **ski area was found partly negligent**. Separately, where a participant in
a ski school ran off the piste, the **organising company's safety-consideration duty (安全配慮義務)**
was in issue.

Sources:
- 賢誠総合法律事務所 スキー事故（スキー場の責任について） — https://kensei-law.jp/sports_wisdom_p/ `[SECONDARY]`
- ベリーベスト法律事務所 札幌 — https://sapporo.vbest.jp/columns/general_civil/g_lp_indi/1350/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 長瀬総合法律事務所 スキー・スノーボード事故の法律問題 — https://songai-nagasesogo.com/column/445/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 弁護士 佐藤敬治 スキー事故の損害賠償請求 その2〜保険のお話〜 — https://sato-keiji-law.com/column/259/ `[SECONDARY]`
- アトム法律事務所 — https://atomfirm.com/jiko/45979 `[SECONDARY]`

`[INFERENCE]` **The liability structure is materially different for a private mountain, and mostly
in the client's favour:**
- The duty above attaches to an **operator holding out terrain as a managed ski area**. A private
  landowner who does **not** open a marked, patrolled piste is in a much weaker duty position —
  closer to occupier's liability than to ski-area operator liability.
- **Conversely, the moment you charge guests, market "our ski mountain", groom a run and call it a
  run, you are arguably holding out a managed ski area** and attracting the operator duty — while
  having none of a real resort's patrol, avalanche control, or rescue capability.
- **This is the sharpest tension in the whole business model.** The "private mountain that takes
  paying guests" sits in the worst position: commercial revenue, resort-like expectations,
  no resort-grade safety infrastructure.

`⚠ NOT ESTABLISHED:` **insurance pricing.** No 施設賠償責任保険 (facility liability insurance)
benchmarks were obtainable. `[INFERENCE]` Guided backcountry and snowcat skiing is a **hard-to-place
risk** in Japan; expect a specialist broker, meaningful premiums, strict conditions
(qualified guides, avalanche training, terrain limits, waivers), and possible exclusions for
avalanche. **Get an indicative quote before committing** — an uninsurable operation is not a
business.

`⚠ NOT ESTABLISHED:` **土砂災害警戒区域 / 土砂災害特別警戒区域 (sediment disaster warning and special
warning zones) under the 土砂災害防止法**, **急傾斜地崩壊危険区域**, and **雪崩危険箇所 (avalanche
hazard locations)**. My final two searches on these were refused by the quota. `[INFERENCE]` These
designations are published by prefectures on hazard-map portals, are freely checkable per parcel,
and — critically — **土砂災害特別警戒区域 (the "red zone") carries building restrictions and
structural requirements**. **Add all three to the per-property screening checklist**; they bear
directly on where a lodge can be sited and on avalanche exposure.

## 15. Practical mechanics for a non-resident foreign buyer

`[PRIMARY]` `[SECONDARY]` **Japanese conveyancing normally relies on 印鑑証明書 (seal certificate)
and 住民票 (residence certificate), which a non-resident foreigner does not have.** The system has a
well-established substitution:

| Japanese requirement | Non-resident substitute |
|---|---|
| **印鑑証明書** (registered seal certificate) | **宣誓供述書 (affidavit)** with the signature notarised — by a **notary public in the buyer's country of residence**, or at a **Japanese embassy/consulate**. For Japanese nationals abroad, a **署名証明書 (signature certificate / サイン証明)** from the Japanese embassy |
| **住民票** (residence certificate / proof of address) | A document issued by the buyer's **home or resident-country government (or consulate)** equivalent to a 住民票; **failing that, a notarised affidavit of address plus a copy of the passport** |
| **戸籍謄本, 会社謄本** (family/corporate registry) | Likewise substitutable by affidavit |

**Confirmed:** an affidavit whose signature is authenticated by a local notary or the Japanese
embassy **can be used for the registration application** in place of the Japanese documents.

Sources:
- 法務省 外国居住の外国人や外国法人が所有権の登記名義人となる登記の申請をする場合の住所証明情報について — https://www.moj.go.jp/MINJI/minji05_00574.html `[PRIMARY]` ← **the authoritative page; read this in full**
- 公益財団法人不動産流通推進センター 外国人の不動産の取得売却に係る住民票・印鑑証明書の代替書類 — https://www.retpc.jp/archives/16781/ `[PRIMARY-ish, public interest foundation]`
- 外国人の不動産登記に必要な宣誓供述書とは — https://s-legalestate.com/kaigai/registration/for-foreigners-and-overseas-residents/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 外国人の不動産登記 — https://s-legalestate.com/kaigai/registration/real-estate-registration/ `[SECONDARY]`
- 外国人・海外居住者の不動産登記とは？基礎から2024年4月改正の登記事項まで — https://s-legalestate.com/non-resident-property-registration `[SECONDARY]`
- 司法書士法人さくら事務所 — https://sakura-jimusyo.com/column/c-touki/5903/ `[SECONDARY]`

`[INFERENCE]` **A 司法書士 (judicial scrivener) is effectively mandatory.** They handle the
registration, the document substitutions, and the escrow-equivalent simultaneous exchange. Budget
for one who has done non-resident work before — most have not. There are Japanese firms that
specialise in exactly this (several of the sources above are such firms).

`⚠ NOT RESEARCHED — three material gaps:`
1. **Japanese bank account for a non-resident.** `[INFERENCE]` Historically difficult to impossible
   without residency. Most non-resident purchases settle by **direct international remittance to
   the seller / 司法書士 trust account**. **Confirm the settlement mechanism early — this trips up
   deals.**
2. **Remittance and FX.** `[INFERENCE]` Japan is open; inbound purchase remittances may attract a
   **post-facto report to the Bank of Japan / Ministry of Finance under the 外為法 (Foreign Exchange
   and Foreign Trade Act)** — a filing, not a consent. `⚠ UNVERIFIED.`
3. **Mortgage finance.** `[INFERENCE]` **Assume none is available.** Japanese banks do not lend to
   non-resident foreign individuals against rural mountain land with no income and no comparable
   sales. **Model this as an all-cash acquisition.** This is not a serious constraint given the
   deliberately low ticket size, but it must be stated.

## 16. Synthesis — the regulatory shape of the deal

### 16.1 What Japan makes EASY

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Freehold to a foreigner** | No consent, no screening, no residency, no nationality test — genuinely open `[PRIMARY]` |
| ✅ **Holding cost** | 固定資産税 on forest is **thousands of yen per hectare per year**; often below the **¥300,000 exemption**; **zero on 保安林** `[PRIMARY]/[SECONDARY]` |
| ✅ **Phase 1 operations** | Snowcat / snowmobile / touring on your own land needs **no ropeway licence, no forest development permission, no water permit, no park structure permission** `[INFERENCE]` |
| ✅ **Lodging** | 旅館業法 簡易宿所 gives 365-day operation; 農家民宿 certification relaxes building and fire requirements `[SECONDARY]` |
| ✅ **Guiding** | Guide services **alone** (no transport, no accommodation arranging) fall **outside** 旅行業法 registration `[SECONDARY]` — https://www.jata-net.or.jp/about/page-68252/mog_jjatacomi/homu1204/ ; https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/seisaku_seido/ryokogyoho/ryokogyohogaiyo.html `[PRIMARY]` |
| ✅ **Filings are filings, not consents** | 森林法 90-day owner notification; 国土利用計画法 post-transaction notification (**within 2 weeks**, for **>10,000 m² outside city planning areas**) — https://www.mlit.go.jp/tochi_fudousan_kensetsugyo/const/content/001707658.pdf `[PRIMARY]` |

### 16.2 What Japan makes HARD

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ **The terrain itself** | **20% of Japan's land / 30% of its forest is national forest**, concentrated by design in the **steep remote interior and headwaters** `[PRIMARY]`. The alpine terrain is largely not for sale |
| ❌ **Ranching** | 農地法第3条's **efficient-utilisation + ~150-days-a-year personal farm work** tests effectively **exclude a non-resident buyer** from 農地 and 採草放牧地 `[PRIMARY]/[SECONDARY]/[INFERENCE]` |
| ❌ **保安林** | Felling and land-form change gated behind protective permissions — **kills trail-cutting**, even though it zeroes the tax `[PRIMARY]` |
| ❌ **National park zoning** | **70% preserved-green requirement** for ski projects in National Parks; 工作物 permission in all Special Areas `[PRIMARY]` |
| ❌ **Any lift** | A ropeway plausibly triggers the full 鉄道事業法 regime — ministerial permission, 索道技術管理者, safety supervisor, annual inspection and reporting. **A step-change, not an increment** `[PRIMARY]/[INFERENCE]` |
| ❌ **Inheritance tax** | Japanese-situs assets of a non-resident decedent are in the Japanese net, up to **55%** `[INFERENCE] ⚠ UNVERIFIED — advise immediately` |
| ⚠ **Timing** | The **summer-2026 骨格** from the Cabinet Secretariat panel is live right now `[PRIMARY]` |

### 16.3 Recommended screening criteria to hand to the property workstream

`[INFERENCE]` Every candidate must be tested against:
1. **登記地目** — is any parcel 農地 or 採草放牧地? If yes, **flag as likely blocked** (§5).
2. **保安林 status and category** — obtain the 保安林台帳 extract. Determines both tax and whether
   any development is ever possible (§4.2).
3. **地域森林計画対象民有林?** — triggers the 90-day notification and the 1 ha development gate (§4.1, §4.3).
4. **自然公園 zoning** — 特別保護地区 / 第1種 = no development; 第3種 / 普通地域 = workable (§6.1).
5. **重要土地等調査法 zone?** — check the 内閣府 PDF for the prefecture; is it within ~1 km of an SDF
   or US Forces facility? (§3.3).
6. **車馬等乗入れ規制区域?** — a snowcat model dies here. Hokkaido publishes the maps (§9.2).
7. **土砂災害警戒区域 / 特別警戒区域 / 急傾斜地崩壊危険区域 / 雪崩危険箇所** — prefectural hazard portals (§14).
8. **固定資産税納税通知書** — demand the actual bill. Do not estimate (§12.2).
9. **Existing water rights** — is any 許可水利権 or 慣行水利権 attached? (§13).
10. **Existing road access, trailer-capable, to a plowed public road** (§9.1).
11. **Existing 索道 on site?** — if a defunct ski area, is there an abandoned lift, and **who bears
    the removal obligation**? `⚠ UNVERIFIED — abandoned-lift decommissioning liability was not
    researched and could be a material hidden cost on a 廃止スキー場.`
