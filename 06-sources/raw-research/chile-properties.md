**Independent cross-check — the strongest quantitative finding in this study.** ODEPA's `[P1]` national *minimum* observed farmland price across its 1999–2008 transaction series is **5.8 UF/ha**. The Colbún cordillera fundo prices at **5.9 UF/ha**. Large Andean blocks are transacting **at the observed floor of the entire Chilean rural land market** — one is not merely buying cheap land, one is buying at the price where Chilean rural land stops being priced as land at all and starts being priced as scenery. `[E]` For context on trend, ODEPA's series moved 407 UF/ha (1999) → 235 (2003) → 356 (2008) `[P1]`, i.e. the market is cyclical, not monotonically rising.

**Structural reason the discount exists `[E]`:** Chilean farmland is priced off irrigation water and soil class. Cordillera has neither. Everything the client wants — vertical relief, snow-holding aspect, altitude, remoteness — is **worth zero to every other bidder in the market**. That is the arbitrage. It also means resale liquidity is near nil; this is a hold-forever asset, not a trade.

# 3. Question E — is freehold-to-the-summit actually available?

**Answer: the finding does NOT invert. But it splits cleanly, and the split is favourable to this specific thesis.**

Chile's *developed, lift-served* ski mountains are overwhelmingly **not** freehold. Chile's *undeveloped* alpine terrain overwhelmingly **is**, and trades in enormous blocks. The client wants the second category.

### 3.2 The two decisive counter-examples — freehold alpine terrain, proven
1. **Nevados de Longaví (Maule, ~36.2°S) — private freehold, and the owners say so on the record.** "The Nevado is located on **private property**." Owner and spokesman for the proprietors is **Enrique Gardeweg**, a livestock and agricultural businessman. The family "**do not want to cede the lands, and wish to always conserve ownership of the land**," while remaining open to commercial agreement. A winter-sports centre is under study on it: **~1,000 m of vertical**, good snow accumulation and quality, runs for all levels. `[P2]`
   → **This is the single most important finding in the file.** A named private family holds, in freehold, a Chilean nevado with 1,000 m of vertical and independently assessed ski potential, in the client's priority latitude band, and is publicly courting private partners. It is the exact asset class the mandate asserts should exist — and it demonstrably does.
2. **Ski Arpa** — Austrian skier **Tony Sponar** arrived in the 1960s, **bought a piece of mountain land**, and built what is now South America's only cat-skiing operation. `[P2]`
   → Freehold alpine terrain, run **privately with snowcats and no lift infrastructure** — a working precedent for the client's exact operating model.

### 3.3 What this means
- The correlation is **not** freehold-vs-concession. It is **developed-vs-undeveloped**. Lift-served terrain clusters inside SNASPE units and municipal/fiscal land because that is where roads and public investment went. Undeveloped Andean land is ordinary rural property, privately titled, and — as §1 shows — for sale in 3,000–27,000 ha blocks.
- The client's model (no lifts, no permanent construction, snowcats and ski touring) is **precisely the model that does not need the concession estate**, and precisely the model Ski Arpa already proves out.
- **Caveat, and it is a real one `[E]`:** private title in the Andes commonly *stops* at a reserve boundary, a contour, or the *línea de altas cumbres* — the watershed line, which is also the international frontier and a classic Chilean *deslinde* formula. Whether any specific predio's title runs **to the summit** or **to a lower boundary** is answerable **only from the deslindes in the CBR inscription**. **I verified this for zero properties.** For #1 (Antuco) this is acute: **Parque Nacional Laguna del Laja** occupies much of Volcán Antuco, so the predio may abut rather than include the best ground.

# 4. Holding cost — impuesto territorial `[P1]/[P2]`

- **Agricultural exempt avalúo: CLP 24,222,145** (from 1 Jan 2020). A property whose **total** avalúo is at or below this is **100% exempt**. `[P2]` citing SII
- **Agricultural rate cut from 1% → 0.514%** of taxable avalúo. `[P2]`
- Formula: `contribución = (avalúo total − avalúo exento) × tasa`
- Agricultural avalúo is a function of **soil class, surface, sector, and a deduction %** driven by road type and km to the municipal seat `[P1]` SII. **Remote + bad road + class VIII = the lowest values the system produces.**
- SII publishes **Tablas de Valores Unitarios de Suelos** per soil class in CLP/ha (Reavalúo Agrícola 2024, values in 2nd-half-2023 currency; methodology in Res. Ex. SII N°12, 24-01-2020). `[P1]`
- 2020 agricultural reappraisal covered **1,068,294 properties**. `[P1]`
- Non-agricultural residential exempt amount, 1st semester 2026: **CLP 60,030,710** `[P2]` — context only, does not apply here.

# 5. Snow at 36–39°S

**Positive:**
- **Corralco (38.4°S):** base ~1,450–1,550 m, top of the "La Cumbre" lift **2,400 m**. Operator claims **9 m annual snowfall** `[P3]`. Independently, Nevasport reported Corralco accumulating **almost 4 m in one exceptional season** `[P2]`, and 100% of the ski area open in early August.
- **Nevados de Chillán (36.9°S):** vertical **870 m**; **10,000 ha skiable domain, only 500 ha developed**; 24–30+ runs; *Las Tres Marías* at **13 km is the longest run in South America**; season **June–October** `[P2]/[P3]`.
- **Conflicting snow figures at Chillán — do not rely on either:** one source gives "average annual snowfall **137 cm**", another "fallen snow can reach **10 m**." These are not reconcilable. `[P3]`

**Negative, and material:**
- **Megasequía `[P1]` (CR2):** every year from 2010 onward below the historical precipitation mean; ~**30% average deficit**; the longest run of dry years in the observational record, **with no analogue in the last millennium**.
- **0 °C isotherm rising ~23 m/decade, 1958–2006, central Andes `[P1]`.** Mean storm H0 in central Chile ~**2,200 m**, ranging <1,500 m (cold storms) to >3,500 m (warm storms). Low resorts such as **Lagunillas (2,200 m, 33°S) are already materially impaired** `[P2]`.
- **DGA `[P1]`, snow route of 20 July:** **43% deficit** in snow accumulation vs historical mean, translating to a **69% deficit in water equivalent**. Reported in the Ñuble context. *(Year ambiguous in the snippet — most consistent with 2026. Verify.)*
- DGA runs **11 snow routes** June–August; Ñuble's hydrometric network has **76 stations**; the **Nevados de Chillán station sits above 2,000 m** and is nationally significant `[P1]`.

**Reading `[E]`:** the 36–39°S band is materially more snow-secure than 33°S — Corralco's 2,400 m summit and Chillán's 870 m vertical both sit well above a ~2,200 m mean storm freezing level, whereas 33°S resorts are being squeezed. But a **43%/69% deficit year in the target region** shows the band is not immune. **Base elevation is the whole game.** Any predio whose usable terrain tops out below ~1,800 m should be rejected on snow grounds alone. I could not obtain the altitude range of a single listed property, so **not one candidate here can yet be screened on this criterion.**

# 6. Volcanic risk `[P1]` SERNAGEOMIN

- **Volcán Antuco** (2,985 m; Sierra Velluda 3,585 m): hazard map published as *Carta Geológica de Chile, Serie Geología Ambiental 27*, **1:50,000**. Principal hazards **lahars, lava flows, pyroclastic flows**. SERNAGEOMIN's stated worst case is **lahars down the Río Laja valley at winter's end, when accumulated snow volume is greatest** — i.e. **the hazard peaks exactly in ski season**. A Holocene gravitational collapse ~**6,200 yr BP** dammed the Río Laja and formed Laguna del Laja. **Directly relevant to candidate #1.**
- **Nevados de Chillán:** dominant hazards **lahars, debris flows, lava flows**, channelled down **Renegado, Shangri-La, Chillán, San José, Santa Gertrudis, Gato and Las Minas** valleys. **Renegado and Shangri-La are the Las Trancas / Valle Hermoso corridor** — the region's tourism spine sits in the modelled lahar path.
- **Lonquimay** and **Callaqui** are monitored by the RNVV; **SENAPRED publishes evacuation plans** for Nevados de Chillán (Ñuble), Antuco/Copahue/Callaqui (Biobío), Llaima/Lonquimay (Araucanía).
- **Insurability `[P2]`:** Chilean multi-risk policies **do** offer *"Incendio y Daños Materiales causados por erupción Volcánica."* Named-peril, with exclusions: the complementary earthquake/volcanic cover **excludes adobe construction** and mural paintings; **ash removal is covered only where ash caused direct physical damage** — clearing accumulated exterior ash is not covered.
- **Development restrictiveness — NOT VERIFIED.** I did not establish whether SERNAGEOMIN hazard zoning is binding on rural construction, nor whether insurers decline or load high-hazard zones. `[E]` Note the structural mitigant: **the client's no-permanent-construction model minimises exposure by design** — lahars destroy buildings and roads, not grazing land, and a business with no fixed plant has little to insure. That is a genuine advantage of the thesis, not a workaround.
