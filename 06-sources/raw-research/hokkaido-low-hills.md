# Hokkaido: Privately-Ownable Isolated Hills with 250–400 m of Local Relief
### Terrain-first survey for a small ski area of the 吉雪 (Jixue) type
### 40 ha · 296 m vertical · 910 m longest run · 19° average · no lifts

Research date: 2026-08-28. Method: 75-query Japanese-language web search budget (73 used).
WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy on **every** domain attempted
(ja.wikipedia.org, www.data.jma.go.jp, www.mlit.go.jp), so every figure below comes from
search-result snippets. Figures that could not be confirmed are marked **未確認 (unconfirmed)**
rather than estimated.

Source tags: **[P1]** government / JMA / GSI · **[P2]** news media · **[P3]** other published
sources (hiking databases, encyclopaedic, local) · **[E]** my own inference.

## 0. The headline finding

The client's hypothesis is correct, and the previous research was answering the wrong question.

A 296 m vertical drop at 19° average requires a slope roughly **900 m long and 300 m high** —
which is not a mountain. It is a hillside. Hokkaido has thousands of them, and the 1897
**北海道国有未開地処分法 (Hokkaidō Kokuyū Mikaichi Shobun-hō)** granted exactly this class of
land — valley floors and the low hills above them — to settlers in freehold.

The forest-ownership statistics confirm it directly **[P1]**:

| Owner | Share of Hokkaido's 5.54 M ha of forest | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| 国有林 National forest | **55.3 %** | "奥地の脊梁山地や水源地域に広く分布" — remote spine ranges and headwater zones (Daisetsuzan, Hidaka, etc.) |
| 私有林等 Private forest | **27.9 %** | lowlands and low hills near settlement |
| 道有林 Prefectural forest | 11.0 % (~610,000 ha, 13 管理区) | includes Yōtei-zan, part of the Niseko range, part of Daisetsuzan |
| 市町村有林 Municipal forest | 5.8 % | around towns |

So ~45 % of Hokkaido forest is non-national, and it is concentrated precisely on the
low, road-accessible, farm-adjacent hills the client is asking for.

### The legal point that reverses the problem [P1]

| | 山林 Forest land | 農地 Farmland |
|---|---|---|
| Permission to buy | **None required** | **農業委員会 (Agricultural Committee) permission mandatory** under 農地法 art. 3 |
| Consequence of no permission | n/a | transaction is **legally void** |
| Formality | Notify the mayor within **90 days** (森林法 / 森林の土地の所有者届出制度). From **April 2026** the form records the new owner's **nationality**. Parcels >1 ha also notify under 国土利用計画法 | — |

**The hill is the easy part to buy. The farm is the hard part.** A foreign individual can
acquire forest land in freehold with no prior approval; acquiring the attached 農地 requires
qualifying as a farmer before the Agricultural Committee. Any acquisition strategy should be
built around the 山林 parcel, with the farmland treated as the constraint — not the reverse.

### Recommended order of field investigation

**1. 赤井川村 Akaigawa-mura — the single best match.**
The caldera is an ellipse 6–7 km across with a floor at ~150 m and a rim topping out at 725 m
[P3]. Every metre of the inner wall is a uniform, continuous slope of exactly the gradient the
brief specifies: 300 m of vertical over ~900 m of run is 18.2° — against Jixue's 19° average.
A hiking record describes the ascent of Daikoku-yama from the caldera floor as starting at
about 150 m and steepening from about 250 m [P3] — i.e. the productive 300–400 m band begins
right where the farmland ends. The rim carries at least four named summits, counter-clockwise
from north: **元服山 Genpuku-yama, 大黒山 Daikoku-yama (725 m), 丸山 Maruyama, 石山 Ishiyama**
[P3]; only Daikoku's elevation is published — the other three are **未確認** and must be read
off the GSI 1:25,000 sheet.

Why this village and not its neighbours: Akaigawa is **absent from the 11-municipality list of
the Niseko-Shakotan-Otaru Quasi-National Park** (小樽市・蘭越町・ニセコ町・倶知安町・共和町・
岩内町・積丹町・古平町・余市町・泊村・神恵内村) and **absent from the Shikotsu-Tōya National
Park list** **[P1]**. Its neighbours Yoichi, Kutchan and Kyōgoku are all inside one or the
other. Akaigawa and Niki sit in the gap between them.

Supporting facts: the village is 280.09 km², eight-tenths forest, with the centre and all
farmland on the caldera floor [P3]; it is described as "北海道でも有数の豪雪地帯" — among
Hokkaido's foremost heavy-snow districts, with a basin-interior climate [P3]; and Kiroro Resort
(summit 1,180 m) operates in the same village, proving the snow and the terrain [P3]. The
volcano is extinct — andesite/dacite/rhyolite, active 1.7–1.3 Ma [P3] — so there is no
volcanic-hazard zoning. Aspect is the deciding variable: target the **south and west rim**,
whose inner walls face north and north-east.

**2. 夕張市 Yubari — the exact spec, already cut.**
Reisui-zan gives **404 m from a base of 298 m** [P3] — this is not an estimate, it is the
measured vertical of an existing ski area with 18 runs. The operator (夕張リゾート, a Hong Kong
fund, after the city sold to a Chinese buyer for ¥240 M in 2017) filed for bankruptcy in
February 2021 [P2]. Yubari has Japan's most distressed municipal land market. The 富良野芦別
Prefectural Natural Park (35,756 ha) is centred on 夕張岳 and 芦別岳 well to the east **[P1]**,
so Reisui-zan is almost certainly outside it — **but the boundary must be verified [E]**.

**3. 黒松内町 Kuromatsunai — cheapest land, verified snow, but check the fault.**
740 m summit, ~550 m of relief, 特別豪雪地帯, maximum recorded snow depth 214 cm (Feb 2022,
a 40-year record) with pre-instrumental years around 300 cm [P2]. Outside every park. Caveat:
the **黒松内低地断層帯**, an active reverse-fault zone ≥32 km long running north–south from
Suttsu Bay through the town to Oshamambe **[P1]** — relevant to any building, not to the slope.
Note also that the town centre itself sits on *gentle* hills; the 250–400 m relief is on the
flanking ranges, not in the lowland belt.

**4. 馬追丘陵 Maoi Hills — the ownership-safe fallback.**
The only candidate that is unambiguously private throughout: a 50 km long, 5–8 km wide ridge
rising to 287.1 m out of the Ishikari plain, entirely in farm and settlement country with no
park and essentially no national forest **[E]**. Relief of ~250–270 m sits at the very bottom
of the client's range and snow is the weakest of any candidate. Worth a look only if ownership
certainty outranks snow quality.

## 2. Park exclusions — the search envelope

Hokkaido now has **7 national parks and 4 quasi-national parks**, not the 6 + 5 in the brief.
日高山脈襟裳国定公園 was **abolished** and re-designated as **日高山脈襟裳十勝国立公園** on
**25 June 2024** — Japan's 35th national park and Hokkaido's 7th, the first new one since
Kushiro-shitsugen in 1987 **[P1]**.

### National parks (7)
利尻礼文サロベツ · 知床 · 阿寒摩周 · 釧路湿原 · 大雪山 · 支笏洞爺 · 日高山脈襟裳十勝

- **支笏洞爺 Shikotsu-Tōya** — 6 cities, 7 towns, 1 village: 札幌市, 苫小牧市, 千歳市, 登別市,
  恵庭市, 伊達市, ニセコ町, 真狩村, 喜茂別町, 京極町, 倶知安町, 洞爺湖町, 壮瞥町, 白老町 **[P1]**
  → **Kyōgoku and Kimobetsu are both inside.** The brief's suggestion to work the "west side"
  of Kyōgoku does not help: Yōtei-zan is *west* of Kyōgoku and is both park and 道有林 **[P1]**.
  Kyōgoku is squeezed between Yōtei (park) and the Nakayama/Muine range (park). **Deprioritise.**
- **日高山脈襟裳十勝** — 252,177 ha (245,668 ha land), Japan's largest terrestrial national park;
  帯広市, 日高町, 平取町, 新冠町, 浦河町, 様似町, えりも町, 新ひだか町, 清水町, 芽室町,
  中札内村, 大樹町, 広尾町 **[P1]**
  → **This invalidates the brief's Hidaka targets.** 日高町門別, 平取町 and 新冠町 are all now
  national-park municipalities. The western foothills may still fall outside the drawn boundary,

## 5. Immediate next steps

1. **地理院地図 (GSI Maps, `maps.gsi.go.jp`)** — open the Akaigawa caldera and measure the
   inner-wall profile. Confirm the 300 m band, its length, and its aspect on the south and west
   rim. This settles the terrain question in one session and requires no further web research.
2. **森林計画図 / 国土数値情報 A13** — overlay national vs private forest on the caldera rim.
   This settles the ownership question.
3. **Reverse the acquisition logic.** Buy the 山林; treat the 農地 as a separate, harder problem.
   A forest parcel of 40 ha carrying 300 m of relief is purchasable with no prior permission —
   only a 90-day notification and, above 1 ha, a 国土利用計画法 filing.
4. **Yubari as a parallel track.** Reisui-zan already has 404 m of measured vertical, cut runs,
   and a bankrupt owner. It is the fastest route from "terrain identified" to "terrain owned."
