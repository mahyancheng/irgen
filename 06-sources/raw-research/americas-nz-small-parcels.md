## 0. The benchmark, restated as a geometry test

Jixue (吉雪), Jilin City: 400,000 m² (40 ha), 13 runs, longest run 910 m at 19° average, slopes 6–33°. `[given]`

- 910 m of slope at 19° = **296 m vertical** over a **860 m horizontal footprint**. `[E]`
- To hold that inside 40 ha the parcel must be roughly **860 m long in the fall line × ~465 m wide**. `[E]`
- **Consequence:** 40 ha is the *floor*, and only if the parcel is correctly shaped and steep. A squarish 40 ha parcel (632 × 632 m) dropping 300 m implies a 25° mean gradient — near the top of Jixue's own range. In practice **60–150 ha is the comfortable band** for 300 m of vertical at skiable gradients, plus run-out, road and a base bench. `[E]`
- **Screening rule I applied:** parcel must plausibly contain ≥300 m of vertical *entirely inside the deed*, including the top.

## 2. PER-PROPERTY NOTES

### 2.1 Slopes at St Mary's Glacier, Colorado — **the leading candidate**

- **Area and title.** 373 acres (150.95 ha) made up of **81 individual parcels — 78 on-mountain plus 3 forming the parking area** `[P2]`. That parcel structure is the signature of **patented mining claims consolidated into a fee holding** `[E]`, which is exactly what makes a 12,000 ft summit privately ownable in Colorado. The property is described as *bordered by* Arapaho National Forest and James Peak Wilderness `[P2]` — i.e. it is an **inholding, not a permit area**. **No USFS special use permit is mentioned in any source.** `[E]`
- **Vertical.** Base "a little over 10,000 ft", top "a little over 12,000 ft" → **~1,200 ft / 366 m** `[P2]`. **This clears the 300 m benchmark inside the deed** — the single most important fact in this entire report.
- **Aspect.** Southern aspect of Kingston Peak `[P2]`. A south face is the worst aspect for snow retention; at 3,050–3,660 m in the Colorado Front Range it still holds a season, but expect solar damage in March–April and a materially shorter reliable window than a north face. `[E]`
- **Access.** 8821 Fall River Road `[P2]`, ~8 miles north of I-70, ~45 miles west of Denver `[P2]`. Fall River Road is a county road serving the year-round St Mary's/Alice community, so a maintained public road reaches the base — **but confirm at deed level that the parcel fronts the maintained road and does not rely on a shared or prescriptive spur.** `[E]`
- **Improvements.** Buildings, old lifts and carved ski runs remain `[P2]`; historically one T-bar and two rope tows `[P2]`; it operated mid-1930s to 1986 `[P2]`. Listing describes ~35 acres of buildable lots and capacity for guest services building, lodge, maintenance and parking `[P3]`.
- **Price.** USD 7,000,000 (LIV Sotheby's; brokers Kristin Michas, Josh Jackson, Les Pfenning) `[P2]` = **MYR 28,619,500**; **MYR 189,599/ha**; USD 18,767/acre.
- **Fit.** 151 ha vs 40 ha target (3.8×) and 366 m vs 300 m vertical. **Exceeds both.** Existing cleared pistes, road, and a village at the base = the low-CAPEX case the client wants.
- **Open items:** (a) title commitment showing all 81 parcels and whether the summit parcels are included; (b) **severed minerals** — on ex-mining-claim ground this must be checked claim by claim; (c) Clear Creek County zoning of the parcels and whether private recreation needs a permit; (d) still-on-market confirmation and whether the price has moved since 2023.

### 2.3 Meadowlark Ski Area & Lake Resort, Wyoming — good numbers, **deed/permit unresolved, probably sold**

- Land.com/Land&Farm carry it as **"320 acres in Washakie County, Wyoming"** `[P3]` = 129.50 ha. Price cut from USD 5,750,000 to **USD 4,995,000** (Feb 2026) `[P2]` = **MYR 20,422,058**, **MYR 157,700/ha**.
- 35 trails, **230+ acres of ski terrain, 800+ ft (244 m) vertical, two lifts, ~300 in/yr snowfall** `[P3]`. Big Horn Mountains on US-16 between Buffalo and Ten Sleep `[P3]` — a **plowed federal-aid highway**, which is a genuine winter-access advantage. 15,000–25,000 sq ft base lodge plus 15 cabins, 4 duplexes, 6 motel rooms `[P3]`.

Whether the 320 deeded acres *contains* the 230 acres of ski terrain, or whether the terrain is a USFS special use permit and the 320 acres is the lake resort and lodge, is **not established by any source I could reach.** `[E]` Given the pattern at Antelope Butte and Sleeping Giant in the same state, assume permit until a title report says otherwise.
- **Timing:** best-and-final offers were reported due 1 March 2026 with a target close of 1 June 2026 `[P2]`. **Likely no longer available.**

### 2.4 Cherry Peak Resort, Utah — meets the physical spec exactly, at 4.5× the price

- **174 deeded acres (70.42 ha)**, explicitly "**surrounded by public land**", between the 44,523-ac Mount Naomi Wilderness and 14,000+ ac of Utah state land `[P2]`.
- **Base 5,775 ft → summit 7,040 ft = 1,265 ft / 386 m vertical** `[P2]`; 29 runs, 3 triple chairs, conveyor, snowmaking, 3-level lodge `[P2]`.
- USD 22,500,000 (Jul 2026) = **MYR 91,991,250**; **MYR 1,306,409/ha** (USD 129,000/deeded acre) `[P2]`.
- **On the numbers this is the closest analogue to Jixue in the Americas: 70 ha, 386 m vertical.** But: (a) 220+ acres of skiable terrain against 174 deeded acres means **~46 acres of the ski area is off the deed** `[E]`; (b) base at only 1,760 m in northern Utah — snow-reliable but not alpine; (c) it is a going concern with lifts, snowmaking and staff — the **opposite** of the client's extremely-low-OPEX brief.

### 2.5 Mt Lyford Village & Alpine Resort, New Zealand — the only NZ asset that passes the title test

- **677 ha of freehold high-country land, including some of the highest-altitude freehold land in New Zealand**, with a lodge, land already subdivided for alpine chalets, and **resource consent for a further 149 sections** `[P2]`. Marketed by expressions of interest, **November 2024** `[P2]`.
- No asking price published. The agent (Doug Simpson) said it was "very difficult to put a figure on" and that judged on recent high-country sales it "had to be in the tens of millions" NZD `[P2]`. At NZD 20–50 m that is **MYR 48–120 m**.
- **Still described as family-owned and operated through 2026** `[P2]` — I found **no report of a sale**. The EOI may have failed to clear, which is the client's opening.
- Village context: individual sections and cabins trade at **NZD 150,000–695,000** (MYR 360,000–1,668,000) `[P3]`; a 169 Mt Lyford Forest Drive lifestyle block is listed at **NZD 1,500,000 + GST** (MYR 3,600,000) `[P3]`. The field drew ~20,000 skiers in a recent season `[P2]`.
- **Answering the brief's question directly:** I found **no evidence that a larger block, or the ski field on its own, is separately available** as at Aug 2026. What is on the market at Mt Lyford is individual village sections and one ~lifestyle-scale block. The 677 ha whole-of-resort EOI is the only route to the field itself, and it is not currently advertised.
- **NZ regulatory overlay:** non-urban land over **5 ha is "sensitive land"** under the Overseas Investment Act and needs OIO consent, with investor-test and (for sensitive land) benefit-to-New-Zealand requirements `[P2]`. The **Overseas Investment (National Interest Test and Other Matters) Amendment Act 2026** consolidates this into a national interest test with much shorter timeframes `[P2]`. The previously-established point that a defunct ski area is provisionally **not** "farm land" under s 6(1) still matters — it escapes the farmland advertising/offer regime — but **it does not escape the >5 ha sensitive-land consent.** Any 40 ha+ NZ purchase by this buyer needs OIO consent. `[E]`

A ski area with lifts cannot fit in 65 acres. **The runs were on Eldorado National Forest under a special use permit; the 65 acres is the base parcel.** `[E]` This is precisely the trap the brief warns about. ### 2.8 Chile — the market is real but it subdivides to the wrong size

**Established and not re-researched:** a Malaysian may own Chilean freehold outright with no consent regime; undeveloped alpine is ordinary private property.

What I found on the ground:

- **The dominant product near every Chilean ski centre is the *parcelación* at 0.5–4 ha — far too small.** Confirmed at Malalcahuello/Corralco (Araucanía): **Fundo Las Raíces**, a 96 ha park adjoining the Malalcahuello National Reserve, subdivided into **35 lots of 1–4 ha from UF 2,300**, 15 min from Malalcahuello and 20 min from Corralco `[P3]`; **Loteo Corral de Caballos**, >87 ha in the Manzanar/Río El Indio sector `[P3]`; and generic 5,000 m² lots 3 km from Malalcahuello `[P3]`. **La Helvetia** offers 5,000 m² parcels inside 50 ha of native forest near the snow `[P3]`.
- **Las Trancas / Nevados de Chillán (Pinto, Ñuble).** Cordillera-sector parcels parallel to Las Trancas on **ruta N-555**, reached by **16 km of dirt road, 4×4 recommended**, with views of the Nevado and Volcán Chillán — **only 4 parcels, 1.5–2 ha each** `[P3]`. A 2 ha hotel complex (4 rolls of 5,000 m², 23,375 m² total) fronting a glacier in the Shangri-La corridor at Termas de Chillán, **surrounded by a ~90 ha ecological reserve** `[P3]`. Two parcels of 5,300 m² at CLP 35,000,000 (MYR 156,433) `[P3]`. A 5,000 m² lot **with its own rol** in the Los Pellines precordillera, 22 km on the Termas de Chillán road `[P3]`. A ~3 ha property at km 56, sector La Invernada, Pinto `[P3]`.
- **Campos de Ahumada, San Esteban, Los Andes (Valparaíso Region) — the most promising Chilean sector for this brief.** `[E]` It sits in the Aconcagua precordillera near **El Arpa** (Chile's freehold cat-skiing operation, ~4,000 private acres, snowcats to 12,000 ft, 108 km from Santiago `[P2]`). Listings found: **3.2767 ha at CLP 65,000,000** (CLP 19.8 m/ha = **MYR 88,671/ha**) at **~1,600 m with registered water rights** `[P3]`; **1.8 ha at UF 1,700 at 1,550 m** with a spring yielding 5,000 l/day `[P3]`; an 18,000 m² lot in the community sector `[P3]`; and separately a **58.2 ha property served by Route E-615** `[P3]`. Electricity and APR water feasibility, 5 min from San Esteban, 10 min from Los Andes `[P3]`.
- **The one large priced cordillera block I could document: Futaleufú, 251.5 ha.** CLP 1,760,500,000 total = **CLP 7,000,000/ha = 48,850 UF ≈ USD 2,025,000 = MYR 7,868,555; MYR 31,287/ha** `[P3]`. Bounded by the El Saltillo and Corrida Grande streams and ~800 m of accessible lakeshore on Lago Noroeste; **"la parte más alta del campo colinda con cordillera fiscal Cerro Teta"** — *the highest part of the property adjoins state cordillera* `[P3]`. **That sentence is the Chilean version of the deed/permit trap: the top is not in the title.** Access is by vehicle only to the foot of the property; the interior is passable on foot or horseback `[P3]`. Palena Province, Los Lagos — low elevation, maritime, marginal for a reliable 300 m ski vertical. `[E]`
- **Biobío:** a 2,968 ha *campo cordillerano forestal* at Quilaco `[P3]` — outside the size brief but evidence that hectare-scale cordillera blocks do trade.
- **Price context.** Chilean rural land is priced by productivity and services, not altitude: Frutillar agricultural at **UF 303/ha**; Río Bueno cordillera-adjacent at **CLP 12–14.5 m/ha**; Futaleufú at **CLP 7 m/ha** `[P2]/[P3]`. The same 5,000 m² parcel runs from UF 766 in Aysén to UF 9,895 in Los Ríos `[P2]`. **The previously-established cordillera band of CLP 229,000–600,000/ha is an order of magnitude below every priced listing I found** — that band applies to high, dry, unproductive, roadless cordillera, and it is *not* what is on the open market near ski infrastructure. `[E]` Budget **CLP 7–20 m/ha (MYR 31,000–89,000/ha)** for accessible precordillera with water and power. `[E]`
- **Verdict on Chile:** no single 20–300 ha cordillera parcel with a confirmed 300 m of *in-title* vertical surfaced in this pass. The route is (a) buy an entire small *loteo* before it is subdivided, (b) assemble adjacent rolls in the Campos de Ahumada / San Esteban sector, or (c) commission a Chilean *corredor de campos* to source off-market. **Record the *rol* for every parcel** — several listings advertise "rol propio", which is the mark of a separately titled, separately transferable lot. `[P3]`

### 2.9 Argentina — legally the worst option for this buyer

Included because the brief specified Spanish for Argentina.

- **Ley 26.737** (Régimen de Protección al Dominio Nacional sobre la Propiedad, Posesión o Tenencia de las Tierras Rurales) `[P1]`:
  - **15% cap** on foreign ownership of rural land **per province**; no more than **30%** of that held by one nationality.
  - **A single foreign titleholder may not exceed 1,000 ha in the "zona núcleo"**, or the equivalent area for the territorial location.
  - **Prohibition** on foreign title/possession of land containing or riparian to significant permanent water bodies.
  - **Prohibition** on land in **Zonas de Seguridad de Frontera**, subject to the exceptions and procedures of Decreto-Ley 15.385/44 as amended by Ley 23.554.
- **The Andean ski corridor (Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut) lies inside the border security zone**, so a Malaysian buyer needs prior clearance, not merely a conveyance. `[E]` Foreigners currently hold ~13 m ha (≈5% of Argentine rural land), concentrated in exactly these cordilleran and border areas `[P2]`.
- A reform to **remove the limits on foreign purchases** was before the Senate for 6 August 2026 `[P2]` — **not yet law on the evidence I have.**
- Market colour (Neuquén): **212 ha on RN40 at San Martín de los Andes opposite Chapelco airport**, subdividable to 50–60 ha minimums at **USD 6.5/m² (= USD 65,000/ha)**, total USD 3,250,000–3,900,000 (MYR 13,287,625–15,945,150) `[P3]`; **202 ha with 600 m of Lago Hermoso shoreline, 30 km from Cerro Chapelco** `[P3]`; **62 ha at Aluminé** on paved RP23 `[P3]`.
- **Recommendation: deprioritise Argentina.** Chile delivers the same cordillera with no consent regime and no border-zone prohibition. `[E]`

### 2.10 Canada — reconsidered at the new scale, still fails on tenure

- **Alpine land in BC is Crown.** ~94% of BC is Crown-owned, and since 1999 any tourism operator on Crown land must hold tenure — temporary licence, licence, or lease `[P2]`. **Powder King** is the concrete example: 900+ acres of skiable terrain with a renewed **60-year development agreement and master plan permitting expansion and acquisition of Crown land**, i.e. the mountain is not owned `[P2]`. Asking CAD 6,495,000 (cut from 8,250,000) `[P2]` = **MYR 19,160,250**. The **340-acre Azouzetta Lake Resort** is the tangible land component `[P3]`.
- **Fee-simple mountain land at 40–200 ha with real vertical does exist in the Kootenays/Selkirks** but it is valley-and-bench land, not alpine: a **307+ acre Selkirk Mountains property with direct Crown land frontage**; **160 south-facing acres near Rosebud Lake, zoned R4, bordered by Crown land**; **50 private acres above Kootenay Lake**; a **1,026-acre ranch** `[P3]`. **None of these is advertised with a vertical drop**, and "bordered by Crown land" is the Canadian statement that **the top is not yours.** `[E]`
- **Quebec / Chic-Chocs:** no private freehold alpine listings surfaced. The Chic-Chocs are dominated by the 1,129.71 km² Chic-Chocs Wildlife Reserve and Sépaq/park land; Mount Lyall's backcountry ski facilities are run by a co-op `[P2]`. `[E]`
- **Federal foreign-buyer ban — answering the brief's question directly.** The *Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act* runs to **1 January 2027**. It bites only on **residential property (buildings of three dwelling units or fewer, plus their land) inside a CMA or CA**. **Since 27 March 2023 it expressly does not apply to vacant land**, including vacant land zoned residential or mixed-use, which a non-Canadian may buy and use for any purpose. Property **outside a CMA/CA — rural and small-town Canada — is outside the ban entirely.** `[P2]` **Conclusion: the ban does not touch vacant rural or recreational mountain land.** The Canadian obstacle is Crown tenure, not the buyer's nationality. `[E]`

### 2.11 Patented mining claims — the assembly play

**Answering the brief's question: are contiguous groups available that would total 40+ ha?**

- **What a patented claim is:** a grant of land *and* minerals from the federal government — private land, conveyed by **general warranty deed with title insurance available**, carrying **both surface and mineral rights** `[P2]`. Unpatented claims carry mineral rights only `[P2]`. This is the only mechanism by which genuinely high alpine ground in the US Rockies is privately titled. `[E]`
- **Size ceiling confirmed by the market:** listings cluster at **5.5, 8.32, 8.35, 10.33 and up to 20.65 acres** `[P3]` — consistent with the ~20.66-acre lode maximum. **A single claim can never reach 40 ha (99 acres).** Assembly is mandatory.
- **What I found that is already assembled:**
  - **San Juan County, CO (Silverton/Eureka):** the *Frances Lode*, 5.513 ac, ~8 mi N of Silverton, camping/building/RV/prospecting permitted `[P3]`; the *Ben Bolt*, 8.35 ac, same area `[P3]`; a spread of 8.32–20.65 ac claims via LandHub, Land Resellers, Out West Land Sales and Silverton Realty `[P3]`.
  - **Lake County, CO (Leadville) — the best-value high ground found in this study `[E]`:** a **41-acre patented claim between Monitor Gulch and Black Cloud Creek, surrounded entirely by San Isabel National Forest** `[P3]`; a **10.33-acre claim with surface and mineral rights at 12,500 ft** just east of downtown Leadville `[P3]`; a **40.8-acre recreational parcel in the Backcountry (BC) zone accessed via CR 52 / Forest Road 111**, 20 minutes from Leadville `[P3]`; a 10-acre claim in Empire Valley `[P3]`. Lake County's total advertised rural inventory was ~USD 64 m over 749 acres `[P3]`.
  - **Montana:** **45.58 acres of patented land plus a ~20-acre claim in contiguous parcels below Antoine Butte** — the closest thing to a ready-made 26 ha assembly `[P3]`; a 7.7-acre patented hard-rock claim ¼ mile from Yellowstone and ¼ mile from the Absaroka-Beartooth Wilderness `[P3]`. 32 mining land/claim listings statewide, USD 6,500 to 3,900,000, average USD 826,411 `[P3]`.
- **Verdict:** contiguous groups totalling 40+ ha are **not currently on the open market as a package** anywhere I could see. Building one means buying 4–8 separate claims from separate owners over a period, with **National Forest in the gaps between claims**, and no guarantee the claims line up in the fall line. This is a multi-year assembly project, not a purchase. `[E]`
- **CERCLA:** the Bonita Peak Mining District Superfund site covers the Silverton/Animas headwaters. **Buying patented ground inside it exposes the owner to potential CERCLA liability as a current owner.** Any San Juan assembly needs an All Appropriate Inquiries Phase I ESA to preserve the bona fide prospective purchaser defence. `[E]` — *flagged in the brief; I could not obtain a primary EPA boundary source within budget.*

## 3. THE RISKS THAT APPEAR AT SMALL SCALE

### 3.1 Legal road access — a 40 ha parcel frequently does not own its access

This is the risk that kills small mountain deals. `[E]`

- **A landlocked parcel has no legal access to a public right of way, and it is common in rural and mountain areas** `[P2]`.
- **The three ways access exists in Colorado** `[P2]`:
  1. **Deeded (express) easement** — recorded, runs with the land, insurable, survives sale of the servient parcel. **This is the only form to accept.**
  2. **Easement by necessity** — implied when a parcel was severed in a way that left it without a road. Depends on proving unity of title and severance; litigable.
  3. **Prescriptive easement** — requires **open, notorious, adverse and continuous use for more than 18 years** in Colorado. Adverse is the hard element: use that was *permitted* by the neighbour is by definition not adverse and creates **no right at all**. A gate that has been unlocked for 30 years out of neighbourliness is worth nothing.
- **Private condemnation** exists as a backstop: the Colorado Bill of Rights provides that private property shall not be taken for private use *except for private ways of necessity* `[P2]`. But the owner must first show there is no other reasonable access and no present legally enforceable right, then make **reasonable good-faith efforts to purchase** an easement, and only then petition the court `[P2]`. That is years and lawyers, after closing.
- **Practical rule for this buyer:** require the title commitment to show a **recorded, appurtenant, non-exclusive easement for ingress/egress and utilities, of stated width, benefiting the specific parcels**, and require it to be affirmatively insured. Reject "historic use", "the neighbours have never minded", or an access road shown only on a plat. `[E]`
- **The 81-parcel structure at St Mary's makes this harder, not easier** — access must be traced parcel to parcel through the on-mountain group, not just to the parking parcels. `[E]`

### 3.2 Winter road maintenance — who ploughs, and at what cost

- **Counties routinely do not plough what you assume they plough.** Summit County, Colorado states it does not maintain state highways, streets in incorporated towns, **or private roads** `[P2]`. Many Colorado mountain county roads are formally designated "not maintained in winter". `[P2]`
- **Colorado law contemplates exactly this situation:** CRS 33-14-110 allows snowmobiles on the roadway of streets and highways **not maintained for winter motor vehicle traffic**, where the local subdivision has authorised snowmobile routes by ordinance or resolution `[P1]`. In other words, the statutory fallback for an unploughed mountain road is *a snowmobile*, which happens to suit Phase 1 — but it also means no delivery truck, no ambulance, and no guest in a rental car. `[E]`
- **Costs** `[P2]/[P3]`: per-visit residential USD 30–100; seasonal residential contracts USD 200–600; **commercial seasonal contracts USD 2,000–10,000+**; an HOA plouging a whole subdivision can get to ~USD 70 per unit. For a private access road of a few kilometres to a ski parcel, budget the commercial band — **USD 5,000–15,000 per season (MYR 20,000–61,000)** as an order of magnitude, rising steeply with length, gradient and snowfall. `[E]`
- **Ask three questions before any offer:** (1) Is the access road a *county-maintained* road for its full length to the parcel? (2) If it becomes private at some point, who ploughs that segment today, under what agreement, and does the agreement bind successors? (3) Is there a road maintenance agreement recorded against the title, and what is the cost-share formula? `[E]`

### 3.3 Whether the land above the parcel is federal/Crown — this caps the achievable vertical

**This is the structural reason small parcels underperform on vertical.** `[E]`

- In the US West, a private parcel almost always stops below the ridge, and the ground above is National Forest. **St Mary's is the exception that proves the rule** — its deed reportedly runs to ~12,000 ft `[P2]`, which is why it is the leading candidate.
- **What you may and may not do on the National Forest above your parcel** `[P1]`:
  - **A special use authorisation is NOT required for non-commercial recreation** — camping, picnicking, hiking, fishing, boating, hunting, horseback riding — unless it is a non-commercial *group* use (the threshold cited is gatherings of 75 or more) or involves filming/photography. **Private ski touring and personal turns on adjoining NF land therefore need no permit.**
  - **A permit IS required** to operate a business, for outfitting and guiding, and for use of the Forest **for financial gain**. So: no paying guests, no guided operation, no commercial cat-skiing on the NF above you.
  - **Any installation or occupancy — a rope tow, a groomed run, a snowcat road, a shelter — requires authorisation.** Ski area term special use permits under **16 USC 497b** are issued for terms up to 40 years `[P1]`, which is a multi-year NEPA process, not a formality.
- **Net effect:** the vertical you can *develop* is the vertical inside your deed. The vertical you can *tour* may be larger, for your own party only, with skins and no infrastructure. **Underwrite on deeded vertical alone.** `[E]`
- **Canada is stricter:** in BC any tourism use of Crown land requires a tenure — licence or lease `[P2]` — and there is no "dispersed non-commercial use" carve-out equivalent to the US one that I could verify. `[E]`
- **Chile has the same problem under a different name:** the Futaleufú listing states the highest part of the property adjoins **"cordillera fiscal"** — state land `[P3]`. Check every Chilean parcel for where the *deslinde* (boundary) sits relative to the ridge.
- **New Zealand is the extreme case:** tenure review put the tops in Crown ownership, and the two large NZ ski assets on the market prove it — **Ōhau is 10.1 ha freehold against 145.2 ha of DOC concession** `[P2]`, and **Mt Dobson's freehold is a workshop in Fairlie while the alpine land is a DOC concession** `[P2]`.

### 3.4 Severed mineral estates — the western default, and dominant over the surface

- **The mineral estate is dominant. The surface owner cannot prohibit the mineral owner from accessing and developing the minerals.** `[P1]`
- **Scale of the problem:** BLM estimates it administers **57.2 million acres of federal mineral estate underlying privately owned surface, ~90% of it in the West** `[P1]`. Much of this is the legacy of the **Stock Raising Homestead Act 1916**, which patented the surface to settlers while the United States retained the minerals `[P1]`.
- **Protections are thin.** Federal law gives split-estate surface owners some protection over federal coal and hardrock minerals but **near-zero protection where the federal oil, gas or coalbed methane below has been leased** `[P1]`. Surface owners "often get quite shocked" to find the land can be disturbed and permanently occupied for mineral development `[P2]`.
- **What this means here:** a ski slope is a surface use with no legal priority. A mineral owner could put a pad, a road and a rig across the fall line. `[E]`
- **Due diligence:** run a **full mineral title chain, not a current-owner search**, on every parcel; the severance may be a century old and invisible on the ground `[P2]`. Where minerals were severed, price the risk or negotiate a **surface use agreement / non-disturbance agreement** with the mineral owner before closing. `[E]`
- **Patented mining claims are the useful exception** — they convey surface *and* minerals together `[P2]`, which is another argument for the Leadville/San Juan claim route.

### 3.5 Zoning — does private recreational use of a small rural parcel need a permit?

The answer is county-by-county, and the good news is that at least one high-elevation Colorado county has a zone built for exactly this. `[E]`

- **Lake County, Colorado — Backcountry (BC) Zone District** `[P1]`. The county's own statement: there are **a limited number of possible commercial uses allowed by Conditional Use Permit in the Backcountry Zone District, but it is not the intent of the district to promote any commercial opportunities but rather to accommodate personal use by the property owner.** **Minimum acreage to establish the BC district is 160 acres.** This is close to a bespoke fit: private, personal, non-commercial recreation on a large high parcel is the zone's stated purpose. Lake County is Leadville — base elevations above 3,000 m and the highest inventory of patented claims in Colorado. **This should be a primary search area.** `[E]`
- **Clear Creek County, Colorado (St Mary's) — MR-1 Mountain Residential.** Established "for the purpose of providing for single-family residential and other appropriate uses in mountainous terrain"; newly created parcels must contain at least 2 acres of MR-1 land `[P1]`. **I could not confirm the zoning of the St Mary's parcels, nor whether private recreation is a use by right, a conditional use, or unlisted.** Clear Creek County Planning must be asked directly (303-679-2436) `[P1]`. Unlisted uses in a residential district are the risk: a rope tow and a snowcat operation may read as a "commercial recreation" use requiring a Special/Conditional Use Permit even with no paying public. `[E]`
- **General Colorado pattern:** conditional and special use permits are hearing-based, with applications due weeks ahead of a Planning Commission hearing, and conditions attached that run with the land `[P1]`. Routt and Pitkin counties publish uses-by-zone-district tables; Boulder County's land use code Article 4 sets out permitted uses `[P1]`.
- **Practical guidance:** (1) a **purely private, non-commercial** ski hill with no lift, no fee and no public is the least regulated configuration — which is exactly the client's Phase 1; (2) **any lift, tow, or structure will trigger a building permit and probably a use permit**; (3) get a **written zoning verification letter** from the county naming the intended use before going hard on a deposit. `[E]`
- **Chile:** rural parcels with "rol propio" are separately titled and transferable; the binding constraints are subdivision minima and any *plan regulador* — not researched in this pass. `[E]`
- **New Zealand:** Ōhau's freehold is zoned **Rural S (Scenic)** `[P2]`, and Mt Lyford holds resource consent for 149 further sections `[P2]` — evidence that alpine development consents are obtainable in Hurunui but are project-specific.
