# Japan — Small Freehold Ski-Terrain Parcels (40–200 ha, 300 m+ vertical)

**Prepared:** 2026-08-28
**Brief:** Private ski hill sized to 吉雪滑雪場 / Jixue Ski Area, Jilin, China — approx. 40 ha (400,000 m²), 300 m vertical, 910 m longest run, 19° mean gradient, 6–33° range.
**Absolute condition:** registered freehold (所有権 / *shoyūken*) must include the skiable slope **and** the summit/ridge.
**Buyer:** Malaysian individual, non-resident. Display currency MYR.
**Evidence tags:** [P1] government / law / official statistics · [P2] press · [P3] listing or trade-press information (NOT verified fact) · [E] inference.

---

## 0. Headline conclusion

**No property was found that satisfies all of the brief's conditions simultaneously — i.e. 40–200 ha, ≥300 m vertical, freehold including the ridge, and currently on the open market at a stated price.**

Two properties match the *geometry* extremely well (糸魚川 / Itoigawa's two municipal ski areas) but are **not yet for sale** and their land-vs-facilities transfer terms are undecided. One property has *confirmed freehold* and a stated price (ルーデンス湯沢 / Ludens Yuzawa) but is **under-sized on both area and vertical**, and its listing appears withdrawn.

The binding constraint is **not** price, and it is no longer the 500 ha scarcity or the Agricultural Land Act from the previous round. It is a **structural ownership pattern**: in Japan the ridge tops of snow-reliable mountains are disproportionately national forest (国有林 / *kokuyūrin*) or municipal land, and national forest is **leased, not sold**, for ski use.

## 2. Property notes

### 2.1 シャルマン火打 / Charmant Hiuchi — best geometric fit in Japan
- 94 ha; 500 → 1,009 m; **509 m vertical**; 16 courses; longest run 1,623 m; max 37°; course mix 20/45/35 beginner/intermediate/advanced; ~65 % ungroomed powder terrain. [P3]
- Average gradient over the longest run ≈ **18.2°** [E] — almost exactly the brief's 19° target.
- Land was **acquired by the city** through the 「火打山麓スカイパーク整備事業」 land-purchase and construction programme, 1993–1997. [P3] This is the strongest available indication that the terrain is held in fee by the municipality rather than leased from the national forest.
- Since April 1998 land and buildings have been **owned by Itoigawa City**, with the operator holding a 使用貸借契約 (gratuitous use agreement). [P2]
- The city is studying **private transfer (民間譲渡) from FY2029 onward**, to be worked through during the FY2026–2028 designated-management period. [P2]
- **Open question — decisive:** whether a future transfer conveys the *land* or only the *facilities*. Not yet decided.

### 2.2 糸魚川シーサイドバレー / Itoigawa Seaside Valley — most actionable timing
- 74.5 ha; 240 → 740 m; **500 m vertical**; 6 courses; mix 17/50/33. [P3]
- Same 1998 ownership structure: **land and buildings owned by Itoigawa City**. [P2]
- Council's Construction & Industry Standing Committee (15 December session) set out: concrete procedures from **FY2026**, transfer targeted for completion **by FY2028**, designated-management period extended one year. [P2]
- Operating cost to the city: ~¥90.9 M FY2022 (incl. ¥39 M management fee), ~¥87.53 M FY2023, ~¥80.42 M FY2024. [P2] The city's motive is to stop this bleed — which is leverage for a buyer, but also signals the terrain does not wash its face commercially.
- **Base elevation 240 m is a snow-reliability concern** for the lower third of the hill [E].

### 2.3 ルーデンス湯沢 / Ludens Yuzawa — the only confirmed freehold with a price
- Owner: 株式会社ルーデンス湯沢ファーム. **Dormant since the 2018/19 season.** [P3]
- Listed on 楽待 (Rakumachi): **¥380,000,000**; **土地権利 = 所有権 (freehold)**; land 129,900 m² (12.99 ha); building 6,195.21 m² RC, built November 1978; quoted 26.31 % gross yield on an assumed ¥100 M annual income. [P3] The yield figure is a listing claim on a dormant asset and should be disregarded.
- Ski specs: 450 → 680 m, **230 m vertical**, max 25°, longest run 1,000 m. [P3]
- **¥/ha = ¥29.25 M/ha** — but this bundles a 6,195 m² building, so land-only ¥/ha is lower.
- **Correction to prior research:** the figures "12.99 ha 所有権, ¥380–400 M" previously attributed to **Mt.乗鞍 / Mt. Norikura** in fact belong to **this** property. Mt. Norikura's land is explicitly **rented**, and a transferee "would need a direct contract with the current landowner." [P2] Mt. Norikura therefore **fails the absolute freehold condition**.
- Shortfall vs. brief: 12.99 ha vs. 40 ha; 230 m vs. 300 m vertical; ~13.3° mean gradient over the longest run [E] vs. 19° target.

### 2.4 京極町 / Kyogoku, Hokkaido — speculative
- 山林, 140,805 m² (14.08 ha), 42,588 tsubo, **都市計画区域外** (outside city-planning area), no designated land-use category. Handled by 一般社団法人原野土地再生機構. Described as a **水源地 (water-source land) without water rights**. [P3]
- Kyogoku sits on the eastern flank of 羊蹄山 / Mt. Yōtei, adjacent to Kutchan and Rusutsu — excellent snow. **But:** Mt. Yōtei's upper slopes fall inside 支笏洞爺国立公園 / Shikotsu-Tōya National Park, where the Natural Parks Act restricts construction and earthworks **regardless of private ownership** (see §4.2) [E]. Price and elevation not obtainable.

### 2.5 Precedents that do NOT deliver freehold
- **M's resort Ashibetsu / 旧国設芦別スキー場** (Ashibetsu, Hokkaido): closed March 2020, reopened January 2022 by SUNFLAKE (Sapporo) as **指定管理者 (designated manager)** — a concession, **not ownership**. First season ran without lifts, using **snowmobile uplift**. [P2] Operationally this is precisely the client's intended low-CAPEX model, and proves it works — but it confers no title.
- **ぶどうスキー場 / Budō, Murakami City, Niigata**: abolished March 2025 after 36 years; **facilities transferred free of charge (無償)** from the city to 株式会社シンクファースト (a Tokyo IT company), reopening as ぶどうスノーリゾート for 2025/26. [P2] Again — **facilities**, not land.
- **白馬岩岳 / Hakuba Iwatake** base area: ~2 ha sold to Mitsubishi Estate + Shimizu Corp for **¥1.5 bn** (book value ¥240 M), existing buildings demolished, delivery March 2026. [P2] → **¥750 M/ha** — the ceiling benchmark for prime resort base land.

### Holding cost (OPEX)
- 山林 fixed-asset tax (固定資産税) runs roughly **¥1,000–5,000 per ha per year**, with a wider quoted band of ¥5,000–50,000/ha/yr. [P3]
- For a 40 ha parcel this is on the order of **¥40,000–2,000,000/yr**, most plausibly in the low hundreds of thousands of yen [E]. Where the land is not being managed for timber, tax is essentially the only recurring cost. **This strongly supports the low-OPEX thesis.**

## 4. Structural obstacles to "freehold including summit and ridge"

### 4.1 National forest holds the ridges — and is not for sale
- Japan's forest area is ~25 million ha, two-thirds of national land. **69 % is 民有林** (私有林 58 %, prefectural 5 %, municipal etc. 7 %); **国有林 is ~7.69 million ha = 20 % of national land and 30 % of forest.** [P1 — Forestry Agency]
- Critically, national forest is **"widely distributed across the remote backbone mountain ranges (奥地の脊梁山地) and headwater areas."** [P1] These are exactly the high-vertical, snow-reliable ridges the brief targets.
- The Forestry Agency **does** publish 国有地の売払い情報, but disposal is **prioritised for public/public-use purposes**, and the assets actually offered are **surplus properties such as nurseries (苗畑) and log yards (土場)** — not ridge terrain. [P1]
- For ski use, the route is **貸付 (lease)**, not purchase: national forest land is expressly leased for ski-slope sites, with annual rent computed from the per-m² inheritance-tax assessed value × a rate that differs for commercial vs. non-profit use. [P1]
- **[E] Conclusion:** where a Japanese mountain's summit is national forest, the client's absolute condition is **unachievable at any price**. Screening must begin from the cadastre/ownership map, not from the ski geometry.

### 4.2 Natural Parks Act binds private land too
- Parks are zoned: 特別保護地区 (Special Protection) → 特別地域 1st / 2nd / 3rd type → 普通地域 (Ordinary). Permission is required in special areas for building construction and earthworks; **1st-type special areas and special protection districts are managed to preserve the status quo**. Ordinary areas need only notification, but large-scale acts require prior notification. [P1]
- **These restrictions apply regardless of whether the land is private or public.** [P1] Buying freehold inside a national park does **not** buy the right to cut a piste, erect a rope tow, or build a lift.
- Most of the brief's target regions (Daisetsuzan, Shikotsu-Tōya, Hakkōda, Zaō, Chōkai, Gassan, Myōkō-Togakushi, Jōshin'etsukōgen, Chūbu-Sangaku incl. Norikura) sit inside national or quasi-national parks [E].

### 4.3 Forest development permit (林地開発許可)
- In 民有林 covered by a regional forest plan, any 開発行為 (earthworks, tree-root excavation, clearing, change of land form) **exceeding 1 ha requires the prefectural governor's permission**. **Ski-resort construction (スキー場の造成) is expressly listed** as a purpose caught by this regime. [P1]
- **[E]** Grooming existing cleared terrain and running snowcats does not trigger it; cutting new pistes over 1 ha does.

### 4.4 保安林 (protection forest)
- Protection forest is "particularly difficult to obtain development permission for," and **de-designation is in principle not possible**. The exception — "public-interest reasons" — is however broad and **expressly includes ski resorts**, alongside roads, golf courses, housing, factories, power plants and quarries. [P1]
- **[E]** So 保安林 is a serious but not automatically fatal encumbrance. Confirm designation before offer; assume 12–24 months and specialist counsel if release is needed.

## 5. Section E — risks specific to a *small* parcel

### 5.1 Road frontage and access (接道 / 囲繞地通行権) — the sharpest risk
- A landlocked parcel (袋地) has a statutory right of passage over surrounding land to reach a public road under **Civil Code Art. 210 (囲繞地通行権)**, and neighbours **cannot refuse** it. [P1]
- **But three qualifications gut it for ski operations:** (a) passage is confined to the route causing least damage to the burdened land; (b) **vehicular passage is not automatically guaranteed**; (c) a passage payment (償金) is generally owed. [P1]
- **[E] Decisive implication:** a 40 ha landlocked parcel may yield only a *walking* right. A snowcat, piste basher, fuel truck or materials lorry is exactly what is *not* guaranteed. **Any offer must be conditional on either (i) direct frontage onto a public road, or (ii) a registered 通行地役権 (easement of way) expressly permitting tracked and heavy vehicles year-round.** A bare 囲繞地通行権 is not sufficient security for this business.

### 5.2 Winter access and snow clearing — decisive for a non-resident owner
- Municipalities **do not clear 私道 (private roads)**: roads owned by individuals or several parties are not municipal-managed and cannot be cleared by the city. For 生活道路 (private living roads), **clearing is in principle the beneficiaries' own responsibility**, and most municipalities operate on beneficiary-pays. [P1 — municipal sources: Shinjō, Kitakami, Higashine, Sanjō]
- Roads that are practically impossible to clear: **dead-ends with nowhere to push snow**, roads too narrow for the plough, and roads where frontagers will not cooperate. [P1]
- For mountain villas, **because the owner does not actually live there and is not resident-registered, the municipality does not plough up to the property.** [P3]
- **[E] Implication:** a non-resident Malaysian owner of a private mountain parcel should budget for **self-performed winter access** — a dedicated plough/snowblower or a contracted local operator — from day one. On the low-CAPEX model this is not optional overhead; it is the thing that determines whether the hill is usable at all. It also argues for buying terrain whose access road is an existing **市町村道 (municipal road)** already on a ploughing route. Verify the road's legal classification, not just its appearance.

### 5.3 Undetermined boundaries (境界未確定)
- National cadastral survey (地籍調査) progress is **53 %** overall, but only **47 % for forest land (林地)**, and was **44 % in mountain-village areas**. **Over 100,000 km² of forest has never been cadastrally surveyed.** [P1 — MLIT / 地籍調査Webサイト]
- Causes: poor accuracy of the legacy 公図 (cadastral maps) and the difficulty of getting ageing owners to attend on-site boundary walks. [P1]
- **[E]** A 40 ha parcel is just as exposed as a 500 ha one. Registered area (公簿面積) may diverge materially from true area (縄伸び/縄縮み). **Buy on a surveyed basis (実測売買) with a boundary confirmation, or price the risk in.** Where the uphill neighbour is the national forest, boundary confirmation requires the Forest Management Office as a party.

### 5.4 Neighbours above and below
- **[E]** If the parcel's upper boundary abuts 国有林 or 保安林, there is no route to extend upward — the vertical drop is permanently capped at what is inside the title. This is the single most important thing to check *before* valuing a parcel on its vertical.
- **[E]** The downhill neighbour can log or develop. On a narrow parcel shaped for fall-line skiing, a clear-fell below the base can alter runout, drainage and access. Consider buying, or optioning, the run-out.

### 5.5 Foreign-buyer regulatory risk — actively tightening
- **森林法 第10条の7の2:** anyone acquiring forest land must notify the municipal mayor **within 90 days**, **regardless of area**; applies to purchase, inheritance, gift, bequest, exchange, and corporate division/merger. Those who have already notified under the 国土利用計画法 are exempt. [P1]
- **Nationality is now a reporting item:** the acquirer's nationality was added from **1 July 2024**; for corporate acquirers, the nationality of the representative from **1 April 2024**; the notification form was revised again from **1 April 2025**. [P1]
- **[E]** A 40 ha purchase will also exceed the 国土利用計画法 post-transaction notification threshold (10,000 m² outside city-planning areas) — expect that filing too.
- **Live rulemaking:** the Cabinet Secretariat convened the **外国人による土地取得等のルールの在り方検討会** (Study Group on Rules for Land Acquisition by Foreign Nationals), **first meeting 4 March 2026**. It is to compile a **basic policy in summer 2026** and draft a legislative outline. Members include a former National Security Secretariat head. [P1/P2]
- The declared focus is **conservation of water-source areas and forests (水源地・森林保全)** plus land near defence facilities — squarely the asset class in this brief — and includes countermeasures against "hidden acquisition" through paper companies, plus disclosure of the nationality of corporate officers and voting shareholders. [P2]
- A counterweight: confining restrictions to foreigners may conflict with WTO **GATS national-treatment (内外無差別)** obligations, which is itself an open question in the study group. [P2]
- **[E] Assessment:** freehold acquisition by a foreign individual remains **legal and unrestricted today**, but this is the *least* favourable moment in recent memory to be a foreign buyer of Japanese forest and water-source land. As of the report date the summer-2026 policy statement had not been confirmed as published in the sources retrieved. **Re-check before committing.** Expect, at minimum, more disclosure; possibly notification-with-screening for forest and headwater land.

## 6. Method note and limitations

- **WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for every specialist property domain attempted** — yamaichiba.com, happiness-wood-bank.com, blog.skibumpslabo.com. Research therefore relied on search-result summaries.
- The Japanese property portals named in the brief (山いちば, 家いちば, 山林ネット, ハピネスウッドバンク, 林地ドットコム, 不動産連合隊, athome, LIFULL HOME'S, ジモティー, 楽待, 健美家) returned **category and index pages only**; the search index did not surface individual large-parcel listing pages with area, price and elevation together. Repeated numeric and phrase-targeted queries did not break through this.
- **Consequence:** the absence of a qualifying 40–200 ha listing in this report is **not** evidence that none exists. It reflects a genuine tooling limit. Sections 3, 4 and 5 (price bands, tenure structure, legal constraints, small-parcel risks) are robust and are the durable part of this work; Section 1 is an incomplete market snapshot.
- **Recommended next step:** these portals require direct browsing or an on-the-ground buying agent. A Japanese-speaking forest-land broker (山林売買専門) briefed on the *shape* criteria — fall-line-elongated parcel, freehold to the ridge, existing municipal-road frontage — will out-perform any amount of remote search.
