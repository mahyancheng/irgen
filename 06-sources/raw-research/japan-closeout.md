## 1.1 The structural finding from the article

`[P2]/[P3]` Two statements recur across the search abstracts of archives/9403 and its sibling posts:

1. **More than ten Japanese ski areas effectively cease operating every year**, and among the
   **privately owned** ones, many owners are actively considering transfer.
2. **Sellers have begun listing ski areas on public real-estate and M&A portals.** The article frames
   this as a change of practice — historically these were **水面下の交渉 (below-the-waterline
   negotiations)** conducted privately between known parties.

`[E]` This second point is the operationally important one for a foreign buyer. A market that has
moved from private introductions to open portals is one a non-resident can actually enter without a
Japanese sponsor. It is also a **buyer's market signal**: supply is being pushed onto public
channels because the private channel is not clearing it.

## 4.1 `[P1]` Former ski-area sites are an established **national-forest lease** category

From the 林野庁 (Forestry Agency) national-forest utilisation pages: for **solar generation sites**,
the Agency leases **land that does not involve new forest felling** — and it names the categories
explicitly:

> **スキー場跡地 (former ski-area sites)**, ゴルフ場跡地 (former golf courses), 苗畑跡地 (former
> nurseries), ダム建設資材置き場跡地 (former dam-construction material yards).

`[E]` **Two implications, and they cut in opposite directions.**
- **Positive:** it confirms that **cleared, stumped, graded former piste inside national forest is
  obtainable — by lease.** The Forestry Agency is demonstrably willing to lease ex-ski-area land for
  a **non-forestry commercial use**. That is the single strongest support found for the "freehold
  base + national-forest lease above" structure.
- **Negative:** it also tells you **where a lot of the ex-piste inventory actually sits.** If the
  cleared runs are inside 国有林, they are **not purchasable**, and the ex-ski-area land market is
  thinner than a naive count of 200+ closed areas would suggest.

## 5.3 `[E]` Structural read on why ≥500 ha is not appearing

Japan's private forest is **extraordinarily fragmented**. The 758万 ha of 国有林 (≈20% of the
national land area, ≈30% of forest) sits precisely in the **奥地急峻な山地** — the remote steep

small-parcel, multi-heir and often with unlocatable owners (所有者不明土地). **A single 500 ha private
title with real vertical is, on the evidence assembled, close to non-existent in the open market.**
The realistic Japanese structure is **assembly of adjoining parcels**, or **freehold base + leased
upper mountain** (see §8).

# 7. IS A PRIVATE ROPEWAY (専用索道) OUTSIDE THE RAILWAY BUSINESS ACT? — **MATERIAL PROGRESS; NOT CLOSED**

This was flagged as the most important open legal question in the Japanese file. **I advanced it
significantly but did not close it.** Here is exactly what is now established and what is not.

## 7.1 `[P1]` The trigger is commercial character, and it is written into the definition

**鉄道事業法 (Railway Business Act, Act No. 92 of 4 Dec 1986), Art. 2** defines:

> **索道事業** = 「**他人の需要に応じ**、索道により旅客又は貨物を運送する事業」
> — *a business transporting passengers or freight by ropeway **in response to the demand of others**.*

**Art. 32** — confirmed this session:

> A person intending to operate a 索道事業 must obtain the **permission (許可) of the Minister of
> Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism for each ropeway**, **except for ropeways prescribed by
> MLIT ordinance (国土交通省令で定める索道を除く)**.

`[P1]` Classification into **普通索道** (closed carrier with doors — gondola, aerial tramway) and
**特殊索道** (open chair-type carrier — chairlift) is made by the **鉄道事業法施行規則** (Enforcement
Ordinance), not by the Act.

## 7.2 `[P1]` The Act's structure — and the analogy that does *not* hold

The Act is organised into chapters covering **鉄道事業 (railway business)**, **索道事業 (ropeway
business)**, and — **第四章 (Chapter 4) 専用鉄道 (dedicated railway)**.

**専用鉄道 is defined as:** *a railway established exclusively for one's own use, **whose track is
connected to a track used for a railway business***.

`[E]` **This is the most useful single fact recovered this session, and it cuts in the client's
favour.** The statute's own theory of when private, own-use transport infrastructure gets pulled
back into regulation is **physical connection to the commercial network** — a dedicated railway is
regulated because its rolling stock can run onto a public line. **A ski tow on a private mountain
connects to nothing.** There is no ropeway analogue of the network-connection trigger, which is
consistent with a private ropeway sitting outside the licensing regime.

## 7.3 ⚠️ A genuine conflict between my own two sources — flagged, not smoothed over

- **One search abstract stated** that the Act defines **専用索道 = 「自己の用に供するため設置する索道」**
  (*a ropeway established exclusively for one's own use*), and that **a person establishing one must
  maintain and manage the facilities in accordance with MLIT technical standards.**
- **A second, more targeted search for that exact phrase in Art. 2 could not locate it**, and returned
  **only 専用鉄道**.

`[E]` **I cannot reconcile these from search abstracts alone, and I will not guess.** The two
possibilities have materially different consequences:

| If… | Then… |
|---|---|
| **専用索道 is a live statutory category** | A private tow is **outside the 許可 (permission) regime** but **inside a technical-standards / maintenance duty**, and probably a **届出 (notification)**. Buildable without a licence; not unregulated. |
| **専用索道 exists only in the pre-1987 索道規則 and was not carried into the Act** | A purely private, non-commercial tow may be **outside the Railway Business Act altogether** — but would then need checking against the **労働安全衛生法 (Industrial Safety and Health Act)** and the **建築基準法 (Building Standards Act)** instead. |

## 7.4 ⛔ Still unresolved

1. **The content of the MLIT-ordinance exemption list under Art. 32.** This is *the* provision that
   answers the question and it was not retrieved.
2. **Whether 専用索道 survives in the current Act**, and if so under which article and with what duties.
3. **The paid-lodging question — the actual commercial trap.** Whether bundling lift access into a
   paid stay constitutes 「他人の需要に応じ」. **No source was found either way.** `[E]` My expectation
   is unchanged and remains adverse: **a paid guest riding a lift is very likely "the demand of
   others" whatever the invoice says**, converting the installation into a 索道事業 requiring full
   ministerial permission, a 索道技術管理者, a 安全統括管理者, annual statutory inspection and a
   published 索道安全報告書.
4. **Building Standards Act treatment.** A dedicated search returned **only indoor lift and elevator
   law** (昇降機 under 労働安全衛生法 + 建築基準法). **No evidence was found that an outdoor rope tow is
   a regulated 工作物** — but absence of evidence here is not evidence of absence, and this was one
   search, not a proper enquiry.

## 7.5 `[E]` What this means for the decision, stated plainly

**The snowcat-only Phase 1 remains correct and is now better supported, not worse.** The regulatory
discontinuity between "no ropeway" and "one ropeway" is real and large, and the two most dangerous
unknowns — the exemption list and the paid-guest question — **both sit on the lift side of that
line.** Nothing here changes the recommendation. **Do not underwrite any plan whose returns depend on
installing a lift until a 地方運輸局 鉄道部 has answered §7.4(1) and (3) in writing.** Both are free
enquiries.

# 8. NATIONAL FOREST LEASING (国有林野の貸付) — **ADVANCED; THE FOREIGN-ELIGIBILITY QUESTION REMAINS OPEN**

## 8.1 `[P1]` What is now established

**国有林野の管理経営に関する法律 (Act on the Administration and Management of National Forest, 1951)**:

> National forest land used for forest management **may, to the extent that it does not impede that
> use or purpose, be leased or made available for use by contract** (「その用途又は目的を妨げない限度
> において、**契約により、貸し付け、又は使用させることができる**」).

**Three operational facts confirmed this session:**

1. **Leases are term-limited and renewed on application.** On expiry, continued use requires filing a
   **国有林野貸付等更新申請書 (national forest lease renewal application)**. `[P1]` — This confirms
   renewability, which is the structurally important property for a long-hold thesis, even though the
   term length itself was not obtained.
2. **Former ski-area sites are an established leasing category** (§4.1). The Forestry Agency
   demonstrably leases ex-piste national forest for **non-forestry commercial use**. `[P1]`
3. **MAFF publishes sector-specific lease procedure manuals.** The **風力発電・地熱発電に係る国有林野の
   貸付け等手続マニュアル** (wind and geothermal, 令和3年9月, amended 令和4年3月 and **令和7年3月**)
   is the model. `[P1]` `[E]` **This is the right document to obtain.** There is no ski-specific
   manual, but the wind/geothermal manual will state the **term structure, the rent-calculation
   methodology and the applicant-eligibility test** that the Forestry Agency applies to a commercial
   lessee — which is very likely the same framework applied to a ski lessee.

## 8.2 ⛔ The question the brief called decisive — **NOT ANSWERED**

> *"If a long-term lease is available to a foreign-invested company, the Japanese answer becomes
> 'freehold at the base, national-forest lease above' — and that is a major finding."*

**The query `国有林野 貸付 外国法人 外国資本 制限 申請資格 借受人 要件` was the query that hit the
exhausted budget. It never executed.** I have **no evidence either way** on foreign eligibility, and
I decline to infer one — the answer is genuinely uncertain and the stakes are high.

`[E]` What can be said: **nothing found in this or the previous session indicates a nationality test
on national-forest leasing.** The statutory language is a plain contractual power with no nationality
qualifier, and Japan applies no nationality test to freehold acquisition. **But** the 2026 policy
environment is moving — the Cabinet Secretariat panel is due to publish its framework in **summer
2026**, and nationality declaration at registration is slated for **FY2026** — and **state-land
disposal to foreign capital is exactly the kind of item such a review picks up.** Treat foreign
eligibility as **open and politically live**, not as a technicality.

## 8.3 How to close it — three free enquiries

1. Request the **風力発電・地熱発電 貸付け等手続マニュアル** (令和7年3月改正) and read the term, rent
   formula and eligibility sections.
2. Write to the relevant **森林管理局** — 北海道 / 東北 / 関東信越 — asking directly: 外国法人・外国資本
   の法人は国有林野の貸付けを受けられるか、契約期間と貸付料の算定方法は。
3. Ask any existing **ski area operating on leased national forest** what its term and renewal history
   is. This is the fastest route to a real-world answer, and operators are generally willing to say.
