### 1.2 What this means for the buyer

- **This is a distressed-asset municipal bail-out, not a municipal development.** The city did not assemble the land; it absorbed a failed private resort in 1998 to keep it open. That is materially different from the assumption in the brief that this was a 1980 municipal project.
- **It was the OLD Itoigawa City (pre-merger), not 能生町 (Nou Town).** The 2005 merger combined 糸魚川市 + 能生町 + 青海町. Seaside Valley sits at Ōaza Yamaguchi in the old Itoigawa City area (the 根知谷 / Nechi valley side). The Nou Town project was **Charmant Hiuchi**, opened December 1998 — i.e. Nou Town built its own ski area at the very moment Seaside Valley was failing. Nou Town's decision to build Charmant is explicitly cited as one reason Seaside Valley's owner quit. The two resorts are competitors that a 2005 merger placed inside one balance sheet. **[P3]**

### 2.2 Foreign eligibility — the strongest positive finding

The city, describing the target transferee, stated it **"also has the intention of considering companies with foreign capital"** — 「海外資本の企業も視野に入れたい考えもある」. **[P2]**

This is unusually explicit. Most Japanese municipal resort disposals are silent on the point and then quietly weight local-employment criteria. Itoigawa volunteering it suggests (a) the city expects a thin domestic bidder field, and (b) staff have already prepared the ground politically. It is a *staff* statement, not a council resolution — see the Tsunan risk in §2.4.

No Japanese statute restricts land acquisition by foreign nationals, foreign corporations, or non-residents. Constraints are procedural and post-hoc, not prohibitive — see §8.

### 2.4 Precedent analysis — and two corrections to the brief

**Correction 1 — Murakami City's Budo Ski Area (村上市ぶどうスキー場) was NOT a free transfer.**
On 30 Sep 2025 the Murakami City Council **unanimously** approved a **無償貸与 (mushō taiyo / free LOAN)** of the city-owned facilities and equipment to **Thinkfirst Inc. (シンクファースト)**, a Tokyo IT company. Contract signed October 2025; resort reopened as ぶどうスノーリゾート (Budo Snow Resort) targeting 20 Dec 2025, with year-round camping use planned. **The city retained ownership.** The brief's premise of 無償譲渡 (free conveyance) is wrong. **[P2]**

*Why this matters:* the structure a Niigata municipality reaches for first is a free loan — it preserves council control, avoids a land-disposal vote, and lets the city claw the asset back if the operator fails. A buyer whose absolute condition is *ownership of the slopes and summit* must expect to be offered a lease and must refuse it.

**Correction 2 — New Greenpia Tsunan (ニュー・グリーンピア津南) collapsed, and it is directly on point.**

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| — | Tsunan Town signs a 基本協定書 (basic agreement) with **イントランス / INTRANCE** (Tokyo, hotel operator) as preferred negotiator; sale contract targeted for March 2026 |
| — | A residents' group petitions the council to scrutinise the two purchase proposals |
| **13 Mar 2026** | On the final day of the regular session, the **town council passes an advisory resolution (勧告決議) by majority opposing the sale** and demanding the town cancel the basic agreement. Stated grounds: **"almost the entire area is a water-source recharge zone" (「ほぼ全域が水源涵養地」)** and the sheer size of the land |
| Early Apr 2026 | Town indicates it will cancel the agreement. Sale selection **back to square one (白紙)**. Mayor **桑原悠 (Kuwahara Yū)** simultaneously announces she will **not** seek re-election |
| May 2026 | Council submits a formal reply **accepting a private sale in principle but limiting the sale area to roughly 60–70 ha** (hotel building site, car park, etc.), with **water abstraction subject to prior consultation** |
| 20 May 2026 | Mayor states at a press conference that resolving it within her term is unrealistic; the sale should be executed under the next mayor |

**[P2]** — Niigata Nippo and Tokamachi Shinbun.

### 2.5 How Tsunan changes the Itoigawa calculus

1. **A Niigata council can kill a signed basic agreement at the eleventh hour.** Staff-level enthusiasm — including Itoigawa's openness to foreign capital — is not a decision. The binding vote is a council 議決 on the disposal of public property.
2. **The objection that worked was water, not xenophobia.** The council's stated ground was 水源涵養 (water-source recharge). Seaside Valley is ~74.5 ha of mountain forest above a valley; the identical objection is available to any Itoigawa councillor. Under the **新潟県水源地域の保全に関する条例**, roughly **97% of privately-owned forest in Niigata** is inside a designated water-source area (§8.3). This objection is pre-loaded.
3. **The council's counter-offer was a scope cut, not a veto.** Tsunan's council ultimately accepted a sale confined to the *built* footprint. Expect Itoigawa to land in the same place: base area, car park, lodge — with the mountain retained or leased. **That is precisely the opposite of what this buyer requires.**
4. **Timing risk is political, not procedural.** Tsunan's process died into a mayoral transition. Itoigawa's FY2026–FY2028 window spans a mayoral term boundary; verify Itoigawa's next mayoral election date before assuming the window holds. 未確認.
5. **The one genuine asymmetry in Itoigawa's favour:** the city is running an *open public solicitation* (公募) from the outset, whereas Tsunan negotiated bilaterally with a preferred party and was accused of opacity. A properly noticed 公募 with published criteria is far harder for a council to overturn.

### 2.6 Positioning in the city's asset plans

- **糸魚川市公共施設等総合管理指針 / 個別計画** (Public Facilities Comprehensive Management Guideline / individual facility plans) covers the ski areas. Recorded findings: nationwide decline in the skiing population, **no expected recovery in lift revenue**, rising maintenance cost from ageing, and *"large costs are expected for renovation of the ropeways (索道), lodging facilities, and dining facilities."* **[P1]**
- **FY2022 study: 「糸魚川市スキー場管理活用等調査」** (Itoigawa City Ski Area Management & Utilisation Survey), carried out by a private consultancy. Verdict: both resorts loss-making; the city spends **~¥200 million per year combined**; the resorts are **"low in competitiveness and structurally dependent on subsidy from the city"** (「競争力が低く、市からの補助に依存した体制」). The plan directed a continue-or-close decision by **FY2025**, when both designated management periods expired. The FY2026 disposal decision is the output of that process. **[P1]/[P2]**
- Also referenced: 糸魚川市過疎地域持続的発展計画 (Depopulated Area Sustainable Development Plan, FY2021–FY2025), which frames the ski areas as core regional-industry infrastructure — a source of political resistance to closure, and of subsidy eligibility for a buyer who keeps them open. **[P1]**

### 3.3 **Correction: "max gradient 20°" is wrong**

The brief's figure of 最大斜度20度 is an aggregator artefact — it is the *average* gradient of the steepest course. The true maximum is **38°** on the upper pitch of 第2ゲレンデ, described as ungroomed powder ("最大斜度38°の急斜面は未圧雪のパウダーが楽しめます"), with a 300 m tree-lined bypass for skiers who cannot handle it. 第3ゲレンデ averages 21°. **[P3], multiple independent listings**

**This is a material and favourable correction.** A 38° pitch with a 500 m vertical is genuinely steep terrain, not a nursery hill. For a snowcat / ski-touring private-use model this is a real mountain.

### 3.4 Aspect and outlook

- Courses **fall toward the Sea of Japan** — "海に向かって滑り降りるコース" (the run that descends toward the sea satisfies even advanced skiers). Combined with unobstructed sea views from the top, the main fall line is broadly **north / north-northwest**. **[E, inferred — direct aspect data 未確認]**
- A north aspect at this latitude is the single most favourable terrain attribute the asset has, and it partly offsets the low base.
- From the summit: 360° panorama — Sea of Japan, **佐渡島 (Sado Island)**, **能登半島 (Noto Peninsula)**; inland, the **白馬連峰 (Hakuba range)** of 中部山岳国立公園 (Chūbu-Sangaku National Park); nearer peaks 駒ヶ岳 (Komagatake) and 雨飾山 (Amakazari-yama). **[P3]**
- **Name of the summit peak: 未確認.** No source names a peak at 740 m. The high point sits on a ridge called 明星尾根 (Myōjō-one / Myōjō Ridge). **[E]** The buyer's absolute condition that ownership include "the summit" is therefore not verifiable here — the 740 m top is a ridge shoulder on a larger massif, and whether the parcel boundary reaches even that shoulder is unknown.

### 4.2 Operating record — the real snow-reliability evidence

**FY2019 (2019–20 season): the resort could not open at all.**
Itoigawa City's own tourism statistics (糸魚川市観光統計) state: 「令和元年度は少雪により、シーサイドバレースキー場が開業できず、入込数が大幅に減少しました」 — *"in FY2019, owing to light snow, Seaside Valley Ski Resort could not open, and arrivals fell sharply."* City-wide tourist arrivals that year were 2,177,480 (96.2% YoY; −85,540 against FY2018's 2,263,020). **[P1]**

For context, 2019–20 was Japan's historic warm winter: Yuzawa-area resorts saw skier visits fall ~33% and the national figure hit a record low. Seaside Valley did not merely have a short season — **it had no season.** No other Itoigawa resort is recorded as having failed to open.

**2025–26 season (most recent) — direct comparison within the same city:**

| Resort | Base elevation | Opened | Closed | Approx. days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Seaside Valley** | **240 m** | **29 Dec 2025** | **22 Mar 2026** | **~84** |
| **Charmant Hiuchi** | **500 m** | **19 Dec 2025** | **5 May 2026** | **~138** |

**[P3]** — Itoigawa Tourism Guide, 2025–26 season opening information.

The two resorts are ~20 km apart under the same weather systems. The **54-day gap is almost entirely an elevation effect.** Seaside Valley opened 10 days later and closed **six weeks** earlier.

**Other season markers:** a normal-year target opening of **18 Dec**; a season-end of **20 Mar** in another year. Typical season therefore mid/late-December to late March. A base-area snow depth of **150 cm** appears in reporting. **[P3]**

### 4.3 Assessment for the buyer

- The lower third of the mountain (240–400 m) is the marginal zone and it is where the entire base infrastructure sits.
- **No evidence of snowmaking (人工降雪機) was found** (§5.3). A 240 m base in a warming climate without snowmaking has no mitigation.
- For the buyer's *private* use case (snowcat, snowmobile, ski touring) the constraint is different but not eliminated: the upper mountain will hold snow well, but **vehicle access to the top depends on the bottom being snow-covered or the access road being driveable to a snowline start**. In a FY2019-type winter there is neither a season nor a snow road.
- **[E] Twenty-year outlook:** Japanese climate projections consistently show Sea-of-Japan-side low-elevation snowfall declining fastest, with the reliable snow line rising. A 240 m base is on the wrong side of that line. Treat the bottom third as a wasting asset and value the property on the 450–740 m band only.

## 8. Regulatory position

### 8.1 自然公園法 (Natural Parks Act)

- Itoigawa City contains **three Niigata prefectural natural parks (新潟県立自然公園)**, including **白馬山麓県立自然公園 (Hakuba Sanroku Prefectural Natural Park)** and **久比岐県立自然公園 (Kubiki Prefectural Natural Park)**. Niigata has 13 prefectural natural parks totalling 128,580 ha. **[P1]**
- **Whether the ski area lies inside a park boundary, and if so its zone classification (特別地域 1種/2種/3種 or 普通地域), is 未確認.**
- **[E]** Given the site's position on the 白馬 (Hakuba) foothills at Ōaza Yamaguchi, **白馬山麓県立自然公園 is the plausible candidate** and this must be checked before any development is contemplated. Verify against 国土数値情報 自然公園地域データ (MLIT National Land Numerical Information, dataset A10) or with 新潟県 環境対策課.
- Consequence if inside a 特別地域: construction, tree felling, and land-form alteration require the governor's permission. For a buyer intending *no* development this is tolerable; for one intending lodges or new lifts it is decisive.

### 8.2 保安林 (protection forest) and 林地開発許可 (forest land development permit)

- **保安林 designation over the site: 未確認.**
- **林地開発許可 history: 未確認.** The 1974–80 development predates or coincides with the modern permit regime; original permit files would sit with the prefecture.
- Framework **[P1]**: under 森林法 (Forest Act), alteration of forest land **exceeding 1 ha** requires the **prefectural governor's permission**. 保安林 is subject to a separate and stricter regime (felling and land-form change need permission; de-designation 解除 is difficult).
- Competent authority: **新潟県 糸魚川地域振興局 農林振興部**, 糸魚川市南押上1-15-1.

### 8.3 新潟県水源地域の保全に関する条例 — the ordinance that actually bites

**Niigata Prefecture Water Source Area Conservation Ordinance**, prior-notification system in force since **1 July 2014**. **[P1]**

- The **landowner must notify the prefecture 30 days BEFORE concluding** any contract transferring ownership of, or creating rights over, forest land within a designated water-source area.
- **Designated areas = privately-owned forest in zones assessed as high or medium water-source conservation function ≈ 97% of all privately-owned forest in Niigata.**
- Sanctions for failure or false notification: recommendation (勧告), **public disclosure of the party's name (公表)**, and fines.
- Separately, **森林法 §10-7-2** requires a new forest-land owner to notify the municipality **within 90 days** of acquisition.

**Two consequences for this buyer:**
1. A forest-land purchase here is **near-certain** to trigger the 30-day prior notification, and the buyer's identity becomes a matter of prefectural record before completion.
2. **This is the exact legal hook the Tsunan council used.** "水源涵養地" was their stated ground for killing the INTRANCE sale. Assume an Itoigawa councillor will reach for the same argument, and prepare a water-management commitment (no abstraction, no alteration of catchment, prior consultation) **before** it is demanded.

### 8.4 重要土地等調査法 (Act on Regulation of Land Use around Important Facilities, 2021)

- Designated 注視区域 / 特別注視区域 in **Niigata Prefecture** are **柏崎市・刈羽郡刈羽村** (Kashiwazaki / Kariwa — nuclear plant) and **新発田市** (Shibata — JGSDF camp). **Itoigawa City is not listed.** **[P1]**
- **[E] Conclusion: the site is very probably outside any 注視区域**, so no pre-transaction notification under this Act, and no scrutiny of the purchaser's nationality on national-security grounds. The resort's inland position ~10+ km from the coast supports this.
- **Verify** at the Cabinet Office Niigata page (`cao.go.jp/tochi-chosa/kuiki/`) and the official viewer `resum2.go.jp` — designations are extended periodically.

### 8.5 Foreign ownership generally

No Japanese statute prohibits acquisition of land by foreign nationals, foreign corporations, or non-residents. Obligations are notification-type and post-hoc: 国土利用計画法 (National Land Use Planning Act) post-transaction notification above area thresholds; 外国為替及び外国貿易法 (FEFTA) reporting for certain inward investment; the prefectural water-source ordinance above; 森林法 §10-7-2. **A Malaysian individual can hold this land in his own name.** The obstacles here are political and procedural, not statutory. **[E]**

### 9.2 土砂災害警戒区域 / 雪崩危険箇所 (landslide alert zones / avalanche hazard sites)

- **[E]** A 500 m-vertical, 38°-max, north-facing slope in one of Japan's snowiest prefectures will almost certainly contain mapped avalanche terrain. For a buyer intending snowcat and ski-touring use, this is a **life-safety and liability** matter, not a paperwork item.

### 9.3 Asbestos, seismic, ageing
- **未確認** — no survey data published.
- **[E]** Base facilities date from **1980**, the hotel from **December 1982**. Japan's revised seismic code (新耐震基準) took effect June 1981 — **the 1980 base buildings pre-date it**; the 1982 hotel probably post-dates it in design. Asbestos was not fully banned in Japan until 2006, so 1980–82 structures are prime candidates for ACM in sprayed fireproofing, boiler and pipe lagging, and roofing sheet. Under 大気汚染防止法 (Air Pollution Control Act) as amended, a **pre-demolition asbestos survey is mandatory** and must be reported. Budget for survey and abatement before assuming any building can be cheaply demolished.

### 9.4 Removal / restoration obligation (撤去・原状回復義務)
- **未確認** whether the transfer terms will impose one.
- **[E]** This is the most likely poison pill. Five ageing lift lines plus a 1982 hotel constitute a demolition liability the city has every incentive to transfer. A "free" conveyance carrying a removal covenant is a **negative-value** asset. Insist on either (a) the city retaining and removing surplus lifts pre-transfer, or (b) an explicit written release from restoration obligations.

### 9.5 Structural / governance risks
- **The operator is a third-sector company ≥50% city-owned.** Its future — wound up, sold with the asset, or left in place — is unaddressed in anything published. There may be employees, contracts, and accumulated losses attached. **未確認.**
- **Competitor inside the same balance sheet.** The city owns Charmant Hiuchi too and will not dispose of it until FY2029+. A Seaside Valley buyer competes for three-plus years against a city-subsidised, snow-superior neighbour 20 km away.
- **Council veto risk** — see §2.4/2.5.

### 10.3 Verdict

**On asset quality against this buyer's thesis, Charmant Hiuchi is clearly the better mountain.** Twice the base elevation, a 54-day-longer proven season, two fewer lift lines to maintain and eventually remove, and a slope inventory (65% ungroomed, 35% advanced, north-facing) that is exactly what a snowcat / snowmobile / ski-touring private operation wants. Seaside Valley's advantages — access, marginally smaller size, a slightly smaller inherited deficit — do not offset a documented failure to open at all in a warm winter.

**On availability, only Seaside Valley is in play**, and by three or more years. Charmant's disposal is FY2029+, with a 3-year designated management term running to FY2028 that the city has deliberately left intact.

**The strategic implication is not "which one to buy" but "how to bid".** Seaside Valley at 74.5 ha with 5 lifts, a 1982 hotel, an onsen and a restaurant is roughly double the buyer's target size and carries a heavy legacy-capex and removal liability. The rational bid is a **carve-out: the upper mountain (roughly 450–740 m), lift-free or with a single retained lift, with the base-area buildings and surplus lift lines retained and removed by the city.**

But note the tension. **Tsunan's council forced the opposite carve-out** — it insisted the sale be limited to the *built* footprint (~60–70 ha of hotel site and car park) and that the *mountain and its water catchment* stay in public hands. A buyer asking Itoigawa for the mountain and not the buildings is asking for precisely what the neighbouring council refused. **[E] Expect this to be the central negotiation, and expect the water-source argument (§8.3) to be deployed against it.**

**Recommended sequence:**
1. Order title extracts (登記事項証明書) and cadastral maps (公図) for Ōaza Yamaguchi — settles §1.3 cheaply and without alerting anyone.
2. FOI the 公有財産台帳 and any land leases.
3. Ask 糸魚川市 商工観光課 directly, in writing: *is land in scope; paid or free; will restoration obligations attach; will a partial-area bid be entertained.* Their volunteered openness to foreign capital suggests they will answer.
4. Retrieve the 第3セクターの経営状況 and 観光統計 series for the real financial and skier-day history.
5. Check 白馬山麓県立自然公園 boundaries, 保安林 status, and the 糸魚川地域振興局's 土砂災害警戒区域 and なだれ危険箇所 maps.
6. Do not bid before the council's disposition is visible. Tsunan proves a signed basic agreement is not a deal in Niigata.
