# Acquiring a Municipally-Owned Ski Resort in Japan as a Non-Resident Foreign Individual
## Procedure, Law, Tax and Structure — Itoigawa City (糸魚川市), Niigata Prefecture

**Scope note:** This memo covers *how you buy*, not what the assets physically are. Physical specs of Seaside Valley (糸魚川シーサイドバレースキー場) and Charmant Hiuchi (シャルマン火打スキー場) are covered by a separate workstream.

**Confidence tags:** [P1] statute / government / assembly record · [P2] press · [P3] other · [E] inference by author · **未確認 = not verified**

**Baseline facts assumed known and not repeated:** no nationality bar on freehold acquisition; Forest Act (森林法) Art. 10-7-2 post-acquisition notification within 90 days, nationality added as a reportable item from 1 July 2024; Cabinet Secretariat study panel on foreign land acquisition rules first met 4 March 2026 with a framework due summer 2026; the Agricultural Land Act (農地法) does not reach forest/wasteland; ropeways are regulated under the Railway Business Act (鉄道事業法).

## 1. The legal framework for disposal of municipal property — THE CORE ISSUE

### 1.1 Administrative property vs ordinary property (行政財産 / 普通財産)

[P1] Under the **Local Autonomy Act (地方自治法)**:

- **Art. 238(3)** divides public property (公有財産) into **administrative property (行政財産)** — property used by the local government itself (公用財産) or provided for public use (公共用財産) — and **ordinary property (普通財産)**, everything else.
- **Art. 238-4(1)**: administrative property **may not be leased, exchanged, sold, gifted, contributed as capital, entrusted, or subjected to private rights**, save for the narrow exceptions in paras (2)–(3) (e.g. leasing land under a solid structure that furthers the property's public purpose; permission for use outside the intended purpose under para (7)).
- **Art. 238-5**: ordinary property **may** be leased, sold, exchanged, gifted, contributed as capital, or entrusted.

[P1] Consequence, stated plainly in municipal handbooks: *"行政財産は処分できないことから、用途廃止により普通財産としたうえで処分することになる"* — administrative property cannot be disposed of; it must first be **abolished as to use (用途廃止)** and thereby converted into ordinary property.

A ski resort operated by a municipality for public use is a **公の施設 (public facility)** under **Art. 244** and is therefore administrative property. **The wall is real: nothing can be sold until the use designation is abolished.**

### 1.2 The de-designation sequence and how long it takes

[P1]/[E] Typical sequence (each municipality's 公有財産管理規則 differs in detail; Itoigawa's own rule text is **未確認**):

1. **Abolish or amend the establishing ordinance (設置条例の廃止)** for the public facility — a council bill, so a **council resolution** is required. (Municipal ski resorts are established by ordinance, e.g. 由利本荘市営スキー場条例, 国見平スキー場条例.)
2. **Terminate / cancel the designated manager's designation (指定管理者の指定の取消し)** — the designation itself was made by council resolution under Art. 244-2(6), and the ordinance must contain a cancellation provision.
3. **用途廃止 (abolition of use)** — an internal administrative decision (決裁) by the mayor under the property management rule; the administering department then hands the asset to the finance/property department (財産所管換え).
4. Asset becomes **普通財産**; then disposal under Art. 238-5.
5. **Council resolution on the disposal itself** (see 1.3).
6. Registration transfer.

[P1] **Duration:** no statutory clock. A worked municipal example (Nisshin City, for public-use land) states roughly **6 months** from application to completion of the transfer registration. For a facility of this scale, the realistic path is measured against the council calendar: Itoigawa holds ordinary sessions in March, June, September and December, so **each resolution step is gated to a quarterly session**. [E] Two to three separate resolutions across different sessions ⇒ **12–24 months minimum** from decision to closing, which is consistent with the city's own FY2026→FY2028 timetable.

### 1.3 Council resolution thresholds (地方自治法第96条第1項)

[P1] Two independent gates:

**(a) Art. 96(1)(6)** — *"Except where an ordinance provides otherwise, to exchange property, contribute it as capital, use it as a means of payment, or **transfer or lend it without fair consideration**"* requires a council resolution. This is mirrored in **Art. 237(2)**: municipal property may **not** be transferred or lent **without proper consideration (適正な対価なくして)** unless by ordinance or council resolution.

[P1] **Supreme Court, 17 Nov 2005 (First Petty Bench)**: for the Art. 237(2) resolution to be valid, the council must have deliberated **on the premise that the transfer is not for fair consideration** and resolved to permit *that*. A generic budget or property resolution is not enough. → **A free or discounted transfer of a ski resort must be voted on explicitly as such, in public, on the record.** Politically this is the most exposed vote in the whole process.

[P1] Most municipalities also enact a **財産の交換、譲与、無償貸付等に関する条例** (ordinance on exchange, gift and free lending of property) which pre-authorises certain gratuitous transfers without a case-by-case resolution — but such ordinances are normally limited to transfers to the State, other public bodies, or public-interest purposes. **Itoigawa's version: 未確認.**

**(b) Art. 96(1)(8)** — acquisition or disposal of property "of a type and value fixed by ordinance in accordance with standards prescribed by Cabinet Order" requires a council resolution.

[P1] The Cabinet Order standard is **地方自治法施行令第121条の2第2項 and 別表第4 (Appended Table 4)**. The tiers are:

| Entity | Predetermined price (予定価格) | Land area condition |
|---|---|---|
| Prefecture (都道府県) | ¥70,000,000 or more | 20,000 m² or more per case |
| Designated city (指定都市) | ¥40,000,000 or more | 10,000 m² or more per case |
| City (市, non-designated) | **¥20,000,000 or more** | **5,000 m² or more per case** |
| Town/village (町村) | ¥7,000,000 or more | 5,000 m² or more per case |

[P2] Multiple municipal ordinances retrieved confirm the ¥20m / 5,000 m² pairing for cities. **Itoigawa's own 「議会の議決に付すべき契約及び財産の取得又は処分に関する条例」 text and figures: 未確認** (the city's 例規集 is not reachable through the tooling available here — the city clerk, 議会事務局, 025-552-1511, holds it).

[E] **Practical read:** a ski resort's land is far above 5,000 m². So:
- If sold at a price ≥ ¥20m → Art. 96(1)(8) resolution required.
- If sold below ¥20m or transferred free → Art. 96(1)(8) may not bite, **but** Art. 96(1)(6) / Art. 237(2) bites instead, and per the 2005 Supreme Court ruling the under-value nature must be squarely put to the council.
- **There is no price at which you avoid a council vote.** The only question is which vote.

### 1.4 The "particularly important public facility" supermajority trap

[P1] **Art. 244-2(2)**: where a municipality seeks to **abolish** a public facility that an ordinance designates as **"particularly important" (特に重要な公の施設)**, or to allow long-term exclusive use of one, it needs the consent of **two-thirds of the members present**, not a simple majority.

[E] This bites **only** if Itoigawa has an ordinance listing the ski resorts as "particularly important". Most cities' lists are short (halls, hospitals, water works). **Itoigawa's list: 未確認 — check it early.** If a ski resort is on it, a hostile third of the council can block the deal outright.

## 2. How the solicitation will actually run

### 2.1 Contract method

[P1] **Art. 234(1)–(2)**: contracts are made by **general competitive tender (一般競争入札)** as the principle; designated tender, **negotiated contract (随意契約)** and auction are permitted **only** in cases prescribed by Cabinet Order.

[P1] **施行令第167条の2第1項**: the grounds for a negotiated contract include (No. 1) value below a threshold set by municipal rule, and (No. 2) **"contracts whose nature or purpose is unsuited to competitive tender" (性質又は目的が競争入札に適しないもの)**. Guidance from Kyoto Prefecture and others confirms that **公募型プロポーザル (public-solicitation proposal method) rests on No. 2**.

[P1] MLIT guidance on public-land disposal: *"公有地売却等は（市民等）共有の財産の処分という観点から、一般競争入札によりできるだけ高い価格で売払い、成果を還元していくことが原則"* — highest-price competitive tender is the default because it is the citizens' shared property.

[E] **Expected method for Itoigawa:** a **公募型プロポーザル / sounding-then-proposal hybrid**, not a pure price auction — because the city's stated objective is to shed an ¥80–90m/yr subsidy while keeping the resort *operating*, which is a qualitative objective that price alone cannot secure. Precedent supports this: Sapporo Moiwa (公募 → 優先交渉権者 → 覚書), Tsunan (提案募集 → 購入意向書 → 優先交渉権 → 基本協定書 → 売買契約), Daisen (公募 for a new operator opening 3 March).

### 2.2 Conditions typically attached — and their collision with a private ski mountain

[P1] The Ministry of Finance's standard national-property contract form is literally titled *"第1号書式（代金延納、用途指定（買戻特約付き）、減額売払用）"* — deferred payment, **use designation with repurchase option**, discounted sale. Municipalities copy this. The standard toolkit:

| Condition | Legal vehicle | Effect on a "private ski mountain" plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Use designation (用途指定)** — must use the land as a ski resort for N years | Contractual covenant, registered | **Fatal conflict.** A covenant to operate a ski area is incompatible with closing it. |
| **Repurchase special clause (買戻特約)** | 民法第579条–581条; **maximum 10 years** (5 if unspecified), **cannot be extended**; registrable against third parties | City can take the property back at the original price if the covenant is breached. |
| **Business continuation obligation (事業継続義務)** | Covenant + liquidated damages | Directly forbids non-operation. |
| **Employment succession (雇用承継)** | Covenant / best-efforts clause | Tsunan's council made this a core grievance (see §3.1). |
| **Resale restriction (譲渡制限期間)** | Covenant + city consent requirement | Locks in the buyer. |
| **Liquidated damages (違約金)** | Covenant | Sized to the deal; unquantified in public sources. |
| **Removal / restoration obligation** | Covenant | See §7 — potentially nine figures in yen. |

[E] **This is the second structural problem for the client, after the council.** A municipality selling a loss-making ski resort is not selling an asset; it is **outsourcing a public service**. Its entire political justification for the sale is *"the resort will keep operating and local jobs will be preserved."* A bid whose plan is "close it and ski it privately" fails the evaluation on the merits, before nationality is even discussed. If the client's real plan is private use, the honest options are (a) bid to operate commercially and then seek release from the covenants later (high risk of 買戻し), or (b) **do not buy from a municipality at all** — buy a private resort (see §3.5, Grandeco).

### 2.3 Can a foreigner or foreign entity even apply?

[P1] **施行令第167条の4(1)** sets the *mandatory* exclusions from general competitive tender: persons without contractual capacity, undischarged bankrupts, and persons under the Anti-Organised Crime Act (暴力団員による不当な行為の防止等に関する法律). **Nationality is not among them.** Para (2) allows discretionary exclusion for misconduct. National-property practice is explicit: *"法人も外国人の方も入札に参加できます"* (both corporations and foreign nationals may bid).

[P1] **But:** MIC/Cabinet materials confirm that local governments **may lawfully impose a business-location qualification** — the so-called **地域要件 (regional requirement)** — on tender participants. This is the real gate. Common formulations in municipal solicitations: *"市内に本店又は主たる事業所を有する法人"*, *"日本国内に本店を有する法人"*, *"法人であること"* (excluding individuals altogether), plus tax-clearance certificates (納税証明書) for national and municipal taxes, and registration on the city's 入札参加資格者名簿.

[E] **Assessment:**
- An explicit **nationality** bar is legally unusual and would be hard for a city to defend (and cuts against GATS, which the Cabinet Secretariat panel is itself weighing — §4.5).
- A requirement that the applicant be a **Japanese-registered legal entity**, or have a **Japanese place of business**, or produce **Japanese tax clearance certificates**, is routine, uncontroversial and very likely to appear.
- **Frequency: 未確認** — I could not retrieve any actual ski-resort solicitation guideline (募集要項) text to quantify how often. Sapporo Moiwa's 募集要項 PDF and Daisen's 公募 documents exist and should be obtained directly from those cities as the best available template.
- **Operational conclusion:** a **non-resident individual should assume he cannot bid in his own name**, and should plan on a Japanese entity (合同会社 or 株式会社) established well in advance, with a Japanese address, a tax filing history and clean tax certificates. Establishing the entity *after* the solicitation opens is usually too late, because tax-clearance certificates require a filing history.

## 3. Precedent analysis — the most important section

### 3.1 New Greenpia Tsunan (ニュー・グリーンピア津南), Tsunan Town, Niigata — the cautionary tale

**This is the single most important precedent for the client, and its lesson is the opposite of what one might assume.**

**Timeline** [P2, Niigata Nippo / Tokamachi Shinbun / Tsuma-ari Shinbun]:

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Sept 2005 | Facility transferred to Tsunan Town; operated by third-sector **株式会社津南高原開発** |
| FY2024–25 | Town resolves to sell land + buildings; hires a transaction advisor. The **supplementary budget for the advisory fee passed the council 6–5** — a one-vote margin, with complaints about disclosure |
| Apr–May 2025 | **Two purchase-intent letters (購入意向書)** received: (1) **株式会社イントランス (INTRANCE)**, Shibuya, Tokyo; (2) a **joint bid by the incumbent operator 津南高原開発 + a regional revitalisation fund** |
| July 2025 | **INTRANCE granted preferential negotiating rights (優先交渉権)**; direction was to attract a **British-brand hotel** |
| Aug 2025 | **Six of the eleven council members publicly declare opposition**, taking issue with the INTRANCE negotiations over **maintenance of employment** |
| Oct 10 2025 | **Basic agreement (基本協定書)** signed. Town expressly writes in that **the water source and reservoirs on the site will NOT be sold**, and that INTRANCE will make efforts to retain existing employees. Sale contract targeted for end-March 2026 |
| late 2025 | Residents' group **「津南町 人と自然と水の会」** ("Tsunan People, Nature and Water Association") petitions the council to examine both bidders' plans and decide which is appropriate |
| **Mar 2026** | **Council passes a recommendatory resolution (勧告決議) 6–5** — *"㈱イントランスへの土地及び建物の売却に反対し、同社との基本協定書の解除を求める勧告決議"* — opposing the sale and demanding termination of the basic agreement. **Stated grounds: the site is "almost entirely water-source recharge land (ほぼ全域が水源涵養地)" and is vast** |
| Apr 2026 | Sale **白紙 (back to blank)**. Town signals it will terminate the basic agreement. **Mayor Kuwabara Haruka announces she will not seek re-election.** Town says it still intends to sell privately |
| Apr–May 2026 | Town sends the council a **17-item questionnaire** (whether to sell at all; sale vs lease; **what land area may be sold**; asking price). **11 May 2026: council returns 17 conditions** — **the area sold must be limited**, **water abstraction (取水) subject to prior consultation**, and the council signals it wants **"Plan B"**: succession of the business by the incumbent 津南高原開発 |

**Why the council said no — the honest answer:**

[P1]/[P2] The *stated* reasons were: (i) **water-source recharge land**, (ii) the **sheer size** of the parcel, (iii) **employment continuity**. Process grievances (poor information disclosure to the council, the town moving ahead of the council) run through the whole record — note the 6–5 votes at both ends.

[P2] **The decisive point for the client: INTRANCE is a Japanese company.** 株式会社イントランス, founded 1 May 1998, headquartered in Dōgenzaka, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo, listed on the **Tokyo Stock Exchange (code 3237)**, capital ≈ ¥1.44bn, ~133 consolidated employees, in real-estate regeneration, hotel operation and hotel investment. **This was not a foreign buyer.** No source I found records foreign or Chinese capital as a ground of objection.

[E] **Therefore the Tsunan lesson is *not* "Japan blocks foreigners." It is worse and more general:**

> A Niigata town council blocked a **domestic, listed, professional** buyer by a one-vote margin, on grounds of **water**, **land area** and **local jobs**, and preferred the **local incumbent operator**. Every one of those objections applies with greater force to a non-resident foreign individual who intends to stop operating the resort. Nationality would be an **aggravating** factor layered on top of objections that already succeeded without it.

[E] Note also the **structural remedies** the Tsunan council converged on, which a buyer should expect to be offered rather than a clean freehold: **sell a limited area only** (carving out the watershed), **lease rather than sell**, and **prior-consultation rights over water abstraction**. A "buy the whole mountain" ask is precisely what got refused.

### 3.2 Murakami City Budoh Ski Resort (村上市ぶどうスキー場) → ぶどうスノーリゾート, 2025 — **not actually a sale**

[P2] Corrects a common misreading. The facts:

- The **city-run** ski resort closed at the end of **March 2025** (falling visitors, ageing equipment).
- **株式会社シンクファースト (Think First)**, a Tokyo IT company, took over. **What it actually received: a three-year FREE LOAN (3年間無償貸付) of the city's facilities and equipment — lifts, lodge, etc. — approved by the city council at the end of September 2025**, with the contract signed in October.
- **The land is not the city's to sell.** The city holds it under a lease from the landowners; **Think First pays rent to the city, and the city passes it to the landowners.** Think First separately obtained the **unanimous consent of all landowners (全地権者の同意)**.
- Reopened as **ぶどうスノーリゾート**, targeting 20 December 2025, with year-round plans (campsite, RV park).

[P2] Some reporting describes this as the operator "receiving the facilities free of charge (無償で譲り受け)". The more detailed local reporting says **無償貸付 for three years**. **Which it legally is — gift (譲与) or free loan (無償貸付) — is 未確認**; but either way it went through the council, because **both** free transfer and free lending are caught by Art. 96(1)(6) / Art. 237(2).

[E] **Relevance to the client, and it is high:**
1. **A municipality may not own the ground.** Ski resorts are frequently built on private, communal or forestry-cooperative land under long leases. If Itoigawa is the same, the client cannot buy the mountain from the city at any price — he must assemble consents from a large number of private landowners, and **unanimity** was needed at Murakami.
2. **The template on offer may be a free 3-year loan, not a freehold.** That gives no security of tenure, no ability to exclude the public, and no asset.
3. The winner was chosen because it promised to **reopen and operate**, not to close.

### 3.3 Daisen White Resort (だいせんホワイトリゾート), Daisen Town, Tottori — transfer *to* the municipality

[P2] Direction of travel is the reverse of the client's:

- **日本交通 (Nippon Kotsu) Group** operated the three areas as designated manager for **55 years** and withdrew at the end of the 2025–26 season (population decline, warm winters).
- On withdrawal it **transferred 13 lifts and related facilities to Daisen Town free of charge, plus a ¥75m cash donation**.
- The town then opened a **public solicitation for a new designated manager from 3 March**, with a "thank-you day" of free lifts/lessons/parking to close the season.

[P2]/[E] Reading: a departing private operator **paid the municipality to take the assets** — a clean measure of the negative economic value of an ageing lift fleet (see §7). **The chain to Mac Earth and then Adoba (April 2026) reported by the client: 未確認** — none of my searches surfaced either name in connection with this resort. Worth re-checking directly with Daisen Town.

### 3.4 Gokase Highland (五ヶ瀬ハイランドスキー場), Gokase Town, Miyazaki, 2026 — the closure economics

[P2] Japan's southernmost ski resort, town-run, closed 2026 after 35 years:

- **To continue:** ~**¥500m capital investment plus over ¥40m/year** in running costs.
- **To close:** dismantling the **lifts and centre house ≈ ¥500m**.
- The mayor stated continuation was impossible; a resolution on the resort's future **passed a special council session unanimously on 7 July 2026**; the site will be repurposed as the 向坂山森林公園 outdoor area.

[E] Note the symmetry the local commentary drew: *"続けてもやめてもお金がかかる"* — it costs money either way. **This is why a municipality may be willing to transfer for ¥0 or even pay: the alternative is a ¥500m demolition bill on its own budget.** It is also exactly why it will try to put the demolition obligation into your contract.

### 3.5 Grandeco (グランデコ), Kitashiobara Village, Fukushima — the private-to-private route that worked

[P2] Fukushima's third-largest ski area. **東急不動産 (Tokyu Land)** announced disposal in July 2022 as part of a resort-business retreat; the buyer was initially undisclosed at the buyer's request. It is **イデラキャピタルマネジメント (IDERA Capital Management)** — since 2014 a subsidiary of China's **Fosun Group (復星集団)** — with the ski area and hotel run by IDERA's wholly owned **The Court (ザ・コート)**. The adjacent 東急ハーヴェストクラブ裏磐梯グランデコ hotel closed at end-March 2023.

[E] **The most instructive contrast in this memo.** Chinese-affiliated capital acquired a major Japanese ski resort **with no council vote, no public solicitation, no use designation, no repurchase clause and no employment covenant** — because the seller was a private company. The entire §1–§2 apparatus exists only because the seller is a municipality. **If the client's objective is control and privacy rather than a specific mountain, a private-market acquisition is dramatically easier.**

### 3.6 Other reference points

- [P2] **Appi Kogen (安比高原), Hachimantai, Iwate** — Asia Gate Group (Chinese capital) acquired the operator 岩手ホテルアンドリゾート in 2016. Record inbound revenue by 2018, then disputes: Japanese condominium owners report **management fees raised 4.25×**, being "expelled from rooms they own", and demands to spend ¥10m+ on interior upgrades. This is the story that Japanese councillors have in mind when a foreign buyer appears.
- [P2] **Suishōzan (水晶山) Ski Resort, Kazuno City, Akita** — the third-sector operator failed; the **city took a free transfer** and re-let it to a new company as designated manager. Again: municipality as receiver of last resort.
- [P2] **Sapporo Moiwa (札幌藻岩山)** — city ski resort; 募集要項 published 22 Feb 2024, questions to 29 Feb, applicant registration to 19 Mar; 北海道スクエア株式会社 selected as preferential negotiator; new structure from April 2025, with lift operation and maintenance still done by りんゆう観光. **Operation transfer, not a land sale.**
- [P2] **Oshu City (奥州市), Iwate** — of three loss-making municipal resorts, two were suspended and operations concentrated on 国見平スキー場 alone from Dec 2022 under a designated manager.
- [P2] **Uonuma City (魚沼市), Niigata** — three municipal resorts to be run by a new body from April 2027, per a council of three NPO/operators.
- [P2] **休暇村協会 (National Park Resort Villages of Japan)** — a public-interest foundation operating ski areas attached to its hotels (羽黒山, Mt. 乗鞍); it operates some ropeways itself. **No transfer-to-private precedent found. 未確認.**
- [P2] Roughly **40%+ of Japanese ski resorts involve a local government** (public, third-sector, or public-built/private-run), against ~50% purely private (Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Research Institute, 21 Mar 2025). So the municipal-disposal problem is systemic, not a one-off.

## 4. Political backlash risk against foreign capital — the candid assessment

### 4.1 Niigata Prefecture's own water-source ordinance — a hard, pre-closing legal step

[P1] **新潟県水源地域の保全に関する条例 (Niigata Prefectural Ordinance on the Conservation of Water Source Areas)**, pre-notification system operative since **1 July 2012**:

- Applies to **transactions in forest land within designated 水源地域 (water source areas)** — forests with water-conservation functions in the upstream basins of the Shinano (信濃川), Agano (阿賀野川) and other rivers.
- A person contracting to **transfer land ownership, or to create rights over land**, in a water source area must **notify the Prefectural Governor up to 30 days BEFORE the contract is concluded**.
- Failure to notify or false notification → **recommendation (勧告), public naming (公表), and a fine (過料)**.
- Certain exemptions (utility operations, land-improvement projects).

[E] **This is a pre-transaction gate the client's baseline briefing does not include, and it is more onerous than the Forest Act's 90-day post-acquisition notice.** It also means the *prefecture* is notified and can comment **before** signing — 30 days during which a politically sensitive transaction becomes visible. **Whether the specific Itoigawa parcels sit inside a designated 水源地域: 未確認 — confirm with 新潟県 治山課 (Erosion & Torrent Control Division) before committing.** Given that Tsunan's entire objection was watershed-based, this must be checked first, not last.

### 4.2 Itoigawa City's own ordinances

[P1] Itoigawa City publishes a **土地取引の届出 (land transaction notification)** page administered by its Planning & Policy Section — this is the National Land Use Planning Act channel (§4.4), not a bespoke ordinance. **No Itoigawa-specific water-source or foreign-capital land ordinance was found. 未確認** — but the negative result is meaningful: the prefectural ordinance is the operative constraint, and Itoigawa has not (yet) layered its own on top.

### 4.3 The Myoko precedent — the closest analogue, and it cuts both ways

[P2] **妙高市 (Myoko City), Niigata**, ~50 km from Itoigawa:

- **Patience Capital Group (PCG)**, a **Singapore**-based real-estate investment fund, has acquired approximately **350 ha including two existing ski areas** across Myoko Kogen and the adjacent Madarao / Nojiriko area in Nagano; headline programme **¥200bn**, phase 1 ~**¥70bn**; four hotels and two commercial facilities by 2034, of which two hotels and two commercial facilities targeted to open **December 2028**; construction start by summer 2026.
- **Mayor Kido Yōji** called it *"a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity"* while stressing careful handling; he has also said he has heard little concrete detail.
- **Land prices in the Sekikawa (関川) district rose 9% year-on-year — the highest rate in Niigata Prefecture.** Local anxiety explicitly references the Niseko bubble (15× land price rises, locals priced out) and Hakuba.

[P2] **The regulatory response — this is the key data point:** Myoko City drafted an ordinance (「市開発行為等の手続きに関する条例」) that, for the ski-area zone and surroundings:
- **lowers the threshold requiring city permission from 3,000 m² (the City Planning Act figure) to 1,000 m²**;
- **mandates prior consultation (事前協議)**; and
- **mandates resident consent (住民同意)**.
Submitted to the September regular session, targeted for effect **April 2026**. A residents' council (協議会) was also established to feed local views into development plans.

[E] **Read this correctly.** It shows (i) a foreign fund **can and did** acquire Niigata ski resorts and hundreds of hectares — the acquisitions were **private-market**, not municipal disposals; and (ii) the municipality's answer was **not** to block ownership (it legally cannot) but to **throttle what you may do with the land afterwards**, at a threshold so low (1,000 m²) that almost any construction — and plausibly any material earthworks, lift removal or piste alteration — needs city permission **plus neighbour consent**. For a buyer who wants to *stop* operating and use a mountain privately, a resident-consent requirement is a very effective veto.

### 4.5 The national policy direction

[P1]/[P2] The Cabinet Secretariat's **外国人による土地取得等のルールの在り方検討会** (Study Panel on the Proper Form of Rules for Land Acquisition by Foreigners) first met **4 March 2026**; a **framework (骨格/骨子案) is due summer 2026**. Membership includes **北村滋** (former Director of the National Security Secretariat), **黒江哲郎** (former Vice-Minister of Defense) and **松尾弘** (Keio University, land systems). Under consideration:
- whether rules should be **nationality-blind (内外無差別)** or **foreigner-specific** — the GATS non-discrimination obligation is an explicit constraint on the latter;
- regulatory instruments: **licensing/permission**, **pre-notification with screening**, **on-site inspection**;
- the geographic scope; nationalisation of ownerless remote islands; possible **mandatory nationality registration**.

[P1] **外国人土地法 (Alien Land Act, Act No. 42 of 1925)** remains formally in force — the Ministry of Justice so testified in the Diet in 2017. Art. 1 permits, **by imperial ordinance (now read as Cabinet Order per Cabinet Order No. 14 of 1947)**, reciprocal prohibitions or conditions on land rights for nationals of states that restrict Japanese; there is also a national-defence provision. **No activating Cabinet Order is currently in force**, so it has no present effect — but it is a loaded weapon that requires only a Cabinet Order, not a new statute, to fire. [E] Low probability, non-zero, and it is the fastest available lever if politics turn.

[P1] Multiple prefectural assemblies have adopted **formal opinion papers (意見書)** to the national government demanding restrictions on land acquisition by foreign entities — e.g. **Kagawa Prefecture (R6/2024)**, **Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly (2012)**, **Fukuoka Prefecture (H30/2018)**. The House of Representatives Research Bureau (衆議院調査局国土交通調査室) published a cross-cutting review, *「外国人による不動産取得に関する整理」*, in **February 2026** (revised 26 Feb 2026).

### 4.7 Bottom line on political risk

[E] Ranked, for a non-resident Malaysian individual bidding for an Itoigawa municipal ski resort:

1. **Highest risk — the plan, not the passport.** "I will not operate it commercially" defeats the city's own stated purpose. Tsunan proves a council will kill a deal over continuity and jobs even for a listed Japanese buyer.
2. **Second — water.** If any part of the site is watershed or in a designated 水源地域, expect the Tsunan playbook: carve-out of the water source, prior-consultation rights over abstraction, and a limited sale area.
3. **Third — foreign ownership as such.** Legally unobstructed today; politically an amplifier, not the primary objection; and the national framework due summer 2026 is a live, unquantified change in the rules mid-transaction.
4. **Fourth — post-acquisition throttling.** Myoko shows the municipal counter-move is development control with resident consent, which can be enacted *after* you buy and can frustrate use without touching title.

## 5. Acquisition structure

### 5.1 Non-resident individual buying and registering title

[P1] A non-resident foreign national **cannot obtain a 住民票 (residence certificate) or 印鑑証明書 (seal registration certificate)**, both of which Japanese registration practice normally requires. Substitutes:

| Japanese document | Substitute for a Malaysia-resident foreign national |
|---|---|
| 印鑑証明書 (seal certificate) | **署名証明 (signature certificate)** issued by the **Japanese Embassy/Consulate** in the country of residence, **or** a **宣誓供述書 (affidavit)** notarised by a local **notary public / commissioner for oaths** |
| 住民票 (residence certificate) | **在留証明 (proof of residence)** from the Japanese Embassy/Consulate; if the applicant lives far from a Japanese mission, a **notarised affidavit** of address is accepted instead |
| Identity | **Passport copy showing the name in Roman letters** |

[P1] The affidavit typically recites name, address, date of birth, nationality, passport number and the facts being attested. A **司法書士 (judicial scrivener)** handles the 所有権移転登記 (transfer of ownership registration); engaging one experienced with non-resident registrations is not optional in practice.

[P1] **A 納税管理人 (tax agent) filing is mandatory**, not optional, for **local** taxes: *"所得税や消費税の納税管理人の届出は任意ですが、自動車税や固定資産税などは納税管理人の届出を必ずしなければなりません."* File a **納税管理人申告書** with the municipality where the property sits (Itoigawa City) for **固定資産税 and 不動産取得税**. For national income tax the appointment is technically elective but practically necessary.

[E] **Apostille:** whether Malaysia-notarised documents need an apostille or consular legalisation for Japanese registry use — **未確認**. Confirm with the司法書士; using the Japanese Embassy in Kuala Lumpur for both the signature certificate and the residence proof sidesteps the question entirely.

### 5.2 Individual vs Japanese company

| | **Non-resident individual** | **合同会社 (GK / LLC)** | **株式会社 (KK)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formation cost [P1] | ¥0 | **¥60,000–100,000** (registration tax = greater of ¥60,000 or 0.7% of capital; **no articles-notarisation fee**) | ~¥200,000–250,000 (notarisation ¥30,000–50,000 + registration tax min ¥150,000) |
| Annual fixed cost [P1] | ¥0 | **法人住民税均等割 ≈ ¥70,000/yr minimum, payable even at a loss** (capital ≤¥10m); accounting/tax agent ¥100,000–300,000/yr | Same, plus heavier governance |
| Formation by a non-resident [P1] | n/a | Possible; no 印鑑証明書, so a **署名証明書** from the country of residence is used | Same |
| Income tax on rent [P1] | 国内源泉所得; **corporate tenants withhold 20.42%**; annual return required | Corporation tax; **effective rate ~36.05% above ¥8m of income** (per BPS Kokusai; [E] Tokyo figures nearer 33–34% are also commonly cited — **未確認**) | Same |
| Capital gain on exit [P1] | **Short-term (held ≤5 yrs): 39.63%** (income tax 30.63% + inhabitant tax 9%); **long-term (>5 yrs): 20.315%** (15.315% + 5%). **Buyer must withhold 10.21% of the gross price** when purchasing from a non-resident | Ordinary corporation tax on the gain; no short/long split | Same |
| **Bidding eligibility (§2.3)** | **Likely excluded** by a "corporation" and/or Japanese-place-of-business requirement | **Qualifies**; can build a tax-filing record and obtain 納税証明書 | Qualifies; higher credibility with a municipal evaluation panel |

[E] **Recommendation: a Japanese 合同会社 (GK), formed at least 12 months before the solicitation opens.** It is the cheapest vehicle that (a) satisfies a "legal entity with a Japanese place of business" participation requirement, (b) accumulates the tax-filing history needed for 納税証明書, (c) presents better than a foreign individual to a municipal evaluation panel, and (d) segregates the demolition/liability exposure in §7. The ~¥70,000/yr 均等割 plus accounting fees is trivial against the deal.

### 5.3 Transaction taxes

[P1]
- **不動産取得税 (real property acquisition tax)** — prefectural; standard **4%** of the 固定資産税評価額, **reduced 3%** for land and residential buildings under the current measure; **non-residential buildings remain 4%**. Land also benefits from a 1/2 valuation reduction. **Owner-occupancy reliefs for second-hand homes are unavailable to a person with no Japanese address.**
- **登録免許税 (registration & licence tax)** — on the transfer registration; base is the 固定資産税評価額. The statutory rate for a sale transfer is **2.0%**, with a reduced rate for **land** in force under successive extensions. [E] **Current land rate and its sunset date: 未確認 — confirm at closing.**
- **印紙税 (stamp duty)** — on the sale contract, on a sliding scale by consideration.
- **消費税 (consumption tax)** — [P1] **land is non-taxable** (a transfer of capital, not consumption); **buildings are taxable at 10% where the seller is a taxable enterprise**. A municipality disposing of buildings is in principle a taxable person for such a supply, so **assume 10% on the building component and negotiate the land/building price split deliberately** — it materially changes the cash cost. [E]
- Same rules apply to residents and non-residents alike: *"国内不動産取得時に課税される不動産取得税、登録免許税、印紙税、消費税などの税金は、原則として居住者と非居住者とで課税関係が異なることはありません."*

### 5.4 Inheritance tax — the largest quiet exposure

[P1]
- A decedent and heirs who are **neither Japanese nationals nor Japanese residents** are **制限納税義務者 (limited taxpayers)**: **only Japan-situs assets** are within the charge.
- But Japan-situs assets are taxed on the **full progressive scale, 10% to 55%**, the top rate applying to a statutory-share portion **exceeding ¥600m**.
- **基礎控除 (basic exemption)** = **¥30m + ¥6m × number of statutory heirs**.
- **債務控除 (deduction for debts) is restricted for limited taxpayers** — broadly, only liabilities attaching to the Japanese assets. [P1] confirmed as a restriction in principle; **the precise scope: 未確認, get tax counsel.**

[E] **Consequences the client should hear plainly:**
1. **Malaysia abolished estate duty in 1991 and has no inheritance tax.** There is therefore **no foreign tax credit and no offsetting relief** — Japanese inheritance tax on this asset is a pure, unrelieved cost to the estate.
2. **The Japan–Malaysia tax treaty is an income tax treaty. It does not cover inheritance tax.** No treaty relief is available. [E]
3. **A Japanese GK does not help.** Shares in a Japanese company are Japan-situs property and remain within the limited taxpayer's charge.
4. **A non-Japanese holding company might.** If the individual holds shares in a **foreign** company that owns the Japanese land, those *shares* are foreign-situs and fall outside a limited taxpayer's charge — the classic structure. **[E] — this is inference, is exactly the sort of arrangement subject to anti-avoidance scrutiny, adds a Japanese permanent-establishment / 外国法人 corporation tax analysis, and MUST be designed by a Japanese international tax specialist before any acquisition. Do not implement on the strength of this memo.**
5. Direct personal ownership by a non-resident is the **worst** structure on this axis and the **best** on simplicity. The decision should be made before signing, not after — restructuring post-acquisition triggers a second round of acquisition tax and registration tax.

## 6. Treatment if the resort is NOT operated commercially

### 6.1 Ropeways and lifts (索道) under the Railway Business Act

[P1] **鉄道事業法 (Railway Business Act) Chapter 3, Arts. 32–38** governs 索道事業. Classification is in the Enforcement Regulations Art. 47: **普通索道 (ordinary ropeway** — enclosed cabins with doors, i.e. gondolas/ropeways**)** and **特殊索道 (special ropeway** — open chair carriers, i.e. **chairlifts**).

- **Art. 32:** operating a 索道事業 requires **permission from the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (国土交通大臣の許可), per ropeway.**
- **Suspension / abolition:** [P1] a 索道事業者 that **suspends or abolishes all or part of** its ropeway business must **notify the Minister without delay (遅滞なく届け出)**. **This is a post-hoc notification, not the one-year advance notice** that applies to a **railway** business (Art. 28-2). **Exact article number for 索道: 未確認** — but the substantive distinction is confirmed by MLIT/prefectural procedure guidance and is materially favourable to the client.
- **Restart after ≥6 months' suspension:** [P1] the operator must **confirm that the ropeway facilities conform to the technical standards** and notify the Minister — in practice a conformity-confirmation / extraordinary inspection (臨時検査).
- **While operating:** [P1] facilities must be maintained to MLIT technical standards, with **daily inspections during the operating period, monthly inspections and quarterly inspections**, plus periodic **保安監査 (safety audits)** of the operator.

[E] **The favourable reading for the client:** if the lifts are **suspended (休止) or abolished (廃止)** and no passengers are carried, the daily/monthly/quarterly inspection regime — which is framed around the operating period — falls away, and exit is by notification rather than by a one-year advance filing or a negotiated abolition process. This is a genuine advantage over, say, a railway.

[E] **The unresolved question — get a written answer from 北陸信越運輸局 (Hokushinetsu District Transport Bureau) before purchase:** 索道事業 is defined as carrying **other persons'** passengers or freight **as a business (事業)**. Running a lift **for yourself and unpaying guests, not as a business**, arguably falls outside the definition entirely and therefore outside the permit regime. **This is inference and is precisely the kind of question on which a regulator's informal view differs from the statute's text.** If the Bureau says the permit regime still applies, the client is a permitted ropeway operator with all the attendant duties, or he cannot run the lifts at all. **未確認 — this is a purchase-condition-level question.**

[E] Note also: **acquiring a resort with lifts still in operation requires the ropeway business itself to be transferred**, which will need MLIT approval of the transfer/succession (譲渡譲受の認可 / 事業承継) — **exact procedure for 索道: 未確認**; the transport bureaus publish 事業計画等を変更される方・事業承継される方 guidance.

### 6.2 Fixed asset tax (固定資産税)

[P1] **Land:** the classification for fixed asset tax purposes is the **actual condition (現況の地目) as of 1 January (賦課期日)**, **not the registered category**. Broad order of assessed value: **宅地 > 雑種地 > 農地・山林・原野**.

[P1] Under the **財産評価基本通達**, land used for *"遊園地、運動場、競馬場その他これらに類似する施設"* — amusement parks, sports grounds, racecourses and similar facilities, which **includes ski areas** — is valued **by applying the rules for 雑種地 (miscellaneous land)**.

[E] **If operations cease:** the assessor could in principle reclassify to 山林 (forest) once the land reverts in fact, which would sharply reduce the tax — forest land assessments are very low. But this is the assessor's judgment on **actual condition**: as long as lift towers, pylons, cleared and maintained pistes, roads, snow-making lines and buildings remain, **the land will very likely stay 雑種地**. **Reduction is therefore contingent on physical removal, not on ceasing to sell tickets.** 未確認 as to Itoigawa's assessment practice — ask the 税務課.

[P1] **Depreciable assets (償却資産):** lifts, snow machines, groomers, plant and equipment are subject to fixed asset tax at **1.4%** of assessed value. The assessment runs off acquisition date, acquisition cost and useful life, with a **floor of 5% of acquisition cost** — so an old lift never depreciates to zero for tax. **No tax is charged if the total taxable base is under ¥1.5m.** [E] **Idle lifts keep generating depreciable-asset tax every year until they are physically scrapped or removed.** Buildings are taxed separately as 家屋.

### 6.3 Vacant buildings

[P1] **空家等対策の推進に関する特別措置法 (Act on Special Measures concerning the Promotion of Countermeasures for Vacant Houses)**, as amended with effect **13 December 2023**:
- Existing category **特定空家 (specified vacant house)** — hazardous, insanitary, blighting or otherwise inappropriate.
- New category **管理不全空家 (poorly-managed vacant house)** — showing signs of becoming a 特定空家.
- Escalation: **指導 (guidance) → 勧告 (recommendation) → 命令 (order) → 行政代執行 (administrative substitute execution, demolition at the owner's cost)**.
- On a **勧告** (管理不全空家) or **特定空家** designation, the **住宅用地特例** (residential land reduction of 1/6 for the first 200 m², 1/3 beyond) is **withdrawn** — the widely reported "tax rises 6×".

[E] **Realistic assessment for a ski resort:**
- The "6× tax" headline **largely does not bite**: a base lodge, hotel or restaurant is **not 住宅用地**, so there is no residential-land reduction to lose. [P1] confirms that **別荘 (holiday homes)** are likewise outside the residential-land reduction because there is no habitual residential use.
- The **real** exposures are the **命令** and **行政代執行** — the city can order remediation and, on default, demolish at the owner's expense and recover the cost as a tax-equivalent claim. For deteriorating structures in a heavy-snow region, this is a live risk on a 10–20 year view.
- Practical mitigation: keep buildings weather-tight, snow-loaded roofs cleared, and appoint a local property manager. **Itoigawa's 空家等対策計画 and whether it has designated any resort structures: 未確認.**

### 7.3 Contractual removal risk — the asymmetry to negotiate hardest

[E] There is **no general statutory duty** to demolish a disused ski lift or lodge. Liability arises from **(a) the sale contract**, **(b) 行政代執行** if a structure becomes dangerous, and **(c) any 保安林 / 自然公園法 / 林地開発許可 restoration conditions attached to the original development consents — 未確認 whether such conditions exist here.**

[E] But **Gokase explains the municipality's incentive precisely**: Itoigawa is looking at a **~¥500m demolition liability** if these resorts simply close on its books, on top of an **¥80–90m/yr** operating subsidy for Seaside Valley alone. A transfer that hands the demolition obligation to a private buyer converts a nine-figure municipal liability into someone else's problem. **Expect the draft contract to contain a restoration/removal covenant, and expect it to be non-negotiable in the city's opening position.**

[E] **Therefore, before bidding, price the downside as:** purchase price (possibly ¥0 or negative) **+** ~¥500m contingent demolition **+** annual 償却資産税 on idle plant **+** annual 固定資産税 on 雑種地 **+** snow-load maintenance on retained buildings **+** contract liquidated damages if the use covenant is breached. On these numbers, **the "free" acquisition of a municipal ski resort is a liability transfer, not a gift.**
