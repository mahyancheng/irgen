# Carrying cost, and the finding that inverts the brief

## Annual carrying cost, modelled 8,000 ha alpine property

| Jurisdiction | Baseline (no skiing) | With private skiing | MYR (with skiing) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Zealand** | NZ$346k–1,210k | NZ$371k–1,280k | **RM 896k–3.09 m** |
| **Japan** | ≈ NZ$212k–935k | *(availability likely prohibitive)* | RM 512k–2.26 m |
| **Chile** | ≈ NZ$106k–351k | **cheapest by 2–4×** | **RM 256k–848k** |

All `[E]`.

## ⚠️ The headline finding: a cheap alpine station is not a low-cost one

> **Carrying cost is almost entirely independent of purchase price.**

Rates follow a low rural valuation. Pest control, tracks, staff, insurance and compliance
follow **area, condition and remoteness** — not what you paid. So the cheaper the entry, the
worse the carry looks as a percentage:

| Purchase price | Total annual cost of ownership @ 5 % cost of capital | **Net carry as % of price** |
|---|---|---:|
| NZ$3 m | NZ$776k (**RM 1.87 m**) | **20.9 %** ⚠️ |
| NZ$8 m | NZ$1,026k (**RM 2.48 m**) | 7.8 % |
| NZ$35 m | NZ$2,376k (**RM 5.74 m**) | 1.8 % |

> **At NZ$3 m you re-spend the purchase price in carrying cost roughly every five years.**

**What actually determines "low cost to hold", in priority order:**

1. **Pest and weed condition** (the largest controllable line)
2. Existing access and buildings
3. ETS-eligible area
4. Assignable grazing lease
5. Total area
6. **Purchase price — least important of the six**

This directly qualifies proposition **T1** of the mandate. Buying cheap rural land is necessary
but nowhere near sufficient; **the condition of the land matters more than its price.**

## The biggest controllable cost: pest and weed condition

Wilding conifers and rabbits swing the New Zealand carry from **NZ$49k to NZ$320k/yr**, and
control is a **legally enforceable obligation inherited with the land.** `[E]`

> Note the conflict this creates: **the species that pays the carbon money is the pest you are
> legally obliged to control.** A radiata-based ETS strategy and a wilding-conifer liability are
> the same tree.

## Insurance is the decisive jurisdictional difference

New Zealand's **Accident Compensation Act 2001 bars compensatory damages claims for personal
injury** — a guest injured skiing **cannot sue**. The residual exposure is HSWA 2015
*regulatory prosecution* (to NZ$1.5 m plus reparations), so the product required is statutory
liability and D&O cover, **not a large public liability tower**.

| Jurisdiction | Estimated annual premium |
|---|---|
| **New Zealand** | NZ$25k–80k |
| **United States** | US$60k–350k+ |

> Over a 20-year hold that delta is **US$0.8–5.6 m — larger than the entire Tier 1 CAPEX.**
> `[E]` The New Zealand regulatory workstream reached the same conclusion independently, from
> the statute rather than the cost side. **This is New Zealand's single strongest advantage in
> the whole study, and it partly offsets the consent difficulty.**

## ⚠️⚠️ The consent–economics inversion

Combining this cost model with the New Zealand regulatory finding — that the benefit-to-NZ test
demands *more* jobs and exports than today, and that cutting stock for a ski operation nets off
*against* the applicant:

> **The configuration that minimises carrying cost is the one the OIO is least likely to
> consent. The configuration that best satisfies the test is the most expensive and
> management-heavy.**

| Model | Economics | Consent |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Grazing leased out, minimal staff, private skiing** | **Best** — net carry NZ$375k, or income-positive with carbon | **Worst** |
| 3. Hunting + lodge + cat-ski + carbon | Nets NZ$460k/yr **while requiring four businesses** — the opposite of a low-cost rural asset | **Best** |

**The private club is doubly adverse**: it cuts jobs *and* restricts access. **Phase 2 as
conceived may simply be unconsentable in New Zealand.** `[E]`

### The single highest-value open legal question in this study

> **Is a defunct ski area, or a non-pastoral alpine title, "farm land" for Overseas Investment
> Act purposes?**

If it is **not**, it escapes the 2025 farmland carve-out, falls under the new consolidated
national interest test with its 15-working-day duty, and **Model 1 becomes viable.** That is
worth **NZ$100–250k/yr plus the removal of any obligation to run four businesses.** `[E]`

**This question should go to New Zealand counsel before any other NZ work is commissioned.**
