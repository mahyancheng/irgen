# Offsetting income — does the ranch pay the rates?

## The short answer: no

> **Pastoral EBIT on the modelled 8,000 ha station is NZ$0–150k/yr, and negative in bad
> years — against a carrying cost of NZ$346k–1,210k.** `[E]`

**Farming is a cost-neutraliser, not a profit centre.** This is the single most important
correction to proposition **T5** of the mandate.

**Challenge any broker who claims a station is self-funding.** Ask for three years of audited
accounts and a stock reconciliation, and read the farm working expenses line rather than the
gross revenue line.

## What actually moves the needle

### 1. Carbon / ETS — the only line that can cover the whole carry, and the easiest to get wrong

Radiata earns roughly **20–26 NZU/ha/yr**; at NZ$55–80/NZU that is **NZ$1,100–2,080/ha/yr**.
`[E]`

**The naive calculation — 8,000 ha × NZ$1,500 = NZ$12 m/yr — is wrong by one to two orders of
magnitude**, for four independent reasons:

1. Above the treeline **nothing meets the forest definition**;
2. The plantable land **is** the farmland — you cannot do both;
3. Planting **destroys the ski amenity** you bought the property for;
4. It **creates the wilding-conifer liability** you are legally obliged to control.

**Realistic: 300 ha conservatively planted = NZ$330–624k/yr.** `[E]`

> **Recommendation: take the native / permanent-forest route as the base case**, despite the
> lower per-hectare yield. No wilding liability, no conflict with the ski terrain, minimal
> establishment cost — **and it strengthens rather than weakens an OIO benefit case.**

**Model the base case at NZU = NZ$0.** Carbon is the largest lever *and* the most volatile —
a 60 % drawdown occurred inside twelve months in 2022–23. **The plan must survive without it.
Treat it as upside, never as the carry.**

### 2. Hunting — the best season-complementary income

| Season | Activity |
|---|---|
| Mar–Apr | The roar (red deer) |
| Apr–Jun | Tahr and chamois rut |
| **Jul–Sep** | **Ski season** |

> **They do not collide.** The same lodge, tracks, vehicles and guides serve both, **roughly
> doubling fixed-asset utilisation.** `[E]`

**Specify the Phase 1 hut for dual-season use from the outset** — this is a design decision
that costs almost nothing at the point of building and is expensive to retrofit.

Estimated net: **NZ$65–250k/yr.** `[E]`

### 3. Cat-skiing has a hard ceiling

**One snowcat carries 10–12 guests per day. Full stop.** That is a physical constraint, not a
marketing one.

- Central case: **NZ$629k gross, roughly NZ$200–250k net.** `[E]`

> Cat-skiing can make the **ski asset** roughly self-funding. **It cannot justify the land
> purchase.** Any model that relies on ski revenue to service the acquisition is wrong.

### 4. The overlooked line

**Telecommunications / repeater site leases: NZ$4–25k/yr.** Check the vendor's records —
agreements may already exist and be assignable. Small, but it is free money against a rates
bill. `[E]`

## Country ranking on economics

| | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Chile** | **The low-cost-to-hold answer.** Carry 2–4× cheaper, cheapest labour, open to foreign buyers — and Model 1 (grazing leased out, minimal staff, private skiing) is **actually permitted there** |
| **New Zealand** | **The low-risk / income-offset answer.** ACC liability bar, carbon, hunting, and a deep pool of guiding talent — at a premium of roughly **NZ$250–600k/yr** plus a hard, uncertain consent |
| **Japan** | **De-prioritise on availability.** Carrying costs are unremarkable, but assembling 8,000 contiguous private alpine hectares is likely impossible |

## Two leads not yet modelled

The tax workstream flagged that **Sweden exempts productive forest land from property tax** and
**Scotland exempts agricultural land from rates.** Both could be cheaper to hold than anything
modelled here. Neither jurisdiction survives on exclusivity grounds — but if the client ever
relaxes the private-access requirement, the holding economics deserve a second look. `[U]`
