Detail supporting `03-country-briefs/new-zealand.md`. The headline conclusion is there;
this is the mechanism.

## 1. The 2026 reform, and why it does not help

The **Overseas Investment (National Interest Test and Other Matters) Amendment Act 2025**
(Public Act 2025/81) came into force **6 March 2026** (Commencement Order SL 2026/2). It
replaces the investor / benefit / national-interest tests with a **single consolidated
national interest test** and imposes a **15-working-day** decision duty for most sensitive
land and business assets. `[P1/P2 — corroborated across Bell Gully, Minter Ellison, Russell
McVeagh, Chapman Tripp, Simpson Grierson, Buddle Findlay, PwC NZ]`

**Farm land, residential land and fishing quota were expressly carved out** and continue under
the old, harsher arrangements, including the advertising regime. A high-country station is
farm land. **The buyer faces the pre-2026 regime in full.** `[P2]`

The new pathway for investor-visa holders is **residential-only** (one home ≥ NZ$5 m) and is
useless for this mandate. `[P2]`

## 2. Consent is unavoidable, and cannot be structured around

- **Sensitive land** includes non-urban land **> 5 ha**; land > 0.4 ha adjoining a DOC reserve,
  lake or park; > 0.2 ha adjoining foreshore; conservation land > 0.4 ha. `[P1]`
- **Adjoining and associated land is aggregated** (s 8(4)) — subdividing under the threshold
  does not work. `[P1]`
- **A leasehold of ≥ 3 years including renewals is an interest in land**, so acquiring a
  *pastoral lease* is screened exactly as a freehold purchase is. `[P1]`
- An entity is an **overseas person** if overseas persons hold **≥ 25 %** of securities, or
  control board composition, or hold ≥ 25 % of voting power. An NZ company changes nothing
  unless the client holds genuinely under 25 % **on all three limbs** — i.e. gives up control.
  `[P1]`

## 3. The farm land benefit test, and why the private-ski thesis fails it

Seven statutory factors under s 17: economic; natural environment; public access; historic
heritage; advancing government policy; **oversight and participation by New Zealanders**;
other consequential benefits. `[P1]`

For farm land > 5 ha, **since 24 November 2021** Ministers **must give high relative importance
to (a) economic benefits and (b) New Zealand oversight/participation**, and the applicant must
show a **"substantial benefit"** under one of those two, **substantially higher than the
current state**. The old hypothetical-alternative-purchaser counterfactual was removed — the
comparison is now against **the station as it operates today**. Negatives net off against
directly comparable positives. `[P1/P2]`

> A private mountain scores near zero on both high-importance factors, and is likely
> **negative** on economics if stock numbers are cut. The two factors where a low-impact
> private owner genuinely shines — natural environment and historic heritage — are precisely
> the ones **not** given high relative importance. `[E]`

### Precedent: public access is the currency the OIO charges

**Hunter Valley Station** (6,468 ha, Lake Hāwea; Orange Lakes (NZ) Ltd) was consented on
continued sheep and cattle farming plus capital investment — with conditions extracting
**public access**: legalising the road to DOC's Kidds Bush campsite, supporting a Sawyer Burn
Track easement, and access to Sentinel Peak. Only 2 of 9 Walking Access Commission
recommendations were adopted, and it still drew litigation threats. `[P2]`

**Motatapu / Soho**: the 29 km Motatapu Track was an OIO condition; 23 tracks were built or
upgraded; QEII covenants followed. `[P2]`

**A private, access-restricted ski club is the direct opposite of what the regulator charges
for consent.** `[E]`

## 4. The one clean structure: s 16 "intention to reside"

**This is the single most useful finding in the New Zealand workstream.**

**Section 16 of the Overseas Investment Act 2005** allows consent for **non-residential
sensitive land** where the applicant is a New Zealand citizen, is ordinarily resident, **or
intends to reside in New Zealand indefinitely.** `[P1]`

Reporting the OIO's own position on **Mt White Station** (39,337 ha pastoral lease + 678 ha
freehold, Arthur's Pass; Lukáš Trávníček / Southern Ranges Ltd), RNZ recorded that applicants
on this pathway *"are not required to show benefits to New Zealand, including walking access,
so the OIO was not in a position to make access a condition of consent."* `[P2]`

> **On this pathway the benefit test does not apply, and public access cannot be imposed as a
> condition.** It is the only route that permits the client's actual thesis — low intensity,
> private access — to be consented in New Zealand.

**The price is emigration, and it is not nominal:**

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Visa | Residence class, or an entrepreneur work visa |
| Evidence | Documented plans — LINZ states "a simple assertion… is not likely to be enough" |
| Move | To New Zealand **within 12 months** of consent |
| Become ordinarily resident | **Within 2 years**: permanent resident visa + resided 12 months + **183 days present** + **New Zealand tax resident** |
| Failure | **Forced sale** |
| Tax consequence | NZ tax residence means **worldwide income taxed at up to 39 %** |

**The Active Investor Plus visa is not a substitute.** Growth (NZ$5 m / 36 months, 21 days
presence) or Balanced (NZ$10 m / 60 months, 105 days); from 1 June 2026 up to 20 % of Growth
may be philanthropy. The land purchase is **not** an acceptable AIP investment — it is on top.
And AIP's 21-day presence requirement is deliberately incompatible with the 183 days needed
for tax residence. **AIP supplies the visa limb only.** `[P2]`

## 5. A pastoral lease is a licence to graze, not a right to build

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Term | 33 years, with a **perpetual right of renewal** |
| Rent | Reviewed every 11 years on **stock-carrying capacity** — very cheap for the area |
| Rights | Exclusive possession and quiet enjoyment |
| **Restriction** | Land **may only be used for pastoral farming** |
| Requires Commissioner of Crown Lands consent | Buildings, infrastructure, soil disturbance, forestry, **and commercial recreation** — with **mandatory DOC consultation** |
| Recreation | LINZ states the CCL grants **recreation permits for tourism, hunting, ski fields or filming** under **s 66A Land Act 1948 / LINZS45002**. Published permit conditions routinely prohibit structures and restrict earthworks |
| Transfer | Needs CCL consent; the CCL **must weigh any past unreasonable refusal of public access** |

> **Do not pay a premium for "skiable terrain" on leasehold. It is an option the Crown can
> decline.** The cheap rent exists *because* the Crown controls non-pastoral use. `[E]`

Scale, for context: tenure review freeholded **346,000 ha (53 % of land disposed)** and moved
**~300,000+ ha to conservation**, creating five new parks. Leases fell from **303 to 171**,
still covering **1.2 m ha (~5 % of New Zealand)**. The freehold boundary was drawn where
"significant inherent values" begin — **which is exactly where skiable terrain begins.** `[P1/P2]`

**Implication for search: target the rare pre-1998 freehold that never went through tenure
review.** Verify against the LINZ record title and the DOC estate boundary, never a marketing
map.

## 6. Planning — Outstanding Natural Landscape is the wall

- **QLDC** is the clearest regime: **Ski Area Sub-Zones are expressly excluded** from ONL, ONF
  and Rural Character categories. Outside a sub-zone you are in an **Outstanding Natural
  Landscape — a s 6 RMA matter of national importance.** Creating a new sub-zone means a
  **private plan change**: years, notified, appealable. `[P1]`
- **Mackenzie** applies PC13 ONL provisions basin-wide. `[P2]`
- Under **s 9 RMA**, informal ski touring and snowcat use on existing tracks is likely
  permitted. What triggers consent is **buildings, earthworks, vegetation clearance, water
  take and discharge** — needed by any real operation, and tightly controlled in an ONL
  **regardless of whether the use is commercial.** `[E]`
- **RMA reform is live**: the Natural Environment Bill and Planning Bill were introduced in
  December 2025, submissions heard February–March 2026, passage intended mid-2026. `[P2]`

## 7. Two genuine New Zealand advantages

1. **ACC bars compensatory personal-injury suits** (exemplary damages only). The US-style
   skier-liability exposure essentially does not exist. Residual risk is a WorkSafe
   prosecution, not a tort claim. `[P1]` **This is a material structural advantage over the
   United States and should weigh in any final comparison.**
2. **WorkSafe policy**: HSWA applies to recreational access only where land is affected by
   work or is a workplace; a landowner is **not responsible for the risks of the recreation
   itself**. But put a paid driver in a snowcat carrying guests and you are a PCBU running
   that undertaking. **The Adventure Activities Regulations 2016 exclude clubs and
   associations of clubs** — the Craigieburn / Broken River / Temple Basin club-field model is
   the cheapest lawful operating wrapper in the country. `[P1]`

### Counter-warning: "simple surface lifts" are not unregulated

Rope tows, platters and T-bars are **passenger ropeways** under the **PECPR Regulations 1999**,
ACOP 1998 and **NZS 8635:2018**, with certified inspection. `[P1]`

> **The regulatory step-change is `no lift → any lift`, not `surface lift → chairlift`.**
> This materially strengthens the case for a Phase 1 built on snowcat, snowmobile and touring
> only — in New Zealand and, as it turns out, in Japan for the same structural reason.

## 9. Least-bad structures, ranked

1. **Emigrate — s 16 intention-to-reside.** The only route avoiding both the benefit test and
   access conditions. Mt White is the precedent. Requires real relocation and NZ tax residence.
2. **Build a commercial benefit case.** Farming intensification plus a *paying* alpine tourism
   venture, an NZ operating company with real NZ equity and directors, a QEII covenant, and an
   expectation of conceding registered public access. It works — but it is not the thesis.
3. **Sub-25 % joint venture.** Outside the regime, but with no control. **Do not use nominees.**
4. **Avoid:** buying leasehold expecting to build, and buying an existing NZ ski area expecting
   to own a mountain. Most sit on DOC concessions under Part 3B, Conservation Act 1987 —
   time-limited (typically 30 years; the Remarkables 40), notifiable, renewal-risked, and
   **not ownership.**
