# Country brief — Japan

**Verdict: the cheapest entry prices in the study, on the hardest terrain to actually own.**

Japan has no nationality bar on freehold, distressed ski assets changing hands for the price
of a car, and the deepest reliable snow of any candidate jurisdiction. It also has a land
tenure structure that keeps almost all genuine alpine terrain out of private hands, and a
foreign-ownership rule review due to report in the middle of the acquisition window.

---

## 1. The structural finding

> **In Japan, private freehold title containing 500 m or more of skiable vertical is close to
> non-existent — and where it exists, it is not cheap.** `[E]`

The reasons are structural, not cyclical:

1. **Above roughly 1,000–1,500 m in Honshū's main ranges, land is overwhelmingly 国有林
   (national forest, administered by 林野庁 Forestry Agency).** It is not for sale. National
   forest is ~30 % of Japan's forest area and is concentrated in exactly the steep, remote,
   high country this mandate wants. `[P1]`
2. Where high ground is *not* national forest, it is typically held by a **財産区** (property
   ward), a **生産森林組合** (production forest cooperative), old village commons (**部落有林**),
   or legacy corporate forest estates (王子, 日本製紙, 住友林業, 三井物産フォレスト). None of
   these are ordinary open-market vendors. `[E]`
3. **Most Japanese ski areas therefore do not own their slopes.** They hold 貸付 or 分収林
   agreements with the Forestry Agency, or leases from a 財産区 or village common. A live
   listing found in this study — an operating Nagano area with **2 lifts and 10 courses** —
   states plainly that **the land is leased and the buyer must contract with the landowner
   after transfer.** `[P3]`
4. **国立公園 / 国定公園 特別地域 zoning** overlays most high terrain (八幡平, 鳥海, 蔵王,
   栗駒, 妙高, 乗鞍, 白山, 大雪), restricting clearing, lift construction and new structures
   **even on privately owned parcels**. The environment ministry's guidance on ski areas in
   national parks requires preserved green cover of **at least 70 %** of the designated ski
   area. `[P1]`
5. **保安林 (protection forest) designation** independently bars felling and earthworks on a
   large share of steep private forest — and **does not appear on a casual listing**. `[P1]`

## 2. Where the thesis *can* work: Hokkaido, at 250–450 m of vertical

In Hokkaido the effective snow line is at sea level, land is far more privately and
municipally held than in Honshū, and 600–1,100 m private blocks genuinely exist. The trade is
**less vertical, better and colder snow, cleaner title.** `[E]`

Candidate belts:

| Belt | Character |
|---|---|
| 余市 / 赤井川 / 喜茂別 / 留寿都 (Niseko–Kiroro snow belt) | Village floors 200–400 m; caldera rims and independent cones 700–1,100 m |
| 富良野 / 南富良野 / 大雪山麓 | 400–700 m bases; private former 開拓 (settlement) blocks exist |
| 日高山脈西麓 | Large private forest, low prices — but access and avalanche exposure are serious |

**The calibration point that makes this less discouraging than it sounds:** typical Japanese
commercial ski areas run **300–700 m of vertical**, and only a handful exceed 800 m
(八方尾根 ~1,070 m, 蔵王 ~880 m, ニセコ全山 ~900 m). **A private 300–400 m freehold drop is
therefore not far below a *median* Japanese ski area.** For a snowcat-and-touring model, that
is a workable mountain. `[E]`

## 3. The 地目 filter — the single most useful screening rule in Japan

The **地目** (registered land category) on the title determines whether the client can buy at
all:

| 地目 | Treatment | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **田 / 畑** (farmland) | Acquisition requires 農業委員会 permission under **農地法第3条**. For a non-resident foreign individual with no farming plan and no local operating record this is **effectively closed** | ❌ Avoid |
| **山林 / 原野 / 牧場** | **Not 農地法 land.** Acquisition unrestricted, including by a foreign individual | ✅ Buy here |

> **Practical rule: buy 山林 or 原野 and graze it, rather than buying a 牧場 that is registered
> as farmland.** Grazing does not require the land to be 農地. This is a hard screening filter
> that should be applied to every Japanese lead before anything else is checked. `[E]`

This has a direct consequence for the "ranch" leg of the thesis: **the pastoral income in
Japan has to be built on forest-category land, not bought as an existing farm.**

| **山村奥地山地 (remote mountain interior)** | **150–650** | **¥0.45–1.97 m** | **RM 11k–50k** |

The client's working assumption of ¥100–1,000/坪 is **correct, but only for the 山村奥地 and
林業本場 classes** — remote interior mountain forest. It is not achievable near an existing
resort, near a road network, or on land with development potential. `[P2]`

### The municipal pipeline — the most interesting channel in Japan

Japanese municipalities are exiting ski areas at pace, and several are actively seeking
private successors. This is the channel that produces the lowest prices anywhere in the study.

| Asset | Status |
|---|---|
| **糸魚川市 シーサイドバレー + シャルマン火打** (Niigata) | City owns two full ski areas, is studying private transfer, and **enters 公募 (open solicitation) for Seaside Valley in FY2026.** The city spends **¥80–90 m/yr** with a ¥39 m/yr management fee — it is *paying* to keep them open, which is the negotiating lever. Charmant Hiuchi is nationally known for deep snow and lift-served off-piste |
| 室蘭市 だんパラ (Hokkaido) | 廃止 scheduled end of FY2026; operator to be dissolved |
| 士別市 あさひ (Hokkaido) | Final ski day 22 Mar 2026; **abolished 30 June 2026**; site's future use not announced |
| 野麦峠 (Matsumoto, Nagano) | Abolished 2026; the city was bearing ~¥100 m/yr; a local volunteer group is pursuing a restart |
| 魚沼市 (Niigata) | Ends 指定管理者制度 at end of FY2027 |
| 柏崎市 (Niigata) | Will **not replace** ageing groomers; abolition anticipated when the groomer fails |
| 藤里町 (Akita) | Abolished Sep 2025 after 2023 rain collapsed part of the slope |
| **村上市ぶどうスキー場 → ぶどうスノーリゾート** | ⭐ **The precedent.** Closed by the city 9 Mar 2025, reopened for 2025/26 by 株式会社シンクファースト. This is the working template for the "take over a closing municipal ski area" play |

> **Correction to a working assumption in this study:** the "無償譲渡 / free transfer" element
> of the Murakami case could not be confirmed, and the asset is 村上市ぶどうスキー場 in Niigata
> — not 「ぶどうの丘」, which is an unrelated winery facility in 甲州市, Yamanashi. Treat the
> free-transfer element as **unverified**. `[U]`

**The gating question on every municipal asset is the same:** does the city's title include
the slopes, or is the mountain 国有林 / 財産区 land? For a Niigata mountain at that elevation a
large national-forest component is likely. **Resolve tenure before anything else.** `[E]`

The HANAZONO datapoint is the one to keep in mind: **Japanese ski assets have historically
traded at extreme discounts to replacement cost.** That is the structural opportunity — and
the reason to be sceptical of any asset priced near its build cost. `[P2]`

## 7. The regulatory clock — a live timing risk

Japan currently imposes **no nationality bar on freehold ownership**. But:

- The Cabinet Secretariat's **外国人による土地取得等のルールの在り方検討会** (Study Group on
  Rules for Land Acquisition by Foreigners) is due to compile a **骨格 (framework) in summer
  2026** — i.e. now. `[P1]`
- **Nationality declaration at property registration** is planned from **FY2026**: individuals
  would declare nationality, and corporations the nationality of major shareholders. `[P2]`
- The **重要土地等調査法** (2022) already designates 注視区域 and 特別注視区域 around defence
  facilities and border islands, with **prior notification** required for transactions in
  special zones. Several Japanese ski regions — Hokkaido, Aomori, Niigata, Nagano — contain
  such areas. `[P1]`
- The **森林法** requires **post-acquisition notification within 90 days** to the municipal
  mayor for any new owner of forest land (第10条の7の2). Nationality is not currently a
  notified item. `[P1]`

> **This is not a reason to avoid Japan, but it is a reason to move deliberately and to build
> the rule review into the transaction timetable.** A buyer who signs in late 2026 without
> tracking the framework outcome is taking an avoidable risk.

## 8. Lifts — the same trap as New Zealand

Rope tows, T-bars and platters are **特殊索道** regulated under the **鉄道事業法** (Railway
Business Act) and its enforcement regulations — the same statute that governs ropeways and
gondolas. The pre-1997 classification put T-bars, rope tows, J-bars and poma lifts in Type C
of 特殊索道. `[P1]`

> **The regulatory step-change in Japan, as in New Zealand, is `no lift → any lift`.** A Phase 1
> built on snowcat, snowmobile and touring avoids the 索道 regime — MLIT permission per
> installation, an 索道技術管理者, a 安全統括管理者, annual inspection and a published safety
> report — entirely. `[P1]`

Whether a genuinely private, non-paying **専用索道** falls outside 索道事業 could not be
verified, and the expectation is that **bundling lift access into a paid stay converts it into
a regulated business.** `[U]` Either way the conclusion is the same: **the regulatory case for
"snowcat-only, indefinitely" in Japan is very strong.**

Full detail, including the negligible holding costs, the 農地法 block on ranching, the 保安林
fork, the inheritance-tax exposure and the 車馬等乗入れ規制区域 risk that would kill the snowcat
model outright, is in `japan-regulatory.md`.
