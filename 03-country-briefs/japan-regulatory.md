# Japan — regulatory annex

Detail supporting `03-country-briefs/japan.md`.

> **Headline:** buying a Japanese mountain as a Malaysian non-resident is unusually easy.
> Buying a Japanese mountain that is actually *alpine* is unusually hard. And buying a
> Japanese *ranch* is close to impossible.

---

## 1. Foreign ownership — genuinely open

No restriction on freehold acquisition by foreign individuals or companies. **No consent, no
residency, no visa, no local partner, no price floor.** This is materially more open than
New Zealand (OIA), Australia (FIRB) or Switzerland (Lex Koller). `[P1]`

The **外国人土地法 (Alien Land Law) of 1925** survives on the statute book with a reciprocity
clause, but the Imperial Ordinance required to activate it **was never enacted**. It is a dead
letter. `[P1/E]`

> **A caution worth stating plainly.** If reciprocity were ever switched on, **Malaysia is a
> plausible target**, because Malaysia itself heavily restricts foreign land acquisition.
> Vested rights would be constitutionally protected (Art. 29) and a nationality-based ban
> would strain Japan's WTO/GATS and OECD commitments — but **this argues for completing early.
> Grandfathering has real value here.** `[E]`

## 2. The 2026 tightening — live, but not yet law

Two separate tracks are running:

| Track | Status |
|---|---|
| **内閣官房 expert panel** on rules for land acquisition by foreigners | First meeting **4 March 2026**. **Basic policy / 骨格 targeted for summer 2026**, then a legislative 骨子案. The panel's own central unresolved question is whether restricting *only* foreigners is lawful under Japan's international agreements `[P1]` |
| **Nationality declaration on registration** | **FY2026** (one specialist source says **October 2026**). Applies to **all applicants, Japanese included**; nationality is **not** printed on the public register. **Forest land is expressly in scope** `[P2]` |

**Assessment:** low probability of retroactive divestment; material probability of new
**pre-acquisition screening on future deals**. Remote, low-value forestry land is the least
likely category to be captured. **Net: buy before the screening regime lands.** `[E]`

## 3. What Japan makes easy

### Holding cost is negligible — the strongest single fact in the Japanese case

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| 固定資産税 on 山林 | **Thousands of yen per hectare per year** |
| Municipal exemption | **¥300,000 threshold** — many forest holdings pay **zero** |
| 都市計画税 | Does not reach land outside 市街化区域 |
| **保安林** | **Exempt from 固定資産税, 不動産取得税 and 特別土地保有税 entirely** |

> **A 500 ha forest plausibly costs ¥1.5–3.5 m/yr (≈ RM 38,100–88,900), and possibly
> nothing at all.** `[P1/P2]` Compare this with New Zealand high-country rates plus wilding
> conifer and rabbit control obligations. **On carrying cost, Japan wins the study outright.**

### Phase 1 needs almost no permits

Snowcat, snowmobile and ski touring **on your own land, using existing roads, with thinning
kept under 1 ha**, triggers: **no ropeway licence, no forest development permission, no water
permit, and no park structure permission.** `[E]`

This is the single clearest regulatory endorsement of the client's Phase 1 design found
anywhere in the study.

### Other easements

- **Guiding alone sits outside 旅行業法** registration — guide services that do not arrange
  transport or accommodation. `[P2]`
- **Lodging works**: 旅館業法 **簡易宿所** gives 365-day operation; **農家民宿** certification
  relaxes building and fire requirements *and* escapes the 180-day minpaku cap. `[P2]`
- **Filings are filings, not consents**: 森林法 **90-day** owner notification; 国土利用計画法
  post-transaction notification within **2 weeks** for >10,000 m² outside city planning areas.
- **Non-resident conveyancing is a solved problem**: a notarised **宣誓供述書** (affidavit)
  substitutes for 印鑑証明書 and 住民票. `[P1 — Ministry of Justice]`
- **Parks are 地域制公園.** Designation overlays private title — **private land inside a
  National Park is normal and lawful.** Only *use* is gated. `[P1]`
- **国立公園 ski projects require 保存緑地率 ≥ 70 %** — only 30 % of a project area may be
  cleared. Notably, **this matches the client's low-clearing Phase 1 design better than it
  matches a conventional resort.** `[P1]`

## 4. The four real obstacles

### (a) The terrain itself — the deepest structural problem

National forest is **7.58 m ha = ~20 % of Japan's land area and ~30 % of its forest**, and the
Forestry Agency states it is concentrated in **"steep remote interior mountains and river
headwaters."** `[P1]`

> **The state owns, by design, exactly the terrain this mandate wants.** What is privately held
> and for sale skews to **mid-elevation plantation forest on workable slopes**. Expect rolling
> 300–1,200 m forested terrain, or a defunct ski area — **not a private alpine summit.**

### (b) Ranching is effectively blocked

**農地法第3条 governs both 農地 and 採草放牧地** (grazing land). The 2023 abolition of the
**下限面積要件** (minimum area requirement) is widely misread: the substantive tests survive —

- **全部効率利用** (efficient use of the whole holding), and
- **常時従事** — a benchmark of roughly **150 days per year of personal farm work**,

applied **irrespective of nationality** and judged by a locally-composed 農業委員会. **A sale
without permission is void.** One practitioner source states flatly that a person living abroad
cannot buy farmland. `[P1/E]`

> **Recommendation: screen for 山林 / 原野 only, exclude any 農地 parcel, and drop livestock
> from the Japanese version of the thesis** — or secure a 農地法第5条 conversion before
> completion. The "ranch" leg of the thesis does not survive contact with Japanese farmland
> law in its intended form.

### (c) 保安林 is a fork in the road

Protection-forest designation gives **zero tax** — but **felling is restricted and 土地の形質の
変更 (alteration of land form or quality) requires the prefectural governor's permission.**
`[P1]`

> **A 100 %-保安林 mountain is a touring asset forever.** If the plan needs the option to cut
> trails, you need non-保安林 forest — and you will pay tax on it. **Establish 保安林 status
> parcel by parcel before offering.** It does not appear on a casual listing.

### (d) Any lift is a step-change

**索道事業** under the 鉄道事業法 is defined as transporting **"in response to the demand of
others"**, and requires **MLIT ministerial permission per installation**, an **索道技術管理者**,
a **安全統括管理者**, annual inspection and a published safety report. `[P1]`

**Rope tows and T-bars are historically inside the 索道 family (旧丙種特殊索道) — do not assume
they are unregulated.** `[P1]`

**専用索道** (own-use ropeway) is defined in law, and a genuinely private, non-paying
installation should fall outside 索道事業 — **but this could not be verified, and the
expectation is that bundling lift access into a paid stay converts it into a 索道事業.** `[U]`

> **This is the single most important open question in the Japanese file.** Its practical
> answer, however, points the same way regardless: the no-lift design removes an entire
> regulatory regime, a scarce salaried specialist hire, and the largest recurring OPEX line.
> **The regulatory case for "snowcat-only, indefinitely" in Japan is very strong.**

## 5. Two risks the brief did not ask about, ranked high

### Inheritance tax — potentially the largest tax in the structure

Japan taxes the **Japan-situs assets of a non-resident foreign decedent at rates up to 55 %.**
`[U — unverified, but the exposure is real enough to price]`

**Malaysia has no inheritance tax, so this risk is invisible from the client's side.** It is
plausibly the single largest fiscal exposure in a Japanese acquisition and **must be taken to
Japanese international tax counsel before purchase, not after.**

### 車馬等乗入れ規制区域 — the rule that would kill the snowcat model

Vehicle-entry restriction zones in national, quasi-national and prefectural parks carry
**up to 1 year imprisonment or a ¥1 m fine.** `[P1]`

> **A designation over the target terrain destroys the entire Phase 1 operating model — and a
> seller has no incentive to mention it.** Hokkaido publishes the maps. **This belongs on the
> pre-offer checklist as a deal-killer check, not a detail.**

## 6. Other confirmed items

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| **重要土地等調査法** | ~1,000 m radius around defence facilities; **特別注視区域 requires pre-contract notification for land ≥ 200 m²**. A filing, not a consent. Zone lists could not be retrieved `[U]`. Structurally these are small discrete zones — deprioritise parcels within 1 km of SDF or US Forces sites |
| **Capital gains** | **39.63 % at ≤ 5 years' holding vs 20.315 % beyond** — a real structuring cliff. A buyer withholds **10.21 % of gross** when purchasing from a non-resident seller |
| **国有林野 leasing** | Exists, and ski-slope sites are an established lease category. **Freehold base + leased upper mountain is how real Japanese resorts are structured.** Term, rent formula and foreign eligibility all `[U]` |
| **Finance** | **Assume no mortgage finance for a non-resident. Model all-cash.** |

> **The 国有林野 lease point may be the most useful unexplored idea in the Japanese file.** If
> the Forestry Agency will lease upper-mountain national forest to a foreign-owned entity on a
> long term, then the Japanese answer becomes *freehold base + leased vertical* — which
> satisfies "controls the mountain" even where it cannot satisfy "owns the peak". **A free
> enquiry to the regional 森林管理局 would settle it.**

## 7. Top open items

1. **Is a private rope tow exempt from the 鉄道事業法 — and does taking paying guests destroy
   the exemption?** → 地方運輸局 鉄道部
2. **Inheritance-tax exposure** → Japanese international tax counsel, **pre-purchase**
3. **国有林野 lease terms and foreign eligibility** → 森林管理局
4. **重要土地等調査法 zone maps** for Hokkaido, Aomori, Niigata, Nagano
5. **Liability insurance quote for guided snowcat skiing** — an uninsurable operation is not a
   business
6. **土砂災害警戒区域 / 雪崩危険箇所 screening** — the last two searches on this were refused by
   the quota
7. **Abandoned-lift decommissioning liability** on any 廃止スキー場 — an inherited removal cost
   that could exceed the purchase price
