# Mandate

**Client:** Malaysia-based private buyer. All values reported in **MYR**.

**Objective:** Identify a small number of genuinely viable acquisition candidates for a
**ranch / working rural landholding that carries a private ski mountain inside its own title** —
not a list of ordinary ski properties.

## The investment thesis, restated as testable propositions

The client's thesis contains six claims. Each is treated below as a proposition that the
research must either support or falsify for a given property. A candidate is only
"viable" if all six hold simultaneously.

| # | Thesis claim | Testable proposition |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Buy the land primarily as a ranch / farm / forestry / low-cost rural asset | The asset is priced as rural land, not as ski real estate or a resort business |
| T2 | Ensure the property includes skiable alpine terrain | The **registered title** includes contiguous skiable terrain with meaningful vertical |
| T3 | Keep OPEX and CAPEX extremely low | Annual carrying cost is a small, predictable number and Phase 1 needs no fixed plant |
| T4 | Start with a private ski mountain using existing roads, snowcats, snowmobiles, touring, or simple surface lifts | Private, non-commercial skiing is lawful without a lift licence, concession or resort consent |
| T5 | Ranching, farming, forestry, hunting, accommodation offset holding costs | Verifiable rural income exists or can be established, at a scale that materially offsets carry |
| T6 | Expand gradually; commercial resort only if justified | The planning regime permits staged intensification, and adjoining land can be assembled |

## The non-negotiable

> *"whether the legal title actually includes the alpine peaks and ski terrain"*

This is the screen that eliminates most of the world's apparently attractive ski assets.
The global default is that **high alpine terrain is public land**: Crown land in New Zealand
and Australia, 国有林 national forest and 国立公園 national park in Japan, USFS/BLM in the
United States, Crown land in Canada, *terrenos fiscales* in parts of the Andes, and state
forest across most of Europe and Central Asia. Ski areas on that land operate under
**concessions, permits, leases and tenures — not ownership.**

Consequently the research is organised first around **tenure**, and only second around
snow, price and terrain. A perfect mountain that cannot be owned fails at gate one.

## What "acquisition-grade" means here

1. **Broker marketing is not evidence.** Every listing claim is tagged as such and, where it
   is decision-relevant, is either corroborated from an independent source or flagged as
   unverified.
2. **Every material number carries a source and a date.** Prices are quoted in the original
   currency with the date of the quote, then converted at a single published FX basis.
3. **The report states what it could not verify.** Remote research cannot substitute for a
   title search, a survey, or a site visit in winter. Section 05 sets out exactly what must
   be ordered before an offer, and from whom.
4. **Negative findings are reported as findings.** Where the thesis fails in a jurisdiction,
   that is the answer, and the reasoning is shown rather than routed around.

## Deliberate exclusions

- Properties whose skiable terrain sits wholly on public land under permit or concession are
  **not** presented as thesis-compliant, however cheap. They appear in `99-rejected/` with
  the reason, and in one case as an instructive comparator.
- Ordinary ski-resort real estate (chalets, lots, apartments, operating resorts sold as
  going concerns at resort multiples) is out of scope by instruction.
- Trophy stations priced as luxury assets are screened out on T1 and T3 even where the
  terrain is excellent; several are recorded as price benchmarks.
