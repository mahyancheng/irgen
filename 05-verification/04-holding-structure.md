# Holding structure: why Cayman is the wrong tool for this asset

The client proposed holding the Japanese land through a **Cayman trust/holding entity** to avoid
inheritance tax, tax generally, and disclosure.

**The instinct is legitimate and the vehicle is a normal one. Applied to this asset it fails on all
three objectives, costs more per year than the asset's entire carrying cost, and makes the
disclosure position materially worse.** The analysis below is not tax advice — it needs a Japanese
税理士 and a Malaysian adviser on the client's actual circumstances — but the load-bearing facts are
verifiable and several of the premises are simply wrong.

---

## Objective 1 — inheritance tax. There are two, and neither bites.

### Malaysia: there is no inheritance tax to avoid

> **Malaysia's estate duty was abolished on 1 November 1991** (Estate Duty Enactment 1941,
> repealed). Malaysia has no death tax, no estate duty, no inheritance tax. `[P1]`

Re-introduction is periodically debated and has not happened. **Whatever the structure is solving,
it is not a Malaysian inheritance tax, because one does not exist.**

### Japan: the tax exists, applies — and this asset sits under the threshold

Japanese-situs assets are **always** subject to Japanese inheritance tax, regardless of the
nationality or residence of the deceased or the heir (制限納税義務者). Directly-held Japanese real
estate is Japanese-situs. On that much the client's concern is well founded. `[P1]`

But:

> **The basic exclusion (基礎控除) is ¥30,000,000 + ¥6,000,000 per statutory heir.** Below that,
> **no tax is owed and no filing is required.** `[P1]`

| | |
|---|---|
| Threshold with one statutory heir | **¥36,000,000** |
| The Akaigawa parcel | **¥25,000,000** |
| Japanese inheritance tax exposure | **Nil, on current facts** |

The offshore-entity route does work in principle — interposing a non-Japanese company converts the
estate asset from Japanese land into foreign shares, which are not Japanese-situs. It is a
recognised planning idea. **It is simply not needed at ¥25 m**, and it would only start to matter if
the Japanese-situs holding grew well past the exclusion.

One caution if it is revisited later: **a trust is not the same analysis as a company.** Japan's
相続税法 contains specific provisions taxing beneficial interests in trusts (信託), and a trust
structure adds complexity to the Japanese analysis rather than removing it. A 税理士 should price
that before anyone assumes a trust behaves like a holding company.

## Objective 2 — tax generally. The entity changes none of it.

Every recurring and transactional Japanese tax on this land is levied on **the owner, whoever the
owner is**:

| Tax | Effect of a Cayman owner |
|---|---|
| 固定資産税 (fixed asset tax, ~1.4%) | **No change.** And on this parcel it may already be ¥0 — 保安林 is exempt, as is forest assessed under ¥300,000. |
| 不動産取得税 (acquisition tax) | **No change.** 保安林 is exempt regardless of owner. |
| 登録免許税 (registration tax on transfer) | **No change.** |
| Capital gains on disposal of Japanese real property | **No change** — Japanese-source income, taxed in Japan whoever sells. |

**A Cayman entity saves nothing on the taxes this asset actually attracts.** It changes the situs of
the *shareholding*, not the taxation of the *land*.

## Objective 3 — disclosure. This is where the premise inverts.

The structure does not reduce disclosure. **It converts a private individual's land purchase into a
flagged foreign-capital forest acquisition, in a dataset the Hokkaido government publishes.**

### Hokkaido publishes exactly this

> **北海道庁 publishes open data titled 「海外資本等による森林取得状況」 — foreign-capital forest
> acquisitions, giving nationality, intended use and area.** 林野庁 runs the parallel national
> survey, 外国資本による森林取得に関する調査. `[P1]`

A Cayman company acquiring 74.4 ha of Hokkaido forest is the exact fact pattern these datasets
exist to record.

### And Hokkaido legislated against this fact pattern first

**北海道水資源の保全に関する条例** — enacted March 2012, in force April 2012, **the first such
ordinance of any Japanese prefecture.** It was passed specifically because foreign capital was
acquiring water-related land and developing it. Inside a designated 水資源保全地域 a land
transaction requires **notification three months before the transaction**, after which the
prefecture consults the municipality and the 水資源保全審議会 and advises the notifying party.
`[P1]`

For a caldera rim — which is, by definition, headwater ground — this is a live risk, and it adds a
quarter to the timeline.

### The rest of the disclosure picture

| Channel | Effect |
|---|---|
| **森林法 notification** | Required within 90 days of acquiring forest land. **From 1 April 2026 the form records nationality.** `[P1]` |
| **国土利用計画法** | Filing required above 1 ha. At 74.4 ha, unambiguously engaged. |
| **登記簿 (property register)** | **Public.** Anyone may order a 登記事項証明書 and read the owner's name and address. A Cayman company there is *more* conspicuous than an individual, not less. |
| **Cayman BOTA 2023** | Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act in force **31 July 2024**, compliance required from **1 January 2025**. Beneficial ownership registers are maintained and accessible to competent authorities. `[P1]` |
| **CRS** | Cayman and Malaysia both participate. Cayman financial institutions report to the account holder's jurisdiction of tax residence — **i.e. to Malaysia**. |
| **Economic substance** | Cayman entities must file annual ES notifications. |

**Cayman stopped being an opacity jurisdiction some years ago.** Post-CRS and post-BOTA, what the
structure buys is administrative burden, not privacy.

## Cost: the structure is larger than the asset it protects

| Item | Estimate `[E]` |
|---|---|
| Cayman company formation | US$1,500–5,000 |
| Trust establishment (legal) | US$10,000–30,000 |
| **Setup total** | **US$20,000–50,000** |
| Registered office / agent | US$1,500–3,000 /yr |
| Government fee | ~US$900+ /yr |
| **Licensed professional trustee** | **US$5,000–15,000+ /yr** — the dominant cost |
| ES and CRS filings, Japanese counsel for a foreign-entity acquisition | additional |
| **Running total** | **US$10,000–25,000 /yr** |

Against an asset of **MYR 635,000 ≈ US$155,000**:

> **The structure would cost roughly 7–16% of the asset's value every year — to hold land whose own
> property tax may be ¥0.**

That is not a close call. It inverts the entire thesis, which is built on near-zero carrying cost.

## The practical risk nobody costs: it may kill the deal

The vendor is **a private individual disposing of inherited land through a classifieds site**. A
rural Japanese seller and their 司法書士 handle offshore corporate buyers rarely or never. Offering
to buy through a Cayman entity introduces unfamiliarity, delay, and — given the political salience
of foreign forest acquisition in Hokkaido — a reason to prefer another buyer.

**The cheapest, fastest, least conspicuous purchaser of this parcel is a named individual.**

## What the client is actually sensing, and the cheap fix

There *is* a real problem here, and it is not tax. It is **cross-border probate friction**.

Japan's 法の適用に関する通則法 (Act on General Rules for Application of Laws) art. 36 refers
succession to the national law of the deceased — so a Malaysian's succession would be governed by
Malaysian law. But the **Japanese registry still has to be satisfied**, which means producing
Malaysian succession documents, translated and legalised, to a Japanese 法務局. That is genuinely
slow and genuinely painful for heirs.

> **The fix is a Japanese-law will (遺言) covering the Japanese asset only** — ideally a
> 公正証書遺言 executed before a 公証人. Order of ¥100,000–300,000 `[E]`.
>
> **It solves most of what the client wants, for roughly 1% of the cost of the trust,** and it does
> not touch the Malaysian estate, create a foreign-capital filing, or complicate the purchase.

## If an entity is still wanted later

Not Cayman. A **Japanese 合同会社 (GK)** — roughly ¥60,000–100,000 to form `[E]`, no minimum
capital, and an entirely ordinary counterparty for a vendor and a 司法書士. It gives liability
ring-fencing and makes later co-investment or partial transfer straightforward.

It does **not** change situs (GK equity is Japanese-situs), but situs is not the problem at this
value. Note also that foreign ownership of the GK may still surface in the 海外資本等 forest data,
so it is not a privacy device either.

**Recommendation: buy in the client's own name, add a Japanese will, and revisit the structure only
if the Japanese-situs holding grows past the ¥36 m exclusion.**
