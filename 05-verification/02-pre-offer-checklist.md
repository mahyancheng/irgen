# Pre-offer verification checklist

Ordered by deal-killing potential. Nothing below is optional, and items 1–6 should be settled
before any money is spent on legal work.

## Tier 1 — deal-killers. Settle these first.

| # | Item | Where | Applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Does the registered title reach the summit?** Order the record of title and survey plan, overlay the parcel polygon on a DEM, and read the highest contour the title actually contains | LINZ / 法務局 / Conservador / county recorder | All |
| 2 | **Freehold vs leasehold vs concession — decompose the marketed area** into its components with the area of each | Registry | All |
| 3 | **Malaysian outward-investment capacity.** A resident individual **with domestic ringgit borrowing** is reportedly limited to **RM 1 m per calendar year** of foreign-currency investment; without such borrowing there is no limit `[U — bnm.gov.my unreachable]` | **Client's own bank, in writing** | All |
| 4 | **Foreign-ownership consent** — is it required, on what test, and what is the realistic grant probability? | OIO (NZ) / n/a (Japan, Chile) / state statute (US) | All |
| 5 | **Encumbrances that survive sale** — QEII covenants (NZ), 保安林 and park zoning (Japan), *tierra indígena* and mining concessions (Chile), severed minerals (US), *renskötselrätt* (Sweden) | Registry + agency | All |
| 6 | **Vehicle-access prohibitions over the ski terrain** — 車馬等乗入れ規制区域 (Japan) and equivalents. A designation here ends the snowcat model | 環境省 / prefecture | Japan especially |

## Tier 2 — pricing and feasibility

| # | Item |
|---|---|
| 7 | **Annual holding cost, evidenced** — the actual rates/tax notice for the last three years, not an estimate |
| 8 | **Water rights** — do they exist, are they registered, do they transfer with the land, and are they adequate for stock, lodge and (if ever) snowmaking? |
| 9 | **Road access** — legal right of access, not just physical. Formed to what standard, maintained by whom, snow-cleared by whom |
| 10 | **Power, communications** — grid distance and connection cost, or the off-grid design |
| 11 | **Existing income, evidenced** — three years of audited accounts, stock reconciliation, and the terms of any grazing, forestry, hunting or accommodation arrangement |
| 12 | **Snow record** — an independent station series, plus a satellite snow-cover-duration curve by elevation band. **Never the broker's snowfall figure** |
| 13 | **Avalanche exposure** — terrain classification over the intended operating area, and whether the plan can stay below 30° |
| 14 | **Planning status** — zoning, landscape overlays (ONL in NZ), and what Phase 1 actually requires by way of consent |
| 15 | **Lift regulation** — confirm whether the intended Phase 1 avoids the ropeway regime entirely (it should) |
| 16 | **Liability insurance** — obtain an actual quotation for the intended operating mode. **An uninsurable operation is not a business.** This is a go/no-go input, not a detail |

## Tier 3 — structuring and exit

| # | Item |
|---|---|
| 17 | **Inheritance and estate tax exposure.** Japan reaches Japan-situs assets at **up to 55 %**, and the Malaysia–Japan treaty is income-tax only. The US taxes US-situs real property at **40 % above a US$60,000 exemption** with **no Malaysia–US estate treaty**. **New Zealand and Sweden: nil.** `[U — verify with counsel]` |
| 18 | **Holding vehicle.** Individuals are outside Malaysia's capital gains tax; a **Malaysian company would drag the foreign gain into Malaysian CGT on remittance.** Sweden charges 1.7 % transfer tax to an individual vs 4.4 % to a company. **The default is to hold personally** `[U]` |
| 19 | **Exit withholding** — US FIRPTA 15 %, Japan 10.21 %, Canada s.116 25 %, all on **gross** |
| 20 | **Decommissioning liabilities** on any former ski area — inherited lift removal can exceed the purchase price |

## The three questions that decide each jurisdiction

If time is short, these are the ones that matter:

- **New Zealand:** *Will the client emigrate?* The s 16 intention-to-reside pathway is the only
  route that consents the thesis intact.
- **Japan:** *Is a private, non-paying rope tow outside the 鉄道事業法, and does taking paying
  guests destroy the exemption?* And separately: *will the 森林管理局 lease upper-mountain
  national forest to a foreign-owned entity?* That second answer could reframe the whole
  Japanese case as freehold base + leased vertical.
- **Chile:** *Does freehold-to-the-summit actually exist on the market, or is the good terrain
  all CONAF / Bienes Nacionales concession?* If the latter, Chile's excellent ownership law is
  moot and the finding inverts.
