# Title verification — the red flags that mean "the peaks are not in the title"

The client's non-negotiable test is whether the registered title actually includes the alpine
terrain. These are the specific failure modes, by jurisdiction, ordered by deal-killing
potential.

---

## New Zealand

| # | Red flag | Why it kills the deal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Estate reads "Leasehold" or Crown Pastoral Lease** | The Crown owns the mountain. Tenure review **ended** with the Crown Pastoral Land Reform Act 2022, so there is **no conversion path**, and historically the process gave the tops to the Crown — Godley Peaks freeholded 2,676 ha while **11,875 ha went to the Crown** |
| 2 | **Marketed area >> freehold area** | Listings routinely combine freehold, leasehold and licensed area into one headline hectare figure. Decompose every time |
| 3 | **DEM maximum inside the title polygon is far below the summit** | The definitive test. Overlay the parcel on a digital elevation model and read the highest contour the title actually reaches |
| 4 | **DOC conservation polygon sits over the tops** | The retired alpine land from tenure review. Visible on the public conservation estate layer |
| 5 | **QEII National Trust open space covenant** | **Perpetual, and runs with the land.** Kills development even where the title does reach the peak. Two are registered against Northburn |

## Japan

| # | Red flag | Why it kills the deal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **公図 is a Meiji-derived 字図/切図 with no coordinates** | **You can hold perfect title to a parcel nobody can locate.** The single most under-appreciated risk in Japanese mountain land |
| 2 | **地籍調査 未実施** in that municipality | No modern cadastral survey. Boundaries are unestablished and 立会い with neighbours may be impossible where heirs are untraceable |
| 3 | **共有 / 記名共有地 / "○○外○名"** | Requires unanimity among co-owners who may be untraceable heirs — the 所有者不明土地 problem. A sale can simply be impossible |
| 4 | **The summit is 国有林** | The peak was never for sale. National forest is ~20 % of Japan's land and is concentrated in exactly this terrain |
| 5 | **保安林 + 自然公園法 特別保護地区 / 第1種特別地域** | **Owning the peak is not the same as being allowed to use it.** Felling restricted, land-form alteration requires the governor's permission |
| 6 | **車馬等乗入れ規制区域** | Vehicle-entry restriction zone in a park — up to 1 year's imprisonment or a ¥1 m fine. **Destroys the snowcat model outright**, and a seller has no incentive to mention it |

## Chile

| # | Red flag | Why it kills the deal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deslindes described by natural features** ("*hasta el divortium aquarum*", "*hasta la línea de altas cumbres*") with no measured area or georeferenced plano | The stated *cabida* is nominal. Assume the hectarage is wrong |
| 2 | **DL 2.695 *saneamiento* in the chain of title** | A fast-track regularisation route that has generated large volumes of contested title |
| 3 | **Doble inscripción** — the same land inscribed twice | A structural feature of a register of inscriptions rather than a Torrens title |
| 4 | **Mining concessions over the property** | ⚠️ **The most-missed Andean risk.** A concession holder can force a surface *servidumbre* **across your ski slope**. Check before anything else |
| 5 | **Water rights excluded from the sale**, or property inside the 10 km frontier strip under DL 1.939 | Water is a separate real right with a separate registry and does not pass automatically |
| 6 | **Parcel is *tierra indígena*** under Ley 19.253 | Sanction for breach is **nulidad absoluta** — the purchase is void, not compensable. Obtain a CONADI non-indigenous certificate for every *rol* south of the Biobío |

## United States

| # | Red flag |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Severed dominant mineral estate** — a third party may own, and be entitled to access, the minerals under your mountain |
| 2 | **Deeded vs BLM/USFS *permitted* acreage** — the base is fee, the mountain is permit. Pull the deed, the title commitment with a plotted legal description, the USFS special-use permit file, and county GIS overlaying fee parcels on the ski terrain |
| 3 | **Water rights** — prior appropriation; severable, and a large part of ranch value |
| 4 | **Agricultural/forest tax classification** — use-based, and lost if grazing or harvesting stops |

## Sweden and Scotland

Freehold title is clean in both. **The problem is not title, it is exclusivity.**

- Sweden: ***renskötselrätt*** burdens the freehold, survives sale and cannot be excluded, plus
  *allemansrätten*.
- Scotland: the **Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003** grants statutory public access over
  essentially all land.

**In both, a private ski mountain is legally impossible regardless of what the title says.**

## Georgia and Türkiye

- **Georgia:** foreigners **cannot own agricultural land** (2017/2018 constitutional amendment).
- **Türkiye:** foreign individuals are capped at **30 ha** nationally — incompatible with "large
  landholding" by definition.
